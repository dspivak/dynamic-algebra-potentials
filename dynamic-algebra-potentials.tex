\title{Compositional Dynamics for Learning and Physics}

\author{David I. Spivak}

\date{\vspace{-.2in}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a single compositional setting in which gradient-based learning and Hamiltonian-style mechanics appear as functorial semantics. The syntax is an operad $\srwd$ whose objects are input-output interfaces (pairs of manifolds) and whose morphisms are \emph{smooth rewiring diagrams}, which consist of a reactive parameter space, smooth output and input maps, and a real-valued potential.

The technical heart of the paper is what we call a \emph{Moore internalization}, a lax monoidal functor $\Lens{\cat{C}}\to\cat{C}$ associated to any symmetric monoidal closed category $\cat{C}$. Using it, we provide two functors $\Phiphase,\Phiconf\colon\srwd\to\org$ into the bicategory $\org$ of polynomial coalgebras---input-output discrete dynamical systems---which we take as the semantics category. $\Phiphase$ stores both position and momentum; $\Phiconf$ stores only position. When applied to a parameterized function, $\Phiconf$ recovers the gradient descent training algorithm, with backpropagation as the lens' backward pass. When applied to a collection of particles wired together in series, $\Phiphase$ recovers the discrete wave equation; applied to any finite directed graph of harmonic particles, it recovers the dynamics of its graph Laplacian.
\end{abstract}

%====================================================================
\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch.intro}
%====================================================================

There is a commonality in Hamiltonian dynamics and gradient descent, namely a map $T^*M\to TM$ from a cotangent bundle to a tangent bundle, transforming covectors into vectors that change the state. We refer to vector spaces with this sort of covector-to-vector structure as \emph{reactive}. In Hamiltonian dynamics, it is the \emph{symplectic sharp} map on phase space; in gradient descent it is the Riemannian metric, which determines the learning rate on parameter space. In those familiar settings the map is an isomorphism, but our framework requires only a linear map. And in each case, the dynamics is compositional: local subsystems---each with their own dynamics---exchange information through which their dynamics assemble into an integrated system.

The goal of this paper is to build a compositional syntax for such smooth reactive, potential-driven systems, including adaptive couplings, and a functorial semantics that turns this syntax into explicit discrete update rules.

\section{Motivation for smooth rewiring diagrams}\label{sec.intro_smooth_rewiring}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

Operads are a natural categorical setting for establishing compositionality results~\cite{Spivak:2013b,Yau:2015a}. An operad $\cat{W}$ establishes a composition \emph{syntax}---what sorts of diagrams can be composed---and a functor $\cat{W}\to\smset$ establishes the corresponding semantics. For example
\[
\varphi=\begin{tikzpicture}[oriented WD, bb port length=4pt, bb port sep=1.5, bb min width=.35cm, bbx=.4cm, bby=.8ex, baseline=(box1), particle/.style={path picture={\fill (path picture bounding box.center) circle (2.4pt);}}]
  \def\N{6}
  \node[bb={1}{1}, particle] (box1) {};
  \foreach \i [evaluate=\i as \prev using int(\i-1)] in {2,...,\N} {
    \node[bb={1}{1}, particle, right=.7 of box\prev] (box\i) {};
    \draw (box\prev_out1) -- (box\i_in1);
  }
  \node[bb={0}{0}, fit={(box1) (box\N)}] (Y) {};
  \node[coordinate] at (Y.west|-box1_in1) (Y_in1) {};
  \node[coordinate] at (Y.east|-box\N_out1) (Y_out1) {};
  \draw (Y_in1) -- (box1_in1);
  \draw (box\N_out1) -- (Y_out1);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
For interfaces $b_1,\ldots,b_K,c$, a morphism $\varphi\colon b_1,\ldots,b_K\to c$ in $\cat{W}$ represents an arrangement of $K$ internal boxes, with interfaces $b_1,\ldots,b_K$, inside an external box with interface $c$. The picture above has $K=6$: it wires up six boxes of the same type inside a box of the same type again. As syntax, it records the architecture of the chain---the types of each wire and how boxes are connected---but it does not say what the boxes do, what signals pass along each wire, or how strongly one box affects the next.

In the examples below we need a richer composition syntax: one where the effective coupling is itself part of the data and may adapt to the signals flowing between boxes. The way it adapts is the data of a dynamical system whose behavior depends on continuous parameters, on the values exposed by the internal boxes, and on values received by the external box. For example, consider an adaptive spring material for which each connecting edge has a variable spring constant that responds both to external control and to a chosen potential $U$.

We call these richer composition patterns \emph{smooth rewiring diagrams}; formally, they will be the morphisms in the operad of smooth rewiring diagrams constructed in \cref{sec.forming_potlens}. Instead of giving this syntax static semantics in $\smset$, we give it dynamical semantics in $\org$, whose morphisms are polynomial coalgebras, i.e.\ input-output dynamical systems. Thus a $0$-ary morphism can represent a component, such as a single dynamical particle, and a $K$-ary morphism can represent an adaptive interaction among $K$ components.

The choice to have semantics land in $\org$ is the choice to take polynomial functors seriously as input-output interfaces. Polynomial functors are built from one representable $\yon$ by coproducts and products---the free distributive category on a single object---so a polynomial
\[
p=\sum_{i:I}\yon^{p[i]}
\]
is just a family of \emph{directions} $p[i]$ indexed by a family of \emph{positions} $i:I$. We regard positions as the possible outputs and the directions as the inputs accepted at that output. A $p$-coalgebra $S\to p(S)$ is then a deterministic dynamical system with state: given a state $s$ it specifies an output position and, for every acceptable input direction, specifies a next state. 

Just as the free coproduct completion of a single object has all limits---i.e.\ $\smset$ has a great deal of structure emerging unexpectedly when simply asking for coproducts---much of the usefulness of $\poly$ comes from structure that emerges unexpectedly from the free-distributive description. For example, as recalled in \cref{sec.poly}, polynomials can be understood as functors $\smset\to\smset$, and under this interpretation they compose, which gives a monoidal product $\tri$ on $\poly$. This leads naturally to considering polynomial coalgebras and the store comonad, both of which will be essential.

But most relevantly for smooth rewiring, $\poly$ also carries the Dirichlet product $\otimes$, which combines interfaces in parallel, and this monoidal structure is closed. A map of polynomials $p\to q$ can be understood as a static coupling pattern: it sends each output position of the internal interface $p$ to an output position of the external interface $q$ and each compatible input direction of $q$ back to an input direction of $p$. Thus the $K$-many internal interfaces $p_1,\ldots,p_K$ of a wiring diagram combine as $p_1\otimes\cdots\otimes p_K$, and a static wiring into an external interface $q$ is a map $\varphi\colon p_1\otimes\cdots\otimes p_K\to q$.

Because $\poly$ is monoidal closed, such maps are equivalently the positions of the internal hom
\[
w\coloneqq\ihom{p_1\otimes\cdots\otimes p_K,q},
\]
so a fixed $\varphi$ is the same thing as a one-state coalgebra on the polynomial $w$. To make the rewiring dynamic, we replace that one-state coalgebra by an arbitrary coalgebra $f\colon S\to w(S)$: its state $s:S$ outputs the current wiring pattern $\varphi_s\colon p_1\otimes\cdots\otimes p_K\to q$, and each acceptable input direction determines the next state $s'$. These internal-hom coalgebra categories are the morphisms in $\org$. We will explain all this in detail in \cref{sec.poly_dynamic_bg}.

\section{From smooth data to dynamics}\label{sec.intro_smooth_to_dynamics}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The bridge from manifolds to polynomial functors is the cotangent functor $\cot\colon\mfd\to\poly$. It sends a manifold $M$ to the polynomial
\[
\cotof{M}\coloneqq\sum_{m\in M}\yon^{T_m^*M},
\]
whose positions are points $m\in M$ and whose directions at $m$ are the covectors $T_m^*M$. A smooth map $f\colon M\to N$ acts forward on positions by $f$ and backward on directions by cotangent pullback $(T_mf)^\top$. Crucially, $\cot$ is strong symmetric monoidal: $\cotof{M\times N}\cong\cotof{M}\otimes\cotof{N}$. Thus parallel products of polynomial interfaces correspond to ordinary products of manifolds.

It remains to describe the source syntax: ordinary smooth data that will be sent through $\cot$ and then converted into the dynamics of a polynomial coalgebra. A neural network, for example, begins as a smooth map
\[f\colon V\times M\to N\]
i.e.\ a choice of parameter, input, and output manifolds, together with a model map; typically, $M=\rr^m$ and $N=\rr^n$ are Euclidean. Categorically, $f$ is a parameterized map $M\to N$ in the sense of the Para construction~\cite[Def.~2]{capucci2022cybernetics} (\cref{sec.para_general}), with parameter $V$. Gradient descent updates this parameter via a map $T^*V\to TV$ that converts a cotangent (the gradient) into a tangent step on $V$; such a map is exactly a reaction structure $\sharpR_V$ (\cref{sec.rvect}), so $V$ is implicitly reactive in the gradient-descent setting.

Aside from making the reactive structure explicit, smooth rewiring diagrams generalize the above familiar situation in three ways. First, an ordinary manifold $M$, i.e.\ in the role of $\rr^m$ and $\rr^n$ above, becomes only one component of an interface $\binom{\inpt M}{\outp M}$, namely the output side $\outp M$; that is, we add a new input side $\inpt M$, which is an ordinary smooth port for information supplied by the surrounding system.
Second, an ordinary map $f\colon V\times M\to N$ becomes only the first component of a pair of maps
\[
\outp f\colon V\times\outp M\to\outp N
\qqand
\inpt f\colon V\times\outp M\times\inpt N\to\inpt M.
\]
The new input map $\inpt f\colon V\times\outp M\times\inpt N\to\inpt M$ routes the new external inputs back to the new input sides of the internal interfaces.
And third, we add a potential $U\colon V\times\outp{M}\times\inpt{N}\to\rr$, whose differential contributes to the dynamics.

The first two pieces, $\outp f$ and $\inpt f$, are exactly the shape of a lens $\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}$. Lenses are a standard language for bidirectional interaction, appearing in bidirectional programming and database theory, compositional game theory, and categorical accounts of backpropagation~\cite{bohannon2006relational,johnson2012lenses,hedges2017coherence,fong2019backprop,cruttwell2021categorical}. In our manifold setting, these two maps route output and input data between the internal boxes and the external box in a composable way.

To accommodate the potential---the last piece---we pass from lenses to the coKleisli category $\plmfd$ for the comonad on $\lmfd$ induced by the monad $\rr\times\blank$ on $\mfd$: informally, the backward direction now handles both inputs and a real number; cf.\ monadic lenses~\cite{abou2016reflections}. We write $\srwd$ for the operad underlying the symmetric monoidal category $\para{\rvect}{\plmfd}$, and call its morphisms \emph{smooth rewiring diagrams}. Under the dynamics functor $\srwd\to\org$, the parameters and potential let the effective coupling not only transport information but also contribute to the update rule.

The passage from lenses to polynomial coalgebras uses a functor we call \emph{Moore internalization}. In any symmetric monoidal closed category, a lens interface $\lensob c$ can be internalized as $\ihom{\inpt c,I}\otimes\outp c$, and this process constitutes a lax monoidal functor (\cref{cor.Theta}). Moore machines \cite{moore1956gedanken}---a simple kind of dynamical system---arise in this way.%
\footnote{Moore internalization sends the ordinary set-lens interface $\binom{A}{B}$, represented in $\poly$ by $\binom{A\yon}{B\yon}$, to the monomial $B\yon^A$ (\cref{ex.moore}), whose coalgebras are precisely $(A,B)$-Moore machines.}

Together, $\cot$ and Moore internalization turn ordinary smooth data---output map, input map, a reactive parameter, and a potential---into data for a discrete dynamical system, i.e.\ a polynomial coalgebra. At that point, the remaining choice is the state set $S$ that the coalgebra carries and how an incoming covector updates it. For example, in Hamiltonian dynamics, we not only store the data of position---which affects the current moment's dynamics---but also a momentum, which affects the next's. We package this choice as an \emph{integrator}: a state space, depending functorially on $V$, together with the rule that updates the current state by an incoming covector. Each integrator $\intg$ gives a dynamics functor $\Phi_\intg\colon\srwd\to\org$.

Thus the integrator is where the dynamical regime gets decided: one smooth rewiring diagram fed through different integrators can produce qualitatively different behaviors. We work out two. We show that the \emph{configuration integrator} ($S\coloneqq V$) generalizes both Newton's method (\cref{sec.newton_warmup}) and training an artificial neural network (\cref{sec.dl_warmup}). Indeed, when applied to the $\srwd$-morphism corresponding to a parameterized neural network, it recovers gradient descent, with backpropagation as the lens backward pass. 
We also show that the \emph{phase integrator} ($S\coloneqq T^*V$) applied to any finite directed graph of harmonic particles yields the discrete dynamics of its graph Laplacian (\cref{sec.graph_laplacian}), generalizing the wave equation. With the same graph wiring and potential but the opposite-sign reaction, the configuration integrator gives the corresponding discrete heat equation (\cref{rmk.graph_heat}). This is the sense in which smooth rewiring diagrams are syntax.

The paper aims to be self-contained for a reader comfortable with monoidal categories and (co)monads and who has a passing familiarity with smooth manifolds; the background chapter reviews polynomial functors, the Para construction, and the bicategory $\org$, while lenses are reviewed where they first enter in \cref{sec.lenses_general}. The next section places this work alongside the open-energy-driven framework of CLS; readers interested only in the constructions of this paper may skip to \cref{sec.plan}.

\section{Relation to open energy-driven systems}\label{sec.related_cls}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The closest predecessor of this paper is Capucci--Lynch--Spivak~\cite{capucci2024organizing} (hereafter CLS), which identifies the reaction $T^*X\to TX$ in both Hamiltonian mechanics and gradient descent and builds a functorial semantics
\[
\mathbf{OpenErg}\To{T^*}\mathbf{OpenODE}\To{\mathrm{collapse}}\mathbf{COrg}
\]
 from their category of \emph{open energy-driven systems}, through \emph{open ODEs}, to $\mathbf{COrg}$, a continuous-time analog of $\org$. A first difference is that our semantics $\Phiconf,\Phiphase\colon\srwd\to\org$ is discrete-time, rather than continuous.

However, neither their framework nor the one in this paper subsumes the other. There is a common subcategory $\cat{R}$ whose objects are manifolds and whose morphisms $M\to N$ are---in CLS notation---tuples $(\rv V,w,E)$ where
\[
\rv V:\rvect,\qquad
w\colon V\times M\to N,\qquad
E\colon V\times M\to\rr.
\]
There is a wide strong monoidal functor $\cat{R}\to\mathbf{OpenErg}$ and a fully faithful operad functor $\cat{R}\to\srwd$, the latter sending $M$ to $\binom{\rr^0}{M}$, output map $\outp f\coloneqq w$, unique input map $\inpt f\coloneqq \bang$, and potential $U\coloneqq E$. 
In other words, the two extensions of $\cat{R}$ go in orthogonal directions: $\srwd$ has richer interfaces and additional input-routing data in its morphisms, while CLS allows manifold (rather than vector-space) state spaces. We conjecture that the latter gap is removable by replacing $\rvect$ with a category of reactive manifolds equipped with connections (\cref{rmk.rmfdc_generalization}), but do not pursue this here.

There is also a semantic comparison between the two.

\begin{proposition*}[Proven as \cref{prop.euler_submersion_lenses}]
For $\cat R$ as above, there is a category $\cat S$, whose objects are submersions, and a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=large]
\mathbf{OpenErg}\ar[d, "{\mathrm{collapse}\circ T^*}"'] &
\cat{R}\ar[l, "{\mathrm{wide}}"']\ar[r, "{\mathrm{ff}}"]\ar[d] &
\srwd\ar[d, "\Phiconf"]\\
\mathbf{COrg} &
\cat{S}\ar[l, "{\mathrm{wide}}"]\ar[r] &
\org
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where the middle vertical functor sends $M$ to the submersion $T^*M\surj M$, and the lower right functor sends a submersion $p\colon E\surj B$ to the polynomial $\sum_{b:B}\yon^{E_b}$.
\end{proposition*}
The point is that, on the overlap $\cat R$, both semantics are built from the same smooth data: cotangent pullback along $w$, the differential of $E$, and the reaction $\sharpR_V$. The category $\cat{S}$ records this common data, at which point it can be read in either of two ways: as a continuous-time morphism in $\mathbf{COrg}$ on the CLS side, or as a discrete coalgebra in $\org$ on our side, where the tangent component $\dot x$ becomes the Euler step $x\mapsto x+\dot x$.

\section{Notational conventions}\label{sec.prelim}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

We fix some typographic conventions. We denote generic categories using calligraphic script, $\cat{C},\cat{M},\cat{A}$, and specifically-named categories using boldface, $\smset,\poly,\mfd$. We write specifically-named functions or operations in roman, $\ev,\coev$, and named functors in sans-serif, $\Fun{T}$. Number systems use blackboard bold, $\nn,\rr$; named bicategorical structures are written with a blackboard-bold initial, such as $\org$ and $\Para$. 

For convenience in the electronic version, many recurring pieces of notation are hyperlinked to where they are defined, e.g.\ $\org$ is clickable.

We write $\blank$ for a ``blank'' placeholder argument in expressions, e.g.\ $\rr\times\blank\colon\mfd\to\mfd$ for the product-with-$\rr$ functor. We write $\defineTerm{bang}$ for a unique map when the domain and codomain make it clear, e.g.\ the unique map $\emptyset\to A$ or the unique map $X\to\rr^0$. We write $\inc$ for any inclusion functor that is clear from its domain and codomain categories, and we sometimes elide them, e.g. sometimes writing $F$ for $F\circ\inc$. For composable maps $A\To{f}B\To{g}C$, we may write $f\then g$ or $g\circ f$ as their composite, based on convenience.

We reserve $\eta$ and $\mu$ for units and multiplications, and $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$ for comonoid counits and comultiplications. Since $\eta$ is also the usual notation for a learning rate, we write learning rates as $\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}$.

We sometimes refer to a $(2,1)$-category simply as a category. More precisely, its homs are groupoids and composition is associative and unital up to coherent isomorphism.

By \emph{operad} we mean \emph{colored operad} unless otherwise stated. We sometimes elide the difference between monoidal categories $(\cat{C},I,\otimes)$ and their underlying operads $\cat{O}_{\cat{C}}$, which have $K$-ary morphisms $c_1\otimes\cdots\otimes c_K\to c'$. Morphisms between underlying operads correspond exactly to lax morphisms between the monoidal categories.%
\footnote{That is, the functor $\Cat{SMC}_{\tn{lax}}\to\Cat{Opd}$ is fully faithful.}

\section{Smooth-manifold notation}\label{sec.manifolds_notation}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

In this section we fix the differential-geometric notation used throughout the paper.

We work in the cartesian category $(\defineTerm{mfd},\rr^0,\times)$ of finite-dimensional smooth real manifolds and smooth maps. For a manifold $M$ and point $m\colon\rr^0\to M$, we write $TM$ and $T_mM$ for the tangent bundle and the tangent space at $m$, and we write $T^*M$ and $T^*_mM$ for the cotangent bundle and the cotangent space at $m$.

The tangent functor $T\colon\mfd\to\mfd$ preserves products, and the cotangent-bundle assignment (which is not functorial) does so on objects: $T(M\times N)\cong TM\times TN$ and $T^*(M\times N)\cong T^*M\times T^*N$; in particular
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.T_products}
T_{(m,n)}(M\times N)\cong T_mM\oplus T_nN
\qqand
T^*_{(m,n)}(M\times N)\cong T^*_mM\oplus T^*_nN.
\end{equation}

For a smooth map $f\colon M\to N$, write
\[
Tf\colon TM\to TN
\]
for the \emph{tangent map} (also called the \emph{global differential}), and
\[
T_m f\colon T_mM\to T_{f(m)}N
\qqand
(T_m f)^\top\colon T^*_{f(m)}N\to T^*_mM
\]
for the \emph{differential at $m$}---the restriction of $Tf$ to the fiber over $m\in M$---and its transpose.%
\footnote{In the literature, what we denote $T_m f$ is often denoted $Df_m$.}

We often elide the inclusion $\inc\colon\vect\to\mfd$, treating finite-dimensional real vector spaces as manifolds. The direct sum $(0,\oplus)$ structure on $\vect$ is (a coproduct and) a product, and $\inc$ is product preserving. By standard manifold theory, the canonical global chart on a finite-dimensional real vector space $V$ provides linear isomorphisms
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.tangent_vec}
T_xV \;\cong\; V \qqand T^*_xV \;\cong\; V^*
\end{equation}
at every point $x\in V$; we use these identifications throughout, often without comment. We also will make frequent use of the canonical identification
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.canonical_dual_sum}
(V\oplus V^*)^*\cong V^*\oplus V,\qquad
\lambda\leftrightarrow(\xi,x)\quad\text{where}\quad
\lambda(x',\xi')=\xi(x')+\xi'(x).
\end{equation}

When the codomain is $\rr$, the tangent map of $U\colon M\to\rr$ can be combined with the standard constant covector field $r\mapsto +1$ on $\rr$ to give a covector field on $M$, also called the \emph{differential} of $U$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.differential}
dU\colon M\to T^*M,\qquad dU|_m\coloneqq(T_m U)^\top(+1)\in T^*_mM.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $dU|_m(\dot m)=(T_mU)(\dot m)$ for $\dot m\in T_mM$. When $M=V$ is a vector space, we regard $dU|_x$ as an element of $V^*$ under \eqref{eqn.tangent_vec}.

\begin{warning}\label{warn.prime}
We will not use $f'$ notation to mean derivatives; ``priming'' a symbol (such as $f$) will only be used to indicate that $f'$ is somehow analogous to $f$.
\end{warning}

\section{Polynomial functors, coalgebras, and $\mathbb{O}\Cat{rg}$}\label{sec.poly_dynamic_bg}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

This section reviews the polynomial-functor machinery underlying our model of dynamics: the symmetric monoidal closed category $\poly$ (\cref{sec.poly}), the substitution product and the store comonad (\cref{sec.substitution_store}), polynomial coalgebras as discrete-time dynamical systems (\cref{sec.coalgebras}), the monoidal bicategory $\org$ of polynomial coalgebras (\cref{sec.org}), and the encoding of cotangent bundles as polynomials (\cref{sec.cot}) that bridges this setup to the differential geometry of \cref{sec.manifolds_notation}.

\subsection{The symmetric monoidal closed category \texorpdfstring{$\poly$}{Poly}}\label{sec.poly}

We briefly review the category $\defineTerm{poly}$ of polynomial functors; see~\cite{niu2025polynomial} for a comprehensive treatment.

For any set $A$, let $\yon^A\colon\smset\to\smset$ denote the functor $\smset(A,\blank)$. A \emph{polynomial functor} is a coproduct $p\coloneqq\sum_{i: I}\yon^{A_i}$ of such functors. We refer to $I$ as the set of \emph{positions} of $p$ and $A_i$ is the set of \emph{directions} at position $i$. For example $(\yon^2+\yon)\colon\smset\to\smset$ is the polynomial that sends a set $X$ to $(X\times X)+X$; it has two positions, with two and one direction, respectively.

The category $\poly$ has polynomial functors as objects and natural transformations as morphisms. It has all sums and products, and these are given by the usual sum and product of polynomials, e.g. $(\yon^2+\yon)\times\yon\cong\yon^3+\yon^2$. Since $p(1)=\sum_{i:I}1=I$, we use the compact notation $p(1)$ for the position set and $p[i]\coloneqq A_i$ for the direction set at $i: p(1)$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.poly}
p=\sum_{i: p(1)}\yon^{p[i]}=\sum_{i: p(1)}\;\prod_{d: p[i]}\yon.
\end{equation}
We use juxtaposition for multiplication: $pq\coloneqq p\times q$, and we write $\defineTerm{yon}\coloneqq\yon^1$ and $1\coloneqq\yon^0$. A \emph{monomial} is a polynomial of the form $M\yon^N=\sum_{m\in M}\yon^N$; we refer to $\yon^N$ as a \emph{representable} and to $M=M\yon^0$ as a \emph{constant}.

One can derive from the universal property of coproducts and the Yoneda lemma that the maps in $\poly$, i.e.\ the natural transformations $\varphi\colon p\to q$, are given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.poly_map}
\poly(p,q)=\prod_{i: p(1)}\;\sum_{j: q(1)}\;\prod_{e: q[j]}\; \sum_{d: p[i]}1.
\end{equation}
The outer $\prod_i\sum_j$ yields a ``forward on positions'' function $\varphi_1\colon p(1)\to q(1)$, which is the component of $\varphi$ at $1:\smset$, and the inner $\prod_e\sum_d$ yields, for each $i:p(1)$, a ``backward on directions'' function from the directions of $q$ at $\varphi_1(i)$ to the directions of $p$ at $i$; we denote it as follows:%
\footnote{What we denote here by $\bk{\varphi}{i}$ is often denoted $\varphi^\sharp_i$; we use different notation because $\sharpR$ is used throughout the paper.}
\[\bk{\varphi}{i}\colon q[\varphi_1(i)]\to p[i].\]

For polynomials $p,q:\poly$, their \emph{Dirichlet product} is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.dirichlet}
p\otimes q\coloneqq\sum_{(i,j):p(1)\times q(1)}\yon^{p[i]\times q[j]}\;.
\end{equation}
Together with unit $\yon$, the Dirichlet product forms a symmetric monoidal structure, which is also closed: the \emph{internal hom} is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.dirichlet_hom}
\ihom{p,q}=\prod_{i: p(1)}\;\sum_{j: q(1)}\;\prod_{e: q[j]}\;\sum_{d: p[i]}\yon
\end{equation}
satisfying the hom-tensor adjunction
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.dirichlet_adjunction}
\poly(r\otimes p,\, q)\cong\poly(r,\,\ihom{p,q}),
\end{equation}
naturally in $r$. Note that $\ihom{p,q}(1)=\poly(p,q)$, as is immediate from comparing \eqref{eqn.dirichlet_hom} with \eqref{eqn.poly_map}. We can rewrite it in standard form as
\begin{equation*}
\ihom{p,q}\cong\sum_{\varphi: \poly(p,q)}\yon^{\sum_{i: p(1)}q[\varphi_1(i)]}
\end{equation*}

\subsection{The substitution product and the store comonad}\label{sec.substitution_store}

The \emph{substitution} (or \emph{composition}) \emph{product} on $\poly$ is defined by $(p\tri q)(S)\coloneqq p(q(S))$; it is a (nonsymmetric) monoidal structure with unit $\yon$. Using the expanded form of \eqref{eqn.poly}:
\begin{align*}
p\hypertarget{term.tri}{\mathbin{\termraw{tri}}} q&=
\sum_{i: p(1)}\;\prod_{d: p[i]}\;\sum_{j: q(1)}\;\prod_{e: q[j]}\yon\\\nonumber&\cong
\sum_{i:p(1)}\;\sum_{j\colon p[i]\to q(1)}\prod_{d:p[i]}\prod_{e:q[j(d)]}\yon.
\end{align*}
A position of $p\tri q$ is a pair $(i,j)$ where $i: p(1)$ and $j\colon p[i]\to q(1)$; the direction set is $(p\tri q)[(i,j)]=\sum_{d:p[i]}q[j_d]$. In particular, viewing a set $S:\smset$ as the constant polynomial $S\yon^0$, we recover the notion of applying $\smset\To{p}\smset$ to $S$ using substition: $p\tri S=p(S)$.

Together, $\otimes$ and $\tri$ form a \emph{normal duoidal} category. That is, they share a unit, $\yon$, and there is a map of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.duoidal}
(p_1\tri p_2)\otimes(q_1\tri q_2)\too(p_1\otimes q_1)\tri(p_2\otimes q_2)
\end{equation}
making the required diagrams commute. See \cite{aguiar2010monoidal,niu2025polynomial} for details.

\begin{definition}[The store comonad]\label{ex.store}
For any set $S$, define $\defineTerm{Store}(S)\coloneqq S\yon^S$. It arises as the comonad associated to the adjunction $S\times\blank\;\dashv\;(\blank)^S$ on $\smset$, i.e.\ the comonad with underlying endofunctor $X\mapsto S\times X^S$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Polynomial coalgebras as dynamical systems}\label{sec.coalgebras}

For a polynomial $p$, a \emph{$p$-coalgebra} is a pair $(S,f)$ where $S:\smset$ is a set and $f\colon S\to p(S)$ is a function. We call elements of $S$ \emph{states}. Unpacking $p(S)=\sum_{i:p(1)} S^{p[i]}$, the function $f$ is equivalently a pair
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.coalg_unpack}
f_1\colon S\to p(1),\qquad
\bk{f}{\blank}\colon\prod_{s:S} S^{p[f_1(s)]},
\end{equation}
where $f_1(s)$ returns a position and $\bk{f}{s}\colon p[f_1(s)]\to S$ returns a state-update function---from directions to new states---for each $s:S$. Given a state $s:S$, we call $r_s\coloneqq f_1(s):p(1)$ its \emph{readout} position and $\bk{f}{s}\colon p[r_s]\to S$ its \emph{update} function, sending each direction $d:p[r_s]$ to a new state $s_d\coloneqq\bk{f}{s}(d)$.

When $p=B\yon^A$ then a $p$-coalgebra $S\to B\times S^A$ is equivalently a readout function $S\to B$ and an update function $S\times A\to S$, which is independent of the readout. Such a coalgebra is called an $(A,B)$-\emph{Moore machine}~\cite{moore1956gedanken}. In general, the type of the input direction $d$ may depend on the output $r_s\in p(1)$, so could be called a \emph{dependent} Moore machine.

A $p$-coalgebra is thus a deterministic dynamical system whose interface is $p$: the state exposes an output in $p(1)$ and transitions in response to inputs in $p[f_1(s)]$, capturing Moore machines, discretized differential equations, Markov decision processes, and other mode-dependent dynamics in a single framework~\cite{niu2025polynomial}. It is a special case of the general theory of coalgebras for endofunctors; see~\cite{jacobs2017introduction} for a comprehensive treatment.

There is a connection between $p$-coalgebras and the $\Store$ comonad from \cref{ex.store}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.store_monoidal}
The assignment $S\mapsto\Store(S)$, sending a bijection $f\colon S\To{\cong}T$ to $f\yon^{f\inv}$, defines a strong symmetric monoidal functor
\[
\Store\colon(\smsetiso,1,\times)\to(\poly,\yon,\otimes).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The Dirichlet product formula \eqref{eqn.dirichlet} gives $1\yon^1=\yon$ and $S\yon^S\otimes T\yon^T=(S\times T)\yon^{S\times T}$. Functoriality on bijections is direct: $\Store(g\circ f)=(g\circ f)\yon^{f\inv\circ g\inv}=\Store(g)\circ\Store(f)$.
\end{proof}

Recall that an \emph{action} of a symmetric monoidal category $(\cat A,I,\otimes)$ on a category $\cat D$ is a functor $(\cdot)\colon\cat A\times\cat D\to\cat D$ equipped with coherent natural isomorphisms $I\cdot x\cong x$ and $(a\otimes b)\cdot x\cong a\cdot(b\cdot x)$; such data is also called an \emph{actegory}~\cite{capucci2024actegories}. When $\cat D$ too is symmetric monoidal and the action functor $(\cdot)$ is itself strong monoidal, $\cat D$ is a \emph{monoidal actegory}~\cite[Def.~5.1.1]{capucci2024actegories}.

\begin{lemma}[Strong monoidal functors give actions]\label{lem.smc_action}
Any strong monoidal functor $J\colon\cat A\to\cat D$ with $\cat D$ symmetric monoidal induces an action of $\cat A$ on $\cat D$, making $\cat D$ a monoidal actegory, by left multiplication,
\[
a\cdot d\coloneqq J(a)\otimes d.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[Store action]\label{def.store_action}
The \emph{store action} of $\smsetiso$ on $\poly$ is the action induced by $\Store$ via \cref{lem.smc_action}:
\[
(\cdot)\colon\smsetiso\times\poly\to\poly,
\qquad
S\cdot p\coloneqq \Store(S)\otimes p.
\qedhere\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coalgebras as polynomial maps]\label{prop.coalg_as_poly_map}
For $p:\poly$ and $S:\smsetiso$, there is a natural bijection
\[
\smset(S,\,p\tri S)\;\cong\;\poly(\Store(S),\,p).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As in \eqref{eqn.coalg_unpack}, a coalgebra $f\colon S\to p\tri S$ unpacks as a pair $f_1\colon S\to p(1)$ and $\bk{f}{\blank}\colon\prod_{s:S}S^{p[f_1(s)]}$. A polynomial map $\phi\colon S\yon^S\to p$ unpacks as a pair $\phi_1\colon S\to p(1)$ and $\bk{\phi}{\blank}\colon\prod_{s:S}S^{p[\phi_1(s)]}$ with the same type signature by \eqref{eqn.poly_map}, so they are in bijection.\footnote{
Equivalently, $\poly$ has a left Kan operation $\lens{p}{q}$ with $\poly(p,q'\tri q)\cong\poly(\lens{p}{q},q')$ given by $\lens{p}{q}=\sum_{i:p(1)}\yon^{q\tri p[i]}$, so $\lens{S}{S}=S\yon^S$.
}

Naturality in $p:\poly$ is just composition. For naturality in $S$, a bijection $g\colon S\To{\cong}T$ acts on the left side via post-composition with $p\tri g$ and pre-composition with $g\inv$, and on the right side via $\Store(g)=g\yon^{g\inv}$ (\cref{lem.store_monoidal}); we check
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
	S\ar[r,"f"]\ar[d, "g"']&p\tri S\ar[d, "p\tri g"]\\
	T\ar[r,"f'"']&p\tri T
\end{tikzcd}
\quad\leftrightsquigarrow\quad
\begin{tikzcd}
	S\yon^S\ar[r,"\phi"]\ar[d, "g\yon^{g\inv}"']&
	p\ar[d, equal]\\
	T\yon^T\ar[r,"\phi'"']&
	p
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The first commutes iff $f'_1\circ g=f_1$ and $g\circ\bk{f}{s}=\bk{f'}{g(s)}$ for all $s:S$; the second iff $\phi'_1\circ g=\phi_1$ and $\bk{\phi}{s}=g\inv\circ\bk{\phi'}{g(s)}$. These coincide under the established bijection $\phi_\blank=f_\blank$.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[$\yon$-coalgebras and trajectories]\label{ex.y_coalgebra_trajectory}
The simplest case is $p\coloneqq\yon$, in which case its positions $p(1)=1$ and directions $p[*]=1$ are both singletons, and $\yon\tri S=S$, so a $\yon$-coalgebra is just a function $f\colon S\to S$. The readout is trivial and the update takes no input direction: at each tick, the state $s$ transitions to $f(s)$. Given an initial state $x_0\in S$, iteration produces a function
\[
x\colon\nn\to S,\qquad x(0)\coloneqq x_0,\qquad x(t+1)\coloneqq f(x(t)).
\]
We call this the \emph{trajectory} of the $\yon$-coalgebra from initial state $x_0$, and use this notation freely below.
\end{example}

\subsection{The monoidal bicategory \texorpdfstring{$\org$}{Org}}\label{sec.org}

We recall the monoidal bicategory $\defineTerm{org}$ from~\cite{spivak2021learners}; see also~\cite{shapiro2022dynamic}. Its objects are polynomials $\ob\org=\ob\poly$, and for polynomials $p,q$ the hom-category is
\[\org(p,q)\coloneqq\ihom{p,q}\tn{-}\Cat{Coalg}.\]
Its monoidal structure $(\yon,\otimes)$ is induced by the Dirichlet product on polynomials. A morphism $p\to q$ in $\org$ is an $\ihom{p,q}$-coalgebra $(S,\,f\colon S\to\ihom{p,q}\tri S)$. We regard it as an interaction, regarding $p$ as an \emph{internal} interface and $q$ as an \emph{external} one: and at each state $s\in S$ the coalgebra converts each internal output $i\in p(1)$ into an external output $j\in q(1)$, then converts each external input $e\in q[j]$ into an internal input $d\in p[i]$ together with a new state $s'\in S$. This can be read off from the formula
\[
\smset(S,\ihom{p,q}(S))\cong\prod_{s:S}\prod_{i: p(1)}\;\sum_{j: q(1)}\;\prod_{e: q[j]}\;\sum_{d: p[i]}\sum_{s':S}1.
\]
We refer to $S$ as the \emph{state set}; a morphism $(S,f)\to(S',f')$ in $\ihom{p,q}\coalg$ is a function $g\colon S\to S'$ such that $(\ihom{p,q}\tri g)\circ f=f'\circ g$. Concisely, we denote the function $f$ by
\begin{equation*}
s\;\mapsto\;i\;\mapsto\;\Bigl(j(s,i),\;e\mapsto\bigl(d(s,i,e),s'(s,i,e)\bigr)\Bigr).
\end{equation*}

In particular, taking the internal interface to be the monoidal unit recovers \cref{sec.coalgebras}: indeed, since $\ihom{\yon,q}\cong q$, a morphism $\yon\to q$ in $\org$ is precisely a $q$-coalgebra.

We often elide the difference between $\org$ as a monoidal bicategory and $\org$ as a category-enriched operad, with maps
\[
\org(p_1,\ldots,p_K;q)\coloneqq[p_1\otimes\cdots\otimes p_K,q]\coalg.
\]
The operadic perspective is closer to the intended semantics---internal interfaces $p_1,\ldots,p_K$ within a larger external interface $q$---than the monoidal perspective is, but the operadic notation $\org(p_1,\ldots,p_K;q)$ is heavier than the monoidal notation $\org(p,q)$.

\begin{proposition}[\cite{spivak2021learners,shapiro2022dynamic}]\label{prop.org}
The hom-categories $\ihom{p,q}\coalg$ assemble into a monoidal bicategory $\org$ with polynomials as objects, with monoidal structure $(\yon,\otimes)$ inherited by the Dirichlet product on polynomials.
\end{proposition}

This structure arises from the fact that the functor $\blank\coalg$ from $(\poly,\yon,\otimes)$ to $(\smcat,1,\times)$ is lax monoidal:
\[
1\to\yon\coalg
\qqand
p\coalg\times q\coalg\to(p\otimes q)\coalg.
\]
From here, the structure maps $\yon\to[p,p]$ and $[p,q]\otimes[q,r]\to[p,r]$ induce the identity and composition functors
\[
1\to[p,p]\coalg
\qqand
[p,q]\coalg\times[q,r]\coalg\to[p,r]\coalg.
\]

\begin{remark}[$\org$ is monoidal closed]\label{rmk.org_closed}
The polynomial $[p,q]$ also serves as the internal hom in $\org$:
\[
\org(a,[p,q])\,=\,[a,[p,q]]\coalg\;\cong\;[a\otimes p,q]\coalg\,=\,\org(a\otimes p,q),
\]
by closedness of $\poly$ (\cref{sec.poly}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cotangent bundles as polynomials}\label{sec.cot}

The category $\mfd$ of smooth manifolds provides the source of polynomials relevant to physics and learning. Each manifold has a cotangent bundle, and each smooth map has a transpose on cotangent fibers; combining these gives a functor into $\poly$.

\begin{definition}[Cotangent functor]\label{def.cot}
The \emph{cotangent functor} $\defineTerm{cot}\colon\mfd\to\poly$ sends a smooth manifold $M$ to
\begin{equation*}
\cotof{M}\coloneqq\sum_{m\in M}\yon^{T^*_mM}
\end{equation*}
where $T^*_mM$ is the cotangent space at $m$. A smooth map $f\colon M\to N$ is sent to the $\poly$ map $\cotof{f}\colon\cotof{M}\to\cotof{N}$ whose positions and directions are
\[
  \cotof{f}_1\coloneqq f
  \qqand
  \bk{\cotof{f}}{m}\coloneqq(T_m f)^\top
 \]
 as maps $M\to N$ and $T^*_{f(m)}N\to T^*_mM$.
\end{definition}

This is indeed functorial: the identity map $\id_M\colon M\to M$ has $T_m(\id_M)=\id_{T_mM}$, whose transpose is $\id_{T^*_mM}$, so $\cotof{\id_M}=\id_{\cotof{M}}$. For composable smooth maps $f\colon M\to N$ and $g\colon N\to P$, the chain rule gives $T_m(g\circ f)=T_{f(m)}g\circ T_m f$, so
\[
(T_m(g\circ f))^\top=(T_m f)^\top\circ(T_{f(m)}g)^\top=\bk{\cotof{f}}{m}\circ\bk{\cotof{g}}{f(m)},
\]
which is the backward part of $\cotof{g}\circ\cotof{f}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.cot_monoidal}
The cotangent functor $\cot\colon(\mfd,\mathbb{R}^0,\times)\to(\poly,\yon,\otimes)$ is strong symmetric monoidal:
\[
\cotof{\mathbb{R}^0}\cong\yon
\qqand
\cotof{M\times N}\cong\cotof{M}\otimes\cotof{N}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The one-point manifold $\rr^0=\{0\}$ has $T^*_0\rr^0=\{0\}$, so $\cotof{\mathbb{R}^0}=\sum_{\{0\}}\yon^{\{0\}}\cong\yon$, so the unit is preserved. By \eqref{eqn.T_products}, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.cot_times}
\cotof{M\times N}=\sum_{(m,n)\in M\times N}\yon^{T^*_{(m,n)}(M\times N)}\cong\sum_{m\in M}\yon^{T^*_mM}\otimes\sum_{n\in N}\yon^{T^*_nN}=\cotof{M}\otimes\cotof{N}
\end{equation}
using the definition of the Dirichlet product \eqref{eqn.dirichlet}.

This isomorphism $\cotof{M\times N}\cong\cotof{M}\otimes\cotof{N}$ is natural: given a product map $f\times g\colon M\times N\to M'\times N'$, the tangent map is block-diagonal: $T_{(m,n)}(f\times g)=T_m f\oplus T_n g$. Hence $(T_{(m,n)}(f\times g))^\top=(T_m f)^\top\times(T_n g)^\top$.
%
%\textit{Coherence.} The associator and unitor for the Dirichlet product correspond to the canonical isomorphisms $(M\times N)\times P\cong M\times(N\times P)$ and $M\times 1\cong M$ in $\mfd$, so the coherence diagrams for a strong monoidal functor commute.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
In fact, $\cot$ also preserves coproducts,
\[
\cotof{M}+\cotof{N}\cong\cotof{M+N}.
\]
Hence $\cot\colon(\mfd,0,+,1,\times)\to(\poly,0,+,\yon,\otimes)$ is a distributive monoidal functor. We will not use this fact in this paper.
\end{remark}

\Cref{prop.cot_monoidal} uses only the underlying sets of the cotangent fibers, e.g.\ in \eqref{eqn.cot_times}. The linear structure is not lost, however: \cref{lem.cot_comonoid} shows that the diagonal and terminal maps in $\mfd$ induce fiberwise addition and zero on $\cotof{M}$.

Since $(\mfd,\mathbb{R}^0,\times)$ is cartesian, every manifold $M$ is a comonoid via the diagonal $\Delta_M\colon M\to M\times M$ and $\bang_M\colon M\to\mathbb{R}^0$. And since $\cot$ is strong symmetric monoidal (\cref{prop.cot_monoidal}), it preserves comonoid structures. 

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.cot_comonoid}
For any smooth manifold $M$, the polynomial $\cotof{M}$ is a comonoid in $(\poly,\yon,\otimes)$. The comultiplication and counit
\[
\cotof{\Delta_M}\colon\cotof{M}\to\cotof{M}\otimes\cotof{M}
\qqand
 \cotof{\bang_M}\colon\cotof{M}\to\yon
\]
send positions by $m\mapsto(m,m)$ and $m\mapsto *$, respectively. On directions, each cotangent fiber $T^*_mM$ carries an induced \emph{commutative monoid} structure
\[
T^*_mM\times T^*_mM\to T^*_mM,\quad (\xi,\xi')\mapsto\xi+\xi'
\qqand \rr^0\to T^*_mM,\quad *\mapsto 0,
\]
i.e.\ fiberwise addition and zero. Thus the $\otimes$-comonoid structure on $\cotof{M}$ recovers the additive structure of each cotangent space.%
%\footnote{
%One can recover scalar structure on $T^*_mM$ as well. For any $M:\mfd$, there is a natural map
%\[
%(\cdot)\colon\cotof{M}\to\yon^\rr\otimes\cotof{M}
%\]
%Axioms relating the comonoid structure of $\cotof{M}$ and various structures on $\rr$ enforce that the forward map is $x\mapsto (!,x)$ and that the backwards map $\bk{(\cdot)}{x}\colon (r,\xi)\mapsto r\cdot\xi$ encodes the following laws:
%\[
%	1\cdot\xi=\xi,\qquad
%	(r_1r_2)\cdot\xi=r_1\cdot(r_2\cdot\xi),\qquad
%	0\cdot\xi=0,\qand
%	r\cdot(\xi_1+\xi_2)=(r\cdot\xi_1)+(r\cdot\xi_2).
%\]
%Notably missing is the axiom $(r_1+r_2)\cdot\xi=(r_1\cdot\xi)+(r_2\cdot\xi)$, which seemingly cannot be encoded this way because there is seemingly no way to copy a cotangent vector ($\xi$).
%}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On positions $\cotof{\Delta_M}$ and $\cotof{\bang_M}$ are given $m\mapsto(m,m)$ and $m\mapsto *$. On directions, $T_m\Delta_M$ is the diagonal $T_mM\to T_mM\times T_mM$, $v\mapsto(v,v)$; its transpose is addition $T^*_mM\times T^*_mM\to T^*_mM$. The map $T_m(\bang_M)$ is $T_mM\to\rr^0$, whose transpose is the map $\rr^0\to T^*_mM$ that selects the zero covector.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Hopf monoid structure on cotangent bundles of Lie groups]\label{prop.cot_hopf}
Let $G$ be a Lie group with unit $\eta\colon\mathbb{R}^0\to G$, multiplication $\mu\colon G\times G\to G$, and inversion $\iota\colon G\to G$. Then $\cotof{G}$ is a Hopf monoid in $(\poly,\yon,\otimes)$: the monoid structure is
\[
\cotof{\eta}\colon\yon\to\cotof{G},\qquad \cotof{\mu}\colon\cotof{G}\otimes\cotof{G}\to\cotof{G},
\]
the comonoid structure is as in \cref{lem.cot_comonoid}, and the antipode is $\cotof{\iota}\colon\cotof{G}\to\cotof{G}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is well known that strong monoidal functors preserve monoids, comonoids, and bimonoids: they preserve both the defining maps and the axioms relating them. For the same reason they also preserve the antipode structure and axioms, hence the Hopf monoid structure. See also~\cite{spivak2023hopf}.
\end{proof}

\noindent In each case there is an associated operad $\cat{W}$ whose objects are the legal interfaces (boxes-with-object-labeled-ports) and whose morphisms $\varphi\colon b_1,\ldots,b_K\to c$ are the legal arrangements of internal boxes in an external box. That is, for a set $O$ of objects, there are operads $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}$, $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-MnCat}}$, $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-TrCat}}$, and $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Opd}}$ each with a 2-ary morphism that can be drawn as in \eqref{eqn.wd_examples}, because each of those wiring diagrams has two internal boxes.%
\footnote{
There is a potentially confusing level-shift in that there is an operad $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Opd}}$ for which algebras are operads with object set $O$. In case it's helpful, the object set for $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Opd}}$ would be $\List(O)\times O$, because every ``box'' has a list of inputs and an output.
}

Category-like structures that can interpret the compositionality syntax from $\cat{W}$ are precisely the $\cat{W}$-algebras, i.e.\ lax monoidal functors
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.W_alg}
\cat{C}\colon\cat{W}\to\smset.
\end{equation}
So any categories are lax monoidal functors $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}\to\smset$ for various $O$, monoidal categories are lax monoidal functors $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-MnCat}}\to\smset$ for various $O$, etc. Indeed, such a functor $\cat{C}$ assigns to each box $b$ the set $\cat{C}(b)$ of ``fillers'' and to each arrangement $\varphi$ the function $\cat{C}(\varphi)\colon \cat{C}(b_1)\times\cdots\times \cat{C}(b_K)\to \cat{C}(c)$ sending fillers for internal boxes to a filler of the external box.%
\footnote{
By a $\cat{W}$-algebra, we mean an operad functor $\cat{W}\to\smset$, where $\smset$ is the operad (multicategory) underlying the monoidal category $(\smset,1,\times)$. In general, the \emph{underlying operad} of a symmetric monoidal category $(\cat{C},I,\otimes)$ has the same objects as $\cat{C}$ and morphisms $\cat{C}(a_1,\ldots,a_n;b)\coloneqq\cat{C}(a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_n,b)$, with operadic composition by tensoring and composing in $\cat{C}$.
}

We'll make this explicit for categories.
\begin{example}[$O$-categories as $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}$-algebras]
For a set $O$, let $\ob\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}\coloneqq O\times O$ and
\[\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}((o_0',o_1),(o_1',o_2),\ldots(o_{K-1}',o_K);(o_0,o_K'))\coloneqq
\begin{cases}
\{1\}&\tn{ if }o_k=o_k'\quad \forall\; 0\leq k\leq K\\
\varnothing&\tn{ otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]
Thus the diagram
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.cat_composite}
\varphi\coloneqq\begin{tikzpicture}[oriented WD, bb port sep=1, bb port length=0, bb min width=.2cm, bby=.2cm, inner xsep=.2cm, x=.2cm, y=.3cm, baseline=(Catf)]

  \node[coordinate] at (Cat.east|-Catg_out1) (Cat_out1) {};
  \draw (Cat_in1) -- (Catf_in1);
  \draw (Catf_out1) -- (Catg_in1);
  \draw (Catg_out1) -- (Cat_out1);
  \draw[label]
    node[left=3pt of Cat_in1] {$A_0$}
    node[above left =2pt of Catf_in1] {$A'_0$}
    node[above right=2pt of Catf_out1] {$A_1$}
    node[above left=2pt of Catg_in1] {$A'_1$}

	represents a map $\varphi\colon (A'_0,A_1),(A'_1,A_2)\to(A_0,A'_2)$ in $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}$, in the case $A_0=A_0'$, $A_1=A_1'$ and $A_2=A_2'$. 

If $\cat{C}\colon\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}\to\smset$ is an algebra, then \eqref{eqn.cat_composite} shows $f_1:\cat{C}(A'_0,A_1)$, $f_2:\cat{C}(A'_1,A_2)$ and $\varphi(f_1,f_2):\cat{C}(A_0,A'_2)$. In other words the $2$-ary morphism $\varphi$ represents composition in a category. The category of small categories with object set $O$ is equivalent to the category of $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}$-algebras.%
\footnote{It is not hard to let the objects change as well. For any function $O\to O'$, there is an induced operad functor $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}\to\cat{W}_{O'\tn{-Cat}}$ and hence a functor $\cat{W}_{O'\tn{-Cat}}\alg\to\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}\alg$. The Grothendieck construction of this data is equivalent to the category of small categories. The same idea applies to all of the wiring diagram operads.}
\end{example}

Each such $\cat{W}$ is a \emph{composition syntax}: its morphisms specify legal arrangements of boxes---how systems compose---with no commitment about what fills them. A $\cat{W}$-algebra is a semantics. A variety of examples of these wiring-diagram operads, whose algebras are structured categories, can be found in the literature. \cite{Spivak.Schultz.Rupel:2016a} treats the traced and compact cases, showing in particular that the operad $\cat{W}_{\tn{TrCat}}$ for traced categories is the operad $1\tn{-}\Cat{Cob}$ of oriented 1-dimensional cobordisms. \cite{fong2019hypergraph} treats hypergraph categories, whose associated operad $\cat{W}_{\tn{HypCat}}$ is given by cospans of finite sets. The symmetric monoidal case~\cite{patterson2021wiring} is in some sense the most basic of the three, but its operad $\cat{W}_{\tn{MnCat}}$ is less easy to describe directly.

An advantage of the operadic perspective is that it allows us to more uniformly treat various sorts of category, including ones that may not have an existing name. For example, 
consider the notion of ``traced monoidal categories without identity''; one might balk at this because they wouldn't know whether the ``yanking'' axiom could so easily just be discarded. But as an operad it is fine because wiring diagrams of this sort can be nested.

Another advantage is that wiring diagram algebras also easily represent $\cat{V}$-enriched categories for monoidal categories $\cat{V}$, namely by using algebras $\cat{W}\to\cat{V}$ valued in $\cat{V}$ in place of $\smset$ as in \eqref{eqn.W_alg}. For example, a $\vect$-enriched category with object set $O$ is an operad functor $\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}\to\vect$.


For our purposes, the goal is to build \emph{dynamic} versions of the structures in \eqref{eqn.wd_examples}, in which the ``fillers'' of a box are ``morphisms that change in time'', i.e.\ coalgebras. In \cite{shapiro2022dynamic}, a dynamic category is defined to be a category enriched in $\org$; similarly for dynamic monoidal categories or dynamic operads. Given the ideas above, these are just operad functors
\[
\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Cat}}\to\org
\qquad
\cat{W}_{O\tn{-MnCat}}\to\org
\qquad
\cat{W}_{O\tn{-Opd}}\to\org
\] 
for any $O:\smset$. We continue with this approach, taking values in the bicategory $\org$ of \cref{sec.org}. In \cref{sec.forming_potlens} we will choose the particular composition syntax $\cat{W}$ used in this paper.

\section{The Para construction}\label{sec.para_general}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

We use the following form of the Para construction. If a symmetric monoidal category $\cat{A}$ acts on a category $\cat{D}$ (\cref{lem.smc_action}), the category $\defineTerm{Para}_{\cat{A}}(\cat{D})$ has the same objects as $\cat{D}$, and a morphism $x\to y$  in $\para{\cat{A}}{\cat{D}}$ is a pair $(a,f)$ with $a:\cat{A}$ and
\[
f\colon a\cdot x\to y
\]
in $\cat{D}$. We call such morphisms \emph{parameterized maps} in $\cat D$. The identity on $x$ has parameter $I$ and action given by the unitor $I\cdot x\cong x$. The composite of $(a,f)\colon x\to y$ and $(b,g)\colon y\to z$ has parameter $b\otimes a$ and uses
\begin{equation*}
(b\otimes a)\cdot x\cong b\cdot(a\cdot x)\To{b\cdot f}b\cdot y\To{g}z.
\end{equation*}

When $\cat D$ is a monoidal actegory, $\para{\cat A}{\cat D}$ inherits a symmetric monoidal structure: on objects, $x\otimes y$ is the $\cat D$-tensor, and the tensor of $(a,f)\colon x\to y$ and $(b,g)\colon x'\to y'$ has parameter $a\otimes b$ and underlying map
\begin{equation*}
(a\otimes b)\cdot(x\otimes x')\To{\cong}(a\cdot x)\otimes(b\cdot x')\To{f\otimes g}y\otimes y',
\end{equation*}
where the first isomorphism comes from $\cat D$ being a monoidal actegory.

The Para construction extends to a bicategory: a 2-cell $(a,f)\Rightarrow(a',f')$ in $\para{\cat A}{\cat D}(x,y)$ is a morphism $\alpha\colon a\to a'$ in $\cat A$ such that $f'\circ(\alpha\cdot x)=f$. When $\cat A$ is a groupoid, all 2-cells are invertible, and each hom-category $\para{\cat A}{\cat D}(x,y)$ is a groupoid.
In this paper, all of our parameter categories are groupoids, so these Para constructions are naturally $(2,1)$-categories; by the convention of \cref{sec.prelim}, we will usually call them categories.

The following is straightforward.

\begin{proposition}[Action squares from monoidal natural transformations]\label{prop.para_strong_induced}
Let $J\colon\cat A\to\cat D$ and $J'\colon\cat A'\to\cat D'$ be strong monoidal functors, inducing actions of $\cat A$ on $\cat D$ and of $\cat A'$ on $\cat D'$ by left multiplication (\cref{lem.smc_action}). A monoidal natural transformation $\theta\colon J'\circ F\Rightarrow G\circ J$ as on the left, with $F$ strong monoidal and $G$ lax (resp.\ strong) monoidal, induces a lax (resp.\ strong) monoidal action square as on the right:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
\cat A\ar[r,"J"]\ar[d,"F"', ""{name=left}]\& \cat D\ar[d,"G", ""' {name=right}]\\
\cat A'\ar[r,"J'"']\& \cat D'
\arrow[Rightarrow, from=left, to=right, shorten <=3mm, shorten >=3mm, "\theta"]
\end{tikzcd}
\quad\leadsto\quad
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
\cat A\times\cat D\ar[r,"(\cdot)"]\ar[d,"F\times G"', ""{name=left}]\& \cat D\ar[d,"G", ""' {name=right}]\\
\cat A'\times\cat D'\ar[r,"(\cdot')"']\& \cat D'.
\arrow[Rightarrow, from=left, to=right, shorten <=6mm, shorten >=6mm]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Para functoriality from action squares {\cite[\S 3.6, eq.~(3.59)]{capucci2024actegories}}]\label{prop.para_square}
Suppose $\cat A,\cat A'$ are symmetric monoidal categories acting on $\cat D,\cat D'$, that $F\colon\cat A\to\cat A'$ is a strong monoidal functor, $G\colon\cat D\to\cat D'$ is a functor, and $\theta\colon F(a)\cdot' G(x)\to G(a\cdot x)$ is a natural transformation compatible with the action unitors and associators:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
\cat A\times\cat D\ar[r,"(\cdot)"]\ar[d,"F\times G"', ""{name=left}]\& \cat D\ar[d,"G", ""' {name=right}]\\
\cat A'\times\cat D'\ar[r,"(\cdot')"']\& \cat D'.
\arrow[Rightarrow, from=left, to=right, shorten <=6mm, shorten >=6mm, "\theta"]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
Then there is an induced pseudo-functor
\[
\para[\theta]{F}{G}\colon\para{\cat A}{\cat D}\;\longrightarrow\;\para{\cat A'}{\cat D'},
\]
which is strict if $F$ is strict monoidal.
When $\cat D,\cat D'$ are monoidal actegories, $G$ is strong (resp.\ normal lax, resp.\ lax) symmetric monoidal, and $\theta$ is a monoidal natural transformation, then $\para[\theta]{F}{G}$ extends to a strong (resp.\ normal lax, resp.\ lax) monoidal functor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The hypothesis is a 1-cell in the 2-category $\Cat{Act}$ of actegories \cite[\S 3.6, eq.~(3.59)]{capucci2024actegories}; the induced functor records the action of $\mathbb{P}\Cat{ara}$ on it. For the normal-lax case: the unitor of $\para[\theta]{F}{G}$ at the unit object $(I_{\cat A}, I_{\cat D})$ is built from $F$'s unitor and $G$'s unitor, both of which are isomorphisms, so the unitor of $\para[\theta]{F}{G}$ is too.\qedhere
\end{proof}

We suppress identities in the decoration $\para[\theta]{F}{G}$ of these induced functors: an identity action square $\theta$ is dropped from the superscript and an identity functor $G$ from the argument, while an identity functor $F$ is recorded by its acting category in the subscript rather than written as $\id$. Thus an action square $\theta$ between two actions of a fixed $\cat A$ on a fixed $\cat D$ (so $F=\id_{\cat A}$ and $G=\id_{\cat D}$) induces a functor we write simply as $\Para^{\theta}_{\cat A}$.

\section{Lenses and backward monads}\label{sec.lenses_general}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

This section reviews the category $\Lens{\cat C}$ of lenses in a symmetric monoidal category $\cat C$, and develops the \emph{backward-monad} refinement that will let us add a real-valued potential to the backward direction. Both ingredients are standard, lightly adapted; the chapter's technical heart is the Moore internalization functor in \cref{sec.abstract_representation}.


\subsection{Lenses}\label{sec.lenses}
We first recall the category $\defineTerm{Lens}_{\cat{C}}$ of lenses in a symmetric monoidal category $(\cat{C},I,\otimes)$; see \cite{hedges2017coherence} and \cite[Def.~2.9]{spivak2019generalized}. An object is a pair $\lensob{c}$ in which $\outp c$ carries a comonoid structure $(\varepsilon_{\outp c},\delta_{\outp c})$. A morphism $f\colon\lensob{c}\to\lensob{d}$ consists of a pair of maps $f=\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}$ where
\begin{equation*}
\outp f\colon \outp c\to\outp d
\qqand
\inpt f\colon \outp c\otimes\inpt d\to\inpt c,
\end{equation*}
and $\outp f$ is a comonoid homomorphism.%
\footnote{
When $(\cat{C},1,\times)$ is cartesian monoidal, every object has a unique comonoid structure and every morphism is automatically a comonoid homomorphism~\cite{fox1976coalgebras}.
}
Such a morphism can be depicted as follows:
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[oriented WD, bb port length=5pt, bb port sep=1, bb min width=.4cm, bbx=.5cm, bby=.7ex, baseline=(fo)]
  \node[bb={1}{1}] (fo) {$\outp f$};
  \node[bb={1}{2}, above left=6 and 1.5 of fo] (fi) {$\inpt f$};
  \node[circle, draw, fill=black, inner sep=0, minimum size=3pt, left=1 of fo] (dot) {};
  \coordinate (co) at ($(dot)+(-3,0)$);
  \coordinate (do) at ($(fo.east)+(1,0)$);
  \coordinate (ci) at (co |- fi_in1);
  \coordinate (di) at (do |- fi_out2);
  \node[left=0 of co] {$\outp c$};
  \node[right=0 of do] {$\outp d$};
  \node[left=0 of ci] {$\inpt c$};
  \node[right=0 of di] {$\inpt d$};
  \draw[ar] (co) to (dot);
  \draw[ar] (dot) to (fo_in1);
  \draw[ar] (fo_out1) to (do);
  \draw[ar] (fi_in1) to (ci);
  \draw[ar] (di) to (fi_out2);
  \draw[ar] (dot) to[out=30, in=0] (fi_out1);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
Morphisms compose as follows: given also $g\colon \lensob{d}\to\lensob{e}$, the composite $f\then g$ is given by
\[
\outp{(f\then g)}=\outp f\then\outp g
\qqand
\inpt{(f\then g)}=
	(\delta_{\outp{c}}\otimes\inpt{e})\then
	(\outp{c}\otimes\outp{f}\otimes\inpt{e})\then
	(\outp{c}\otimes\inpt{g})\then
	\inpt{f}.
\]

The identity on $\lensob{c}$ is $\binom{\varepsilon_{\outp c}\otimes\id_{\inpt c}}{\id_{\outp c}}$, i.e.\ counit in the first argument. The category $\Lens{\cat{C}}$ inherits a monoidal structure with unit $\binom{I}{I}$ and
\[
\lensob{c}\otimes\lensob{d}\coloneqq\lensobs{c}{d}.
\]

Our main example will be $\cat{C}=\mfd$ with its cartesian product. But the following is simple, instructive, and will come in handy.
\begin{example}\label{ex.lens_finsetop}
The category $\finset\op$, opposite to the category of finite sets, is cartesian monoidal where the product is given by the disjoint union $(0,+)$, i.e.\ the coproduct in $\finset$. Thus every object carries a canonical comonoid structure $A\from 0$ and $A\from A+A$, and every morphism preserves it automatically. 

An object in $\Lens{\finset\op}$ is just a pair of finite sets $\binom{\inpt A}{\outp A}$. A morphism $\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}\colon\binom{\inpt A}{\outp A}\to\binom{\inpt B}{\outp B}$ consists of two maps, together sometimes called a \emph{prism}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.lens_finsetop}
\outp A\From{\outp f} \outp B
\qqand
\outp A+\inpt B\From{\inpt f} \inpt A,
\end{equation}
with no further homomorphism condition, as it is automatic.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.lens_functoriality}
The $\Lens{\blank}$ construction is functorial in symmetric monoidal categories and strong monoidal functors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $F\colon\cat{C}\to\cat{D}$ is colax monoidal, it preserves comonoid structures, so we can define $\Lens F$ on objects. If $F$ is lax monoidal, it has a productor $F_2\colon F(A)\otimes F(B)\to F(A\otimes B)$ from which we can define $\Lens F$ on morphisms: given a lens morphism $\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}$ we set $\Lens F\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}\coloneqq\binom{F(\inpt f)\circ F_2}{F(\outp f)}$. For functoriality the lax and colax structures must cohere, i.e. the lax structure must be inverse to the colax structure; we leave the details to the reader.
\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
The functor $\blank\yon\colon(\smset,1,\times)\to(\poly,\yon,\otimes)$ sending $A\mapsto A\yon$ is strong monoidal: $A\yon\otimes B\yon\cong (A\times B)\yon$. As such it induces a strong monoidal functor
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.lens_yon}
\Lens{\blank\yon}\colon\Lens{\smset}\to\Lens{\poly}.
\end{equation}
\end{example}

Since $\finset\op$ is the free cartesian category on one object, any object $M$ in a cartesian category $\cat{M}$ determines a strong monoidal functor $M^\blank\colon\finset\op\to\cat{M}$ sending $A\mapsto M^A$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma.lens_pow}
For any cartesian category $\cat M$ and object $M:\cat M$, the functor $M^\blank\colon\finset\op\to\cat{M}$ is strong monoidal, hence induces a strong monoidal functor
\[
\Lens{M^\blank}\colon\Lens{\finset\op}\to\Lens{\cat M}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Lenses can represent serial, parallel, and feedback}\label{sec.plenspot_ops}

Recall from \cref{sec.wd_operads} that operads---and hence monoidal categories---can be used to encode compositional syntax. In fact, we can think of the operad underlying $\Lens{\cat{C}}$ as giving a syntax for something like ``traced symmetric monoidal semicategories'', as we now explain. This section is meant for intuition and in particular does not define the previously quoted phrase. It will also not be used in any formal development.

By a \emph{semicategory} we mean almost a category, except without the notion of identities (like a semigroup is a group without identities). So to say that $\Lens{\cat{C}}$ is a composition syntax for something like traced symmetric monoidal semicategories, we should show that it interprets diagrams that look like this. 
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.arb_wd}
\varphi\coloneqq
\begin{tikzpicture}[oriented WD, bb min width=.6cm, bb port sep=1, bbx=.6cm, bby=1ex, bb port length=2.5pt, baseline=(X2)]
  \node[bb port sep=2, bb={2}{2}] (X1) {};
  \node[bb={1}{1}, right=.7 of X1_out1] (X2) {};
  \node[bb={0}{0}, fit={(X1) (X2) ($(X1.north)+(0,2.5)$)}] (Y) {};
  \node[coordinate] at (Y.west|-X1_in2) (Y_in1) {};
  \node[coordinate] at (Y.east|-X2_out1) (Y_out1) {};
  \node[coordinate] at (Y.east|-X1_out2) (Y_out2) {};
  \draw[ar] (Y_in1) -- node[above, font=\scriptsize] {$A$} (X1_in2);
  \draw[ar] (X1_out1) -- node[above, font=\scriptsize] {$B$} (X2_in1);
  \draw[ar] (X2_out1) -- node[above, font=\scriptsize] {$C$} (Y_out1);
  \draw[ar] (X1_out2) -- node[below, font=\scriptsize] {$D$} (Y_out2);
  \draw[ar] let \p1=(X2.north east), \p2=(X1.north west), \n1={\y2+\bby}, \n2=\bbportlen in
          (X2_out1) to[in=0,in looseness=.9] (\x1+.7*\n2,\n1) -- node[above, font=\scriptsize] {$C$} (\x2-.7*\n2,\n1) to[out=180] (X1_in1);
  \end{tikzpicture}
 \end{equation}

We will consider boxes as lenses $\binom{\inpt c}{\outp c}$, where the ports on the left of the box constitute the input $\inpt c$ and the ports on the right of the box constitute the output $\outp c$. The wiring diagram itself is a morphism in $\Lens{\cat{C}}$ from the tensor product of the internal boxes to the external box.

For example, the wiring diagram \eqref{eqn.arb_wd} includes three boxes, two internal boxes and the external box, so it should be represented in $\Lens{\cat{C}}$ by a map of the form
\[
\varphi\colon\binom{C\otimes A}{B\otimes D}\otimes\binom{B}{C}\to\binom{A}{C\otimes D}
\]
where let us say that $B,C,D$ are comonoids. Unfolding, we need two maps in $\cat{C}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.wd_as_maps}
B\otimes D\otimes C\to C\otimes D
\qqand
(B\otimes D\otimes C)\otimes A\to C\otimes A\otimes B
\end{equation}
But these maps are obvious: they are just counit projections and symmetries.

One could take $\cat{C}\coloneqq(\mfd,\rr^0,\times)$. Then $A,B,C, D$ are manifolds (all of which are naturally comonoids) and the wiring diagram represents information flow, shuffling coordinates around. But there are many more smooth functions of the form \eqref{eqn.wd_as_maps} than there are wiring diagrams, e.g.\ there is more than one smooth function $\rr\to\rr$.

One can also interpret \eqref{eqn.arb_wd} in $\finset\op$, the opposite of the category of finite sets. Taking the sets $A\coloneqq\{a\}$, $B\coloneqq\{b\}$, $C\coloneqq\{c\}$, and $D\coloneqq\{d\}$ all to have one element for simplicity. Now \eqref{eqn.wd_as_maps} consists of two maps
\[
\{b,d,c\}\from\{c,d\}
\qqand
\{b,d,c\}+\{a\}\from\{c,a,b\}.
\]
These two functions entirely determine the wiring diagram \eqref{eqn.arb_wd} as an operad morphism, saying how each external outgoing port $c,d$ is ``fed by'' some internal outgoing port (in $\{b,d,c\}$), and how each internal ingoing port $c,a,b$ is ``fed by'' either an internal outgoing port (in $\{b,d,c\}$) or the external ingoing port, $a$. Applying $\Lens{M^\blank}$ from \cref{lemma.lens_pow}, for any manifold $M\in\mfd$, realizes such wiring diagrams as lenses between manifolds.

\subsection{Backward monads on lenses}\label{sec.backwards}

We will add a notion of potentials to our lenses by proving a more general result: any strong monad on $\cat{C}$ induces a comonad on $\Lens{\cat{C}}$ by applying it to the input / backward-pass part. We pun on the word ``backwards'' to refer to the fact that it applies to the backward pass and the fact that, as such, it turns a monad into a comonad.

Recall that a monad $\Fun{T}$ on a monoidal category is \emph{strong} if there are natural maps $\sigma\colon X\otimes\Fun{T}Y\to\Fun{T}(X\otimes Y)$, satisfying four diagrams~\cite{kock1972strong}. When $\Fun{T}$ is a monoidal monad with components $\tau_0$ and $\tau_{X,Y}$, it is strong by defining $\sigma_{X,Y}\colon X\otimes \Fun{T}Y\to\Fun{T}(X\otimes Y)$ to be the composite
\[
X\otimes \Fun{T}Y\To{\eta_X}\Fun{T}(X)\otimes \Fun{T}(Y)\To{\tau_{X,Y}}\Fun{T}(X\otimes Y).
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.backward_comonad}
Let $(\Fun{T},\eta,\mu,\sigma)$ be a strong monad on a symmetric monoidal category $(\cat{C},I,\otimes)$. Then there is a comonad $\Lens{\Fun{T}}$ on $\Lens{\cat{C}}$ sending $\lensob{c}\mapsto \binom{\Fun{T}\inpt c}{\outp c}$. We denote its coKleisli category by $\defineTerm{Lcokl}\coloneqq(\Lens{\cat{C}})_{\Lens{\Fun T}}$. 

If moreover $\Fun{T}$ is a monoidal monad, then $\Lens{\Fun T}$ is a colax monoidal comonad, thus the coKleisli category $(\Lens{\cat{C}}^{\Fun{T}},I,\otimes)$ is naturally monoidal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a lens map $\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}$ with $\inpt f\colon\outp c\otimes\inpt d\to\inpt c$, define the backward component of $\Lens{\Fun T}\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}$ to be the following composite:
\begin{equation*}
\outp c\otimes\Fun{T}\inpt d\To{\sigma}\Fun{T}(\outp c\otimes\inpt d)\To{\Fun{T}\inpt f}\Fun{T}\inpt c.
\end{equation*}
The counit and comultiplication of $\Lens{\Fun{T}}$ are given on positions by the identity and on-directions by the unit and multiplication of the monad, together with the counit $\varepsilon_{\outp c}$:
\begin{equation*}
\outp c\otimes\inpt c\To{\varepsilon_{\outp c}\otimes\id}\inpt c\To{\eta}\Fun{T}\inpt c,
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\outp c\otimes\Fun{T}\Fun{T}\inpt c\To{\varepsilon_{\outp c}\otimes\id}\Fun{T}\Fun{T}\inpt c\To{\mu}\Fun{T}\inpt c.
\end{equation*}
We need to prove that $\Lens{\Fun{T}}$ preserves identity and composition (is a functor) and that the functor is counital and coassociative. These four axioms correspond precisely to the four axioms of a monad strength.
%The unit axiom~\cite[(1.5)]{kock1972strong} (left unitor coherence) gives preservation of identity, via $\sigma_{X,Y}\then\Fun{T}\pi_Y=\pi_{\Fun{T}Y}$. The $\eta$-coherence axiom of the strong monad gives the two counit laws. The $\mu$-coherence axiom, together with associativity of $\mu$, gives coassociativity.

We include only the least trivial here: preservation of composition. For $f\colon\lensob c\to\lensob d$ and $g\colon\lensob d\to\lensob e$, it suffices to show that the following commutes (at which point we post-compose with $\Fun{T}\inpt f\colon\Fun{T}(\outp c\otimes\inpt d)\to\Fun{T}\inpt c$ to obtain equality of maps $\outp c\otimes\Fun{T}\inpt e\to\Fun{T}\inpt c$):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.6em, row sep=3em, every label/.append style={font=\scriptsize}, font=\small, ampersand replacement=\&]
\outp c\otimes\Fun{T}\inpt e
  \ar[r, "\delta"]
  \ar[d, "\sigma"']
\& (\outp c)^{\otimes 2}\otimes\Fun{T}\inpt e
  \ar[r, "\outp f"]
  \ar[d, "\sigma"]
\&[5pt] \outp c\otimes\outp d\otimes\Fun{T}\inpt e
  \ar[r, "\sigma"]
  \ar[dr, "\sigma" description]
\& \outp c\otimes\Fun{T}(\outp d\otimes\inpt e)
  \ar[r, "\inpt g"]
  \ar[d, "\sigma"]
\& \outp c\otimes\Fun{T}\inpt d
  \ar[d, "\sigma"]
\\
\Fun{T}(\outp c\otimes\inpt e)
  \ar[r, "\delta"']
\& \Fun{T}((\outp c)^{\otimes 2}\otimes\inpt e)
  \ar[rr, "\outp f"']
\&
\& \Fun{T}(\outp c\otimes\outp d\otimes\inpt e)
  \ar[r, "\inpt g"']
\& \Fun{T}(\outp c\otimes\inpt d)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The first two squares are naturality of $\sigma$ in its first argument, the middle triangle is the associator axiom of strength~\cite[(1.6)]{kock1972strong}, and the last square is naturality of $\sigma$ in its second argument.

When $\Fun T$ is moreover a monoidal monad, we obtain a colax monoidal structure on $\Lens{\Fun{T}}$: the counitor $\binom{\Fun T(I)}{I}\to\binom{I}{I}$ amounts to $\tau_0\colon I\to\Fun T I$ and the coproductor map $\binom{\Fun{T}(\inpt c\otimes\inpt d)}{\outp c\otimes\outp d}\to\binom{\Fun{T}(\inpt c)\otimes\Fun T(\inpt d)}{\outp c\otimes\outp d}$ amounts to $\tau_{\inpt c,\inpt d}\colon \Fun{T}(\inpt c)\otimes\Fun T(\inpt d)\to \Fun{T}(\inpt c\otimes\inpt d)$.
\end{proof}

A map in the coKleisli category $\Lens{\cat{C}}^{\Fun{T}}$ is a map of the form $\binom{\Fun{T}\inpt c}{\outp c}\to\lensob{d}$ in $\Lens{\cat{C}}$, i.e.
\[
\outp f\colon \outp c\to\outp d
\qqand
\inpt f\colon \outp c\otimes\inpt d\to\Fun{T}\inpt c
\]
where $\outp f$ is a comonoid homomorphism.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.lens_T_functoriality}
The construction of \cref{prop.backward_comonad} is functorial in $(\cat{C},\Fun{T})$: a strong symmetric monoidal $F\colon\cat C\to\cat C'$ and strong monad morphism $\theta\colon F\circ\Fun T\Rightarrow\Fun T'\circ F$ induce
\[
\Cat{Lens}_{F,\theta}\colon\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}\to\Lens{\cat C'}^{\Fun T'},\qquad
\lensob c\mapsto\binom{F(\inpt c)}{F(\outp c)},\qquad
\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f}\mapsto\binom{\theta\circ F(\inpt f)\circ F_2}{F(\outp f)},
\]
where $F_2$ is $F$'s productor. If $\Fun T,\Fun T'$ are monoidal monads and $\theta$ is a morphism of monoidal monads, $\Cat{Lens}_{F,\theta}$ is strong symmetric monoidal.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop.lens_functoriality}, $F$ induces $\Lens F\colon\Lens{\cat C}\to\Lens{\cat C'}$. By \cref{prop.backward_comonad}, $\Fun T$ and $\Fun T'$ induce comonads $\Lens{\Fun T}$ and $\Lens{\Fun T'}$. The monad morphism $\theta$ gives the data necessary to define a natural transformation,
\[
\gamma\colon\Lens{\Fun T'}\circ\Lens F\Rightarrow \Lens F\circ\Lens{\Fun T},
\]
whose component at $\lensob c$ has output map $\id_{F(\outp c)}$ and input map given by
\[
F(\outp c)\otimes F(\Fun T\inpt c)\To{\varepsilon_{F(\outp c)}\otimes\id}F(\Fun T\inpt c)\To{\theta_{\inpt c}}\Fun T'F(\inpt c).
\]
Its naturality follows from that of $\theta$ and the counit $\varepsilon_{F(\outp c)}$.

We define $\Cat{Lens}_{F,\theta}$ on objects by $F\lensob{c}\coloneqq\binom{F(\inpt{c})}{F(\outp c)}$. On morphisms, recall that
\[
\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}\left(\lensob c,\lensob d\right)=\Lens{\cat C}\left(\Lens{\Fun T}\lensob c,\lensob d\right).
\]
For $f\in\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}(\lensob c,\lensob d)$, define $\Cat{Lens}_{F,\theta}(f)$ to be the element of $\Lens{\cat C'}^{\Fun T'}(\Lens F\lensob c,\Lens F\lensob d)$ represented by the composite
\[
\Lens{\Fun T'}\circ\Lens F\lensob c\To{\gamma_{\lensob c}}\Lens F\circ\Lens{\Fun T}\lensob c\To{\Lens F(f)}\Lens F\lensob d.
\]
Unfolding, its backward component is
\[
F(\outp c)\otimes F(\inpt d)\To{F_2}F(\outp c\otimes\inpt d)\To{F(\inpt f)}F(\Fun T\inpt c)\To{\theta_{\inpt c}}\Fun T'F(\inpt c),
\]
and its output component is $F(\outp f)$, as claimed. The strong monad-morphism axioms for $\theta$ say exactly that $\gamma$ is compatible with the counits and comultiplications of the two comonads; hence $\Cat{Lens}_{F,\theta}$ preserves coKleisli identities and composition. 

If the monads are monoidal and $\theta$ is monoidal, the natural transformation $\gamma$ is monoidal, so $\Cat{Lens}_{F,\theta}$ is strong symmetric monoidal.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.parameter_lens_action}
Let $\cat A$ and $\cat C$ be symmetric monoidal, let $\Fun T$ be a strong monad on $\cat C$, and let $J\colon\cat A\to\cat C$ be a strong monoidal functor such that each object $J(a)$ carries a comonoid structure $(\delta_{J(a)},\varepsilon_{J(a)})$ in $\cat C$ and each morphism $J(u)$ is a comonoid homomorphism. Then we have an action $\cat A\times\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}\to\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}$ given by
\[
a\cdot\lensob c\coloneqq\binom{\inpt c}{J(a)\otimes\outp c}.
\]
If moreover $\Fun T$ is a monoidal monad, this action makes $\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}$ a monoidal actegory.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given $f\colon\lensob c\to\lensob d$ in $\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}$ and $u\colon a\to a'$ in $\cat A$, set
\[
u\cdot f\coloneqq\binom{(\varepsilon_{J(a)}\otimes\outp c\otimes\inpt d)\then\inpt f}{J(u)\otimes\outp f}\colon
\binom{\inpt c}{J(a)\otimes\outp c}
\to
\binom{\inpt d}{J(a')\otimes\outp d}.
\]
Checking functoriality reduces immediately to the strength axioms for $\sigma$---naturality in its first argument together with unit-coherence give $\sigma_{X,Y}\then\Fun T(\varepsilon_X\otimes\id_Y)=\varepsilon_X\otimes\id_{\Fun T Y}$---and the comonoid-homomorphism assumption on $J$, i.e.\ that $\varepsilon_{J(a)}$ equals the composite
\[
J(a)\To{J(u)}J(a')\To{\varepsilon_{J(a')}} I.
\]

When $\Fun T$ is monoidal, $\Lens{\cat C}^{\Fun T}$ is symmetric monoidal by \cref{prop.backward_comonad}, and the comparisons $J(I)\cong I$ and $(a\otimes a')\cdot(\lensob c\otimes\lensob d)\cong(a\cdot\lensob c)\otimes(a'\cdot\lensob d)$ come directly from the strong monoidality of $J$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def.T_monoid}
Let $(\Fun{T},\eta,\mu,\tau)$ be a monoidal monad on a symmetric monoidal category $\cat{C}$. A \emph{$\Fun{T}$-monoid in $\cat{C}$} is a $\otimes$-monoid $(z,e_z,m_z)$ in $\cat{C}$
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
z\ar[r,"\eta_z"]\ar[dr,equal]\& \Fun{T}z\ar[d,"\alpha"]\\
\& z
\end{tikzcd}
\hspace{1in}
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
\Fun{T}\Fun{T}z\ar[r,"\mu_z"]\ar[d,"\Fun{T}\alpha"']\& \Fun{T}z\ar[d,"\alpha"]\\
\Fun{T}z\ar[r,"\alpha"']\& z
\end{tikzcd}
\]
together with a $\Fun{T}$-algebra structure $\alpha\colon\Fun{T}z\to z$ such that $\alpha$ is a monoid homomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.T_monoid}
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
I\ar[r,"\tau_0"]\ar[d,"e_z"']\& \Fun{T}I\ar[r,"\Fun{T}e_z"]\& \Fun{T}z\ar[d,"\alpha"]\\
z\ar[r,"\eta_z"']\& \Fun{T}z\ar[r,"\alpha"']\& z
\end{tikzcd}
\hspace{1in}
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
\Fun{T}z\otimes\Fun{T}z\ar[r,"\tau_{z,z}"]\ar[d,"\alpha\otimes\alpha"']\& \Fun{T}(z\otimes z)\ar[r,"\Fun{T}m_z"]\& \Fun{T}z\ar[d,"\alpha"]\\
z\otimes z\ar[rr,"m_z"']\&\& z
\end{tikzcd}
\qedhere
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{rmk.T_monoid_EM}
A $\Fun{T}$-monoid in $\cat{C}$ is the same data as a $\otimes$-monoid in the Eilenberg--Moore category $\cat{C}^{\Fun{T}}$, equivalently a $\Fun{T}$-algebra in $\Cat{Mon}_\otimes(\cat{C})$. The two presentations agree because $\Fun{T}$ being monoidal is exactly the condition that the monoidal structure on $\cat{C}$ lifts to one on $\cat{C}^{\Fun{T}}$ (equivalently, that $\Fun{T}$ lifts to a monad on $\Cat{Mon}_\otimes(\cat{C})$); see~\cite{kock1972strong, seal2013tensors}.
\end{remark}

\end{corollary}

\section{Reactive vector spaces and the cotangent endofunctor}\label{sec.rvect}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

We will eventually define $\srwd$ as the operad underlying $\para{\rvect}{\plmfd}$; in \cref{sec.rvect_def} we define the parameterizing category $\rvect$ (whose objects encode the data to turn covectors into vectors), a symplectic example $T^*V$, and the exponential $\exp_V$ that will be used to perform the Euler update. \cref{sec.TT} packages $T^*$ as a strong monoidal endofunctor of $\rvect$ and introduces the two pieces of phase-space data that build the phase integrator---\emph{monoidal 1-forms} (including the kinetic and dissipation 1-forms) and \emph{monoidal readouts}---together with their combination into a single update.

\begin{notation}\label{not.rvect_polynomials}
Going forward we lighten notation in two ways. First, we will denote to the underlying set $\absval V:\smset$ of a finite-dimensional vector space $V:\vect$ simply by $V$; in particular $\Store(V)=V\yon^V$. Second, we will denote the composite $\rvect\To{\inc}\mfd\To{\cot}\poly$ simply as $\cot\colon\rvect\to\poly$ and use \eqref{eqn.tangent_vec} and \eqref{eqn.canonical_dual_sum} to make the following identifications:
\[
\cotof V=V\yon^{V^*}\qqand\cotof{T^*V}=(V\oplus V^*)\yon^{V^*\oplus V}.
\]
\end{notation}

\subsection{Reactive vector spaces}\label{sec.rvect_def}

A reactive vector space is a finite-dimensional real vector space $V$ equipped with a smooth bundle map $T^*V\to TV$, turning covectors into tangent vectors that serve as state updates.

\begin{definition}\label{def.rvect}
A \emph{reactive vector space} is a finite-dimensional real vector space $V$ together with a smooth map
\[
\sharpR_V\colon V\to\vect(V^*,V),
\]
the \hypertarget{term.sharp_map}{\emph{sharp}} map, where we write $\sharpR_x\coloneqq\sharpR_V(x)\colon V^*\to V$ for each $x\in V$.\footnote{The terminology follows~\cite{capucci2024organizing}, where a smooth section of $[T^*X,TX]$ over a manifold $X$ is called a \emph{reaction}; the above is the vector-space case.}%
\footnote{
Equivalently, $\sharpR_V$ is a smooth bundle map $T^*V\to TV$ over $V$, under the canonical identifications $T_xV\cong V$ and $T^*_xV\cong V^*$. It will be more convenient to use the above form.
}
We say the reaction is \emph{constant} if $\sharpR_x=\sharpR_0$ for all $x$. We call a reactive vector space \emph{nondegenerate} if $\sharpR_x$ is an isomorphism for every $x$; in that case the pointwise inverse $\flat_x\coloneqq\sharpR_x\inv\colon V\to V^*$ is the \emph{flat} map and $\pairing{v,v'}_x\coloneqq\flat_x(v)(v')$ is the \emph{pairing at $x$}.

We define the category $\defineTerm{rvect}$ to have reactive vector spaces as objects---we write $\rv V\coloneqq(V,\sharpR_V)$ for the object and $V$ for its underlying vector space---and as morphisms $\rv V\to\rv W$ the linear isomorphisms $\phi\colon V\To{\cong}W$ that are \emph{sharp-equivariant}: for all $x\in V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.pairing_triangle}
\phi\circ\sharpR_x\circ\phi^*=\sharpR_{\phi(x)}
\end{equation}
as maps $W^*\to W$.%
\footnote{
Restricting to isomorphisms (making $\rvect$ a groupoid) is essential for our development below: each $\rvect$-morphism is used both as $\phi$ on vectors and as $(\phi^*)\inv$ on covectors, e.g.\ in $T^*\phi$ \eqref{eqn.TT_phi} below.
}
In the nondegenerate case, \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle} is equivalent to pointwise pairing-preservation: $\pairing{\phi v,\phi v'}_{\phi(x)}=\pairing{v,v'}_x$.

Where the sharp is understood, or is the canonical one (as on $T^*V$, \cref{prop.canonical_symplectic_pairing}), we abuse notation and write simply $V$ for $\rv V$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.rvect_monoidal}
Direct sum gives $\rvect$ a symmetric monoidal structure $(0,\oplus)$, with the zero space as unit and direct-sum sharp $\sharpR_{V\oplus W}$ given by
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.directsum_sharp}
\sharpR_{(v,w)}\coloneqq\sharpR_v\oplus\sharpR_w\colon V^*\oplus W^*\to V\oplus W.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A pointwise direct sum of smooth maps is again smooth. Sharp-equivariance \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle} is preserved by direct sums, and coherence is inherited from $(\vect,0,\oplus)$.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.rvect_smc_action}
The composite $\rvect\to\vect\inj\mfd$ is strong symmetric monoidal, so $\rvect$ acts on $\mfd$ via $((V,\sharpR_V),M)\mapsto V\times M$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The forgetful $\rvect\to\vect$ preserves direct sums (which are products in $\vect$); the inclusion $\vect\inj\mfd$ preserves products.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.canonical_symplectic_pairing}
For any finite-dimensional vector space $V:\vect$, the direct sum $V\oplus V^*$ is a reactive vector space under the \emph{canonical symplectic pairing}: at every basepoint $w\in V\oplus V^*$,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.canonical_form}
\pairing{(x,\xi),(x',\xi')}_{w} \;=\; \xi'(x)-\xi(x').
\end{equation}
The right-hand side does not depend on $w$, so the map is constant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The form in \eqref{eqn.canonical_form} is bilinear. To see that it is nondegenerate, suppose $(x,\xi)$ pairs to $0$ with every $(x',\xi')$. Taking $x'=0$ gives $\xi'(x)=0$ for all $\xi'\in V^*$, hence $x=0$; taking $\xi'=0$ gives $\xi(x')=0$ for all $x'\in V$, hence $\xi=0$.\qedhere
\end{proof}

For the canonical symplectic pairing, under \eqref{eqn.canonical_dual_sum}, the associated sharp map is the constant map
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.canonical_sharp}
\defineTerm{sharp_symp}_{V\oplus V^*}\colon V^*\oplus V\to V\oplus V^*,\qquad(\xi,x)\mapsto(x,-\xi).
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Symplectic sharp produces perpendicular vectors]\label{rmk.symplectic_perpendicular}
For any covector $\alpha=(\xi,x)\in V^*\oplus V$, the resulting vector $\sharpS_{V\oplus V^*}(\alpha)=(x,-\xi)\in V\oplus V^*$ \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp} is annihilated by $\alpha$: pairing the two via \eqref{eqn.canonical_dual_sum},
\[
\alpha\bigl(\sharpS_{V\oplus V^*}(\alpha)\bigr) = \xi(x) + (-\xi)(x) = 0.
\]
Geometrically, the symplectic sharp ``rotates'' each covector to a vector ``perpendicular'' to it. In particular, when $\alpha=dH|_w$ is the differential of a smooth function $H\colon V\oplus V^*\to\rr$, the vector $\sharpS_{V\oplus V^*}(dH|_w)$ is tangent to the level set $\{H=H(w)\}$, so the flow it generates conserves $H$.\qedhere
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Euclidean sharp]\label{ex.euclidean_sharp}
A finite-dimensional real vector space $V$ equipped with an inner product---a symmetric, positive-definite bilinear form $\pairing{-,-}\colon V\otimes V\to\rr$---is a nondegenerate reactive vector space with constant sharp
\begin{equation*}
\defineTerm{sharpEuc}\colon V^*\To{\cong}V,
\end{equation*}
the inverse of $\flat_\euc(v)\coloneqq\pairing{v,-}\colon V\to V^*$. This is the example underlying the gradient-descent dynamics of \cref{sec.dl_warmup}, where ``descent'' corresponds to scaling the sharp by a negative \emph{learning rate} $\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}>0$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.learning_sharp}
\sharpR_x=-\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}\sharpEuc{}.
\end{equation}
The minus sign is what makes the dynamics descent rather than ascent.\qedhere
\end{example}

The \emph{exponential} of a reactive vector space $(V,\sharpR_V)$ is the map%
\footnote{Strictly speaking, what we denote $\exp_{\rv V}$ is the composite $T^*V\To{\id\oplus\sharpR}V\oplus V\to V$ of $\sharpR$ with the genuine exponential. We combine them for brevity.}
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.flow_cot}
\exp_{\rv V}\colon T^*V\to V,\qquad (x,\xi)\longmapsto x+\sharpR_x(\xi),
\end{equation}
which at each $(x,\xi)$ is the first-order Euler step from $x$ along the tangent vector $\sharpR_x(\xi)\in T_xV\cong V$. In the constant case $\sharpR_x=\sharpR_0$, $\exp_{\rv V}(x,\xi)$ coincides with the time-$1$ flow from $x$ of the constant left-invariant vector field $\sharpR_0(\xi)$. It is natural in $\rvect$-isomorphisms by linearity and the sharp-equivariance condition \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle}.

\subsection{The cotangent endofunctor}\label{sec.TT}

For a reactive vector space $(V,\sharpR_V):\rvect$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.TT_def}
T^*V\coloneqq V\oplus V^*.
\end{equation}
This agrees with taking the cotangent bundle of $V$ as a manifold.

It is naturally a reactive vector space via the canonical symplectic pairing, whose sharp map is \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp}.
For a $\rvect$-morphism $\phi\colon V\To{\cong} W$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.TT_phi}
T^*\phi\coloneqq\phi\oplus(\phi^*)\inv.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.TT_endofunctors}
Equipping $T^*V$ with the canonical symplectic pairing of \cref{prop.canonical_symplectic_pairing}, the assignments \eqref{eqn.TT_def}, \eqref{eqn.TT_phi} define an endofunctor $T^*\colon\rvect\to\rvect$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\phi\colon V\to W$ is a linear isomorphism so is $T^*\phi$. Functoriality is obvious. Writing $\psi\coloneqq(\phi^*)\inv\colon V^*\to W^*$, the defining property of $\phi^*$ states that $(\phi^*\xi')(v) = \xi'(\phi v)$ for all $\xi'\in W^*\text{ and }v\in V.$ Setting $\xi'\coloneqq\psi\xi$ and using $\phi^*\circ\psi=\id_{V^*}$, we note that
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.dualeval}
(\psi\xi)(\phi v) = (\phi^*(\psi\xi))(v) = \xi(v).
\end{equation}
To verify sharp-equivariance \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle}, we equivalently check pairing-preservation (both pairings are nondegenerate). Fix any basepoint $w\in T^*V$ and calculate
\begin{align*}
\pairing{T^*\phi(x_1,\xi_1),T^*\phi(x_2,\xi_2)}_{T^*\phi(w)}
&= (\psi\xi_2)(\phi x_1)-(\psi\xi_1)(\phi x_2) && \text{[\eqref{eqn.TT_phi}, \eqref{eqn.canonical_form}]}\\
&= \xi_2(x_1)-\xi_1(x_2) && \text{[\eqref{eqn.dualeval}]}\\
&= \pairing{(x_1,\xi_1),(x_2,\xi_2)}_{w} && \text{[\eqref{eqn.canonical_form}]};
\end{align*}
both sides are independent of $w$ by constancy of the canonical symplectic pairing.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Constancy of $\sharpS_{T^*V}$]\label{rmk.constant_canonical_sharp}
The canonical symplectic pairing depends only on the underlying vector space $V$, not on $\sharpR_V$, so $\sharpS_{V\oplus V^*}$ is the constant map \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp} even when $\sharpR_V$ varies on $V$. Later in \cref{sec.dynamics_functor}, the phase-space dynamics will see only this constant $\sharpS_{V\oplus V^*}$, regardless of any variation in $\sharpR_V$. However, $\sharpR_V$ is not discarded by the framework: it appears in the exponential $\exp_{\rv V}\colon T^*V\to V$ of \eqref{eqn.flow_cot}.
\qedhere
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.TT_monoidal}
The endofunctor $T^*\colon\rvect\to\rvect$ is strong symmetric monoidal with respect to $(0,\oplus)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The unit comparison is the identity since $T^*0=0\oplus 0^*=0$. The product comparison is the swap
\begin{equation*}
\sigma\colon T^*(V_1\oplus V_2)\To{\cong}T^*V_1\oplus T^*V_2,\quad ((x_1,x_2),(\xi_1,\xi_2))\mapsto((x_1,\xi_1),(x_2,\xi_2)).
\end{equation*}
It is a linear isomorphism, using $(V_1\oplus V_2)^*\cong V_1^*\oplus V_2^*$. For sharp-equivariance \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle}, the canonical symplectic sharp on $T^*(V_1\oplus V_2)$ sends $(\xi_1,\xi_2,x_1,x_2)\mapsto(x_1,x_2,-\xi_1,-\xi_2)$ by \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp}. Conjugating by $\sigma$ gives $(\xi_1,x_1,\xi_2,x_2)\mapsto(x_1,-\xi_1,x_2,-\xi_2)$, which is the direct-sum sharp $\sharpS_{T^*V_1}\oplus\sharpS_{T^*V_2}$ from \eqref{eqn.directsum_sharp}.\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.TT_projection_diagonal_monoidal}
The bundle projections and diagonals are smooth maps
\[
\pi_V\colon T^*V\to V,
\qquad
\Delta_{T^*V}\colon T^*V\to T^*V\times T^*V,
\]
which form monoidal natural transformations as follows between functors $\rvect\to\mfd$:
\[
\pi\colon\inc\circ T^*\Rightarrow\inc,\qquad
\Delta_{T^*}\colon\inc\circ T^*\Rightarrow(\inc\circ T^*)\times(\inc\circ T^*).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $\pi$, naturality in $\phi\colon V\To{\cong} W$ is the identity $\pi_W\circ T^*\phi=\phi\circ\pi_V$, and monoidality is the identity $\pi_{V\oplus W}=\pi_V\times\pi_W$ under the product comparisons $T^*(V\oplus W)\cong T^*V\times T^*W$ and $V\oplus W\cong V\times W$. Diagonals are natural, and $\Delta_{T^*(V\oplus W)}=\Delta_{T^*V}\times\Delta_{T^*W}$ under $T^*(V\oplus W)\cong T^*V\times T^*W$.\qedhere
\end{proof}

Whiskering $\pi$ by $T^*$ we obtain a monoidal natural transformation
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.pi_whiskered}
\pi_{T^*}\colon\inc\circ T^*\circ T^*\Rightarrow\inc\circ T^*,\qquad (\pi_{T^*})_V=\pi_{T^*V}\colon T^*(T^*V)\to T^*V.
\end{equation}

Alongside the exponential \eqref{eqn.flow_cot}, the reaction datum $\sharpR_x(\xi)$ admits a second packaging as a 1-form on $T^*V$, used later in the phase integrator \eqref{eqn.phase_integrator}. We first give the abstract notion.

\begin{definition}\label{def.monoidal_one_form}
A \emph{monoidal 1-form on $T^*$} is a smooth family of sections $\omega_V\colon T^*V\to T^*(T^*V)$, natural in $\rvect$-isomorphisms, that is monoidal over direct sums: $\omega_{V\oplus W}=\omega_V\oplus\omega_W$. In other words, $\omega\colon\inc\circ T^*\Rightarrow\inc\circ T^*\circ T^*$ is a monoidal section of the bundle projection $\pi_{T^*V}\colon T^*(T^*V)\to T^*V$, from \eqref{eqn.pi_whiskered}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.one_forms_monoid}
Under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, the monoidal 1-forms on $T^*$ form a real vector space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each fiber $T^*_{(x,\xi)}(T^*V)\cong V^*\oplus V$ is a real vector space, so given monoidal 1-forms $\omega,\omega'$ and a scalar $c\in\rr$ we may set $(\omega+\omega')_V(x,\xi)\coloneqq\omega_V(x,\xi)+\omega'_V(x,\xi)$ and $(c\,\omega)_V(x,\xi)\coloneqq c\,\omega_V(x,\xi)$, the operations taken in that fiber. These are again smooth, natural in $\rvect$-isomorphisms (which act linearly on each fiber), and monoidal, e.g.\
\[
(\omega+\omega')_{V\oplus W}=\omega_{V\oplus W}+\omega'_{V\oplus W}=(\omega_V\oplus\omega_W)+(\omega'_V\oplus\omega'_W)=(\omega+\omega')_V\oplus(\omega+\omega')_W.
\qedhere\]
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Kinetic 1-form]\label{ex.kinetic_one_form}
The \emph{kinetic 1-form} of $(V,\sharpR_V)$ is the section
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.kinetic_one_form}
\defineTerm{beta_kin}_V\colon T^*V\to T^*(T^*V),\qquad
(x,\xi)\mapsto(0,\sharpR_x(\xi)),
\end{equation}
using the identification $V^*\oplus V\cong T^*_{(x,\xi)}(T^*V)$ from \eqref{eqn.T_products} and \eqref{eqn.tangent_vec}.%
\footnote{We borrow the name from physics: when $\sharpR$ is constant and symmetric, $\beta_V$ is the differential of the kinetic energy $\tfrac12\xi(\sharpR_0(\xi))$ (see \cref{rmk.constant_inverse_mass_hamiltonian}).}
It is a monoidal 1-form on $T^*$: naturality follows from sharp-equivariance \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle}, and monoidality holds because $\sharpR_{V\oplus W}=\sharpR_V\oplus\sharpR_W$ (\cref{prop.rvect_monoidal}).

Geometrically, $\beta$ packages the same datum as $\exp$: indeed, there is an equality of bundle maps $\sharp=d\pi_V\circ\sharpS_{T^*V}\circ\beta_V$, and the Euler step of $\sharp$ on $V$ is $\exp_{\rv V}$ \eqref{eqn.flow_cot}.
\end{example}

More generally, when a 1-form $\omega(x,\xi)=(a,b)$ is fed through the symplectic sharp $\sharpS_{T^*V}$ and added to the state---as it will be under the phase integrator \eqref{eqn.phase_integrator} below---it contributes $(+b,-a)$ to the update, since $\sharpS$ swaps coordinates and negates the second \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp}. Thus the $V$-component of $\omega$ drives position and the $V^*$-component drives momentum with a sign flip: the kinetic 1-form ends up adding velocity $\sharp(\xi)$ to position, and the next example ends up dissipating momentum by $-c\xi$.

\begin{example}[Dissipation 1-form]\label{ex.dissipation_one_form}
The \emph{dissipation 1-form} is the section
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.dissipation}
\zeta_V\colon T^*V\to T^*(T^*V),\qquad
(x,\xi)\mapsto(\xi,\,0),
\end{equation}
a monoidal 1-form on $T^*$ by linearity and the $\oplus$-monoidality of the momentum coordinate. For a \emph{damping coefficient} $c\in[0,1]$, the scalar multiple $c\,\zeta$ is again a monoidal 1-form (\cref{prop.one_forms_monoid}); it dissipates a fraction $c$ of the momentum at each step.
\end{example}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.one_form_to_poly}
Every monoidal 1-form $\omega$ on $T^*$ induces a monoidal natural transformation
\[
\omega\colon \cot\circ T^*\Rightarrow\yon .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A section $\omega\colon M\to T^*M$ defines a polynomial map $\cotof{M}\to\yon$ whose position map is unique and whose backward map at $m$ is the function $1\to T^*_mM$ selecting $\omega(m)$. Applying this at $M=T^*V$ gives the components $\omega_V\colon\cotof{T^*V}\to\yon$. Naturality and monoidality are exactly the naturality and monoidality over $\oplus$ in \cref{def.monoidal_one_form}.%
\footnote{A monoidal natural transformation $\omega\colon\cot\circ T^*\to\yon$ may not be a monoidal 1-form because the former does not enforce smoothness, whereas the latter does.}
\qedhere
\end{proof}

Note that we abuse notation, denoting the induced natural transformation by the same symbol as the 1-form. For example, $\beta\colon\cot\circ T^*\Rightarrow\yon$ has components $\beta_V\colon\cotof{T^*V}\to\yon$ whose backward map at $(x,\xi)$ selects $(0,\sharpR_x(\xi))\in T^*_{(x,\xi)}(T^*V)$, and likewise $\zeta\colon\cot\circ T^*\Rightarrow\yon$ selects $(\xi,0)$ at $(x,\xi)$. We will see in \cref{def.one_form_lift,thm.functor} that every monoidal 1-form on $T^*$ defines a semantics $\srwd\to\org$. 

Where a 1-form feeds the backward pass, the following notion sets the position the forward pass reads.

\begin{definition}\label{def.monoidal_readout}
A \emph{monoidal readout on $T^*$} is a monoidal natural transformation $\chi\colon\inc\circ T^*\Rightarrow\inc$: a family of smooth maps $\chi_V\colon T^*V\to V$, natural in $\rvect$-isomorphisms and monoidal over direct sums, $\chi_{V\oplus W}=\chi_V\oplus\chi_W$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.readouts_vector_space}
Under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, the monoidal readouts on $T^*$ form a real vector space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The pointwise argument of \cref{prop.one_forms_monoid} applies verbatim, with values in $V$ in place of the fiber $V^*\oplus V$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.readout_examples}
The projection $\pi$ and the exponential $\exp$ \eqref{eqn.flow_cot} are monoidal readouts, as is the \emph{$\alpha$-step} $\exp^\alpha_V(x,\xi)\coloneqq x+\alpha\,\sharpR_x(\xi)$ for any $\alpha\in\rr$, which interpolates them.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The projection $\pi$ is a monoidal readout by \cref{lem.TT_projection_diagonal_monoidal}, and the exponential $\exp_{\rv V}(x,\xi)=x+\sharpR_x(\xi)$ is monoidal by linearity and sharp-equivariance \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle}. The $\alpha$-step $\exp^\alpha=(1-\alpha)\,\pi+\alpha\,\exp$ is then a monoidal readout by \cref{prop.readouts_vector_space}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.readout_to_poly}
Every monoidal readout $\chi$ on $T^*$ induces a monoidal natural transformation
\[
\chi\colon \cot\circ T^*\Rightarrow\cot ,
\]
with on-positions $\chi_V$ and on-directions the injection $\xi'\mapsto(\xi',0)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At each $V$, the on-positions $\chi_V\colon T^*V\to V$ and the backward maps $\xi'\mapsto(\xi',0)$, from $V^*$ to $V^*\oplus V\cong T^*_{(x,\xi)}(T^*V)$, constitute a polynomial map $\cotof{T^*V}\to\cotof{V}$.

For naturality at an $\rvect$-isomorphism $\phi\colon V\to W$, compare $\cotof{\phi}\circ\chi_V$ with $\chi_W\circ\cotof{T^*\phi}$. On positions these are $\phi\circ\chi_V$ and $\chi_W\circ T^*\phi$, equal because $\chi$ is natural (\cref{def.monoidal_readout}). On directions at $(x,\xi)$, a covector $\zeta'\in W^*$ pulls back along both maps to $(\phi^*\zeta',0)$.

For $\oplus$-monoidality, on positions $\chi_{V\oplus W}=\chi_V\oplus\chi_W$ since $\chi$ is a monoidal readout (\cref{def.monoidal_readout}), and on directions the inclusion $V^*\oplus W^*\to(V^*\oplus V)\oplus(W^*\oplus W)$ is the direct sum of those for $V$ and $W$.
\end{proof}

The following lemma combines monoidal readouts and 1-forms into a package we will use later.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.combined_update}
A monoidal readout $\chi$ and a monoidal 1-form $\omega$ combine into a monoidal natural transformation $u_{\chi,\omega}\colon\cot\circ T^*\Rightarrow\cot$, given by the composite
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em]
\cot\circ T^*
  \ar[rrr, bend left=12, "u_{\chi,\omega}"]
  \ar[r, "\cotof{\Delta_{T^*\blank}}"']
& (\cot\circ T^*)\otimes(\cot\circ T^*)
  \ar[r, "\chi\otimes\omega"']
& \cot\otimes\yon
  \ar[r, "\cong"']
& \cot.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
On positions $u_{\chi,\omega}$ is $\chi_V$; on directions at $(x,\xi)$ it sends $\xi'\mapsto(\xi',0)+\omega_V(x,\xi)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each arrow is a monoidal natural transformation: $\cotof{\Delta_{T^*\blank}}$ by \cref{lem.TT_projection_diagonal_monoidal,prop.cot_monoidal}, the readout $\chi$ by \cref{lem.readout_to_poly}, the 1-form $\omega$ by \cref{lem.one_form_to_poly}, and the unitor $\cot\otimes\yon\cong\cot$ because $\yon$ is the monoidal unit. On positions, $\cotof{\Delta_{T^*\blank}}$ is the diagonal and $\chi\otimes\omega$ applies $\chi_V$ in the first factor while $\omega$ lands in $\yon$, so the composite reads out $\chi_V$. On directions at $(x,\xi)$, a covector $\xi'\in V^*$ pulls back through $\chi\otimes\omega$ to $(\xi',0)$ and $\omega_V(x,\xi)$ in the two factors, which the diagonal adds.
\end{proof}

\section{Potentialized manifold lenses}\label{sec.potentialized_lenses}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

Now that we have $\rvect$ in hand, we can assemble the syntax operad $\srwd$, whose morphisms are $\rvect$-parameterized manifold lenses equipped with a \emph{potential}---a smooth function $U\colon X\to\rr$---on the backward pass. This is the source of the $\rr$ throughout the section. \cref{sec.potentialized_cokleisli} specializes the backward-monad construction of \cref{sec.backwards} to $(\rr\times\blank)$ on $\mfd$, producing $\plmfd$; \cref{sec.potential_algebra} defines the potential monoid $\potd$ used by Moore internalization; \cref{sec.forming_potlens} parameterizes $\plmfd$ over $\rvect$ to form $\srwd$ and provides an explicit diagrammatic depiction of its morphisms.

\subsection{Potentialized coKleisli lenses}\label{sec.potentialized_cokleisli}

\begin{lemma}\label{lemma.monoid_to_monad}
If $\cat{C}$ is a symmetric monoidal category and $(M,e,(*))$ is a commutative monoid object in it, then $M\otimes\blank\colon\cat{C}\to\cat{C}$ carries a monoidal monad structure, functorially in $(\cat C,M)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The monad unit and multiplication are $e\otimes\id$ and $(*)\otimes\id$. The monoidal structure has unitor $e\colon I\to M\cong M\otimes I$ and productor
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.monoid_productor}
(M\otimes X)\otimes(M\otimes Y)\cong M\otimes M\otimes X\otimes Y\To{(*)\otimes\id}M\otimes X\otimes Y.
\end{equation}
The coherence diagrams arise from the monoid laws and commutativity of $M$. Functoriality in $(\cat C,M)$ is straightforward.
\end{proof}

Since $(\rr,0,+)$ is a commutative Lie group, \cref{prop.cot_hopf} gives a commutative monoid---in fact Hopf monoid---object $\cotof{\rr}$ in $\poly$. By \cref{lemma.monoid_to_monad} there are corresponding monoidal monads $(\rr\times\blank)$ and $(\cotof{\rr}\otimes\blank)$ respectively on $\mfd$ and $\poly$, and by \cref{prop.backward_comonad} there are corresponding colax monoidal comonads on $\Lens{\mfd}$ and $\Lens{\poly}$. We let $(\plmfd,\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0},\times)$ and $(\plpoly,\binom{\yon}{\yon},\otimes)$ denote the corresponding coKleisli categories.

A map in $\plmfd$ is a tuple $f=\big(\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f},U\big)\colon\lensob M\to\lensob N$ with
\[
\outp f\colon\outp M\to\outp N,\qquad
\inpt f\colon\outp M\times\inpt N\to\inpt M,\qquad
U\colon\outp M\times\inpt N\to\rr.
\]
We call such maps \emph{potentialized manifold lenses}.
A map $\lensob p\to\lensob q$ in $\plpoly$ is a pair
\[
\outp f\colon\outp p\to\outp q,\qquad
\inpt f\colon\outp p\otimes\inpt q\to\cotof{\rr}\otimes\inpt p.
\]

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.cot_lifts_lens_potential}
The strong symmetric monoidal functor $\cot\colon\mfd\to\poly$ lifts to a strong symmetric monoidal functor
\[
\Lens\cot\colon\plmfd\to\plpoly.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem.lens_T_functoriality} with $F=\cot$ (strong symmetric monoidal by \cref{prop.cot_monoidal}) and the monad morphism $\cotof{\rr\times\blank}\Rightarrow\cotof{\rr}\otimes\cotof{\blank}$ supplied by \cref{lemma.monoid_to_monad}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The potential algebra \texorpdfstring{$\potd$}{d}}\label{sec.potential_algebra}

In \cref{lem.Theta_poly_potential} we will apply the Moore internalization from \cref{thm.Theta_T_alpha}, which works for any $\Fun T$-monoid $(z,e,m,\alpha)$ in $\poly$. For simplicity of presentation, and with a view toward our physics and learning applications, we take $z\coloneqq\yon$. It has a unique monoid structure, but $\alpha\colon\cotof{\rr}\to\yon$ is extra data.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.alpha_constant}
Consider the monad $\Fun{T}\coloneqq(\cotof{\rr}\otimes\blank)$ on $\poly$. The $\Fun{T}$-monoid structures $(z,e,m,\alpha)$ for which $z=\yon$ are in bijection with real numbers $r:\rr$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
There is a unique $\otimes$-monoid structure on $\yon$. Maps $\alpha\colon\cotof{\rr}\to\yon$ are in bijection with (not-necessarily continuous) covector fields $\omega\colon\rr\to T^*\rr$ on $\rr$, where $\alpha$ is identified with $\omega(x)\coloneqq\bk{\alpha}{x}(*)$. The first condition of \eqref{eqn.T_monoid} is vacuous. Thus the only condition on $\alpha$ necessary to make it a $\Fun{T}$-monoid is that the following equation holds for all $x_1,x_2\in\rr$
\[
\left(\omega(x_1),\omega(x_2)\right)=
\left(\omega(x_1+x_2),\omega(x_1+x_2)\right).
\]
Setting $x_2\coloneqq 0$, we find $\omega(0)=\omega(x_1)$ for all $x_1$. In other words, $\omega=r$ is constant at some $r:\rr$. And conversely, any constant covector field is a $\Fun{T}$-monoid.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[General form]\label{rmk.alpha_general}
\Cref{lem.alpha_constant} generalizes in a couple of ways. First, $\cotof{R}$ can be replaced by any commutative Hopf monoid. Second, $\yon$ can be replaced by any $\otimes$-monoid $(z,e,(*))$ in $\poly$ that is \emph{coupled} in the sense that, for every $Z_1,Z_2:z(1)$, the backward action of multiplication $\bk{(*)}{(Z_1,Z_2)}\colon z[Z_1*Z_2]\to z[Z_1]\times z[Z_2]$ is surjective. For any commutative Hopf monoid $m$ in $\poly$ and any coupled $\otimes$-monoid $z$, the $(m\otimes\blank)$-monoid structures on $z$ for which the $m(1)$-action on $z(1)$ is trivial are in bijection with $m[e_m]$, the directions of $m$ at its unit position. For $m=\cotof{\rr}$ this is $T^*_0\rr\cong\rr$.

As examples, note that any $\otimes$-monoid of the form $M\yon=\sum_{m\in M}\yon$ for a monoid $(M,1,\cdot)$ in $\smset$ is coupled trivially (every position has one direction), recovering the $\yon$ case. A more interesting example is the polynomial $\mathrm{rand}\coloneqq\sum_{N:\nn}\sum_{P\in\Delta_N}\yon^N$ of finite random variables (called $\mathrm{lott}$ in~\cite{Spivak2023lotteries}), which underlies (the monad of) the operad of simplices~\cite[Ch.~12]{leinster2021entropy}. Taking $z=\mathrm{rand}$ would give stochastic covector fields, and hence stochastic update rules after applying the dynamics functor; we do not pursue this variation here. For $m\coloneqq\cotof{G}$ with $G$ a commutative Lie group, $m[e_m]=T^*_{e_G}G=\lie g^*$, the dual of its Lie algebra; for $G\coloneqq\rr$ this recovers \cref{lem.alpha_constant}.
\end{remark}

The role of $\rr$ throughout \cref{sec.potentialized_lenses} is to act as the recipient of \emph{potentials}: specializing \cref{lem.alpha_constant} to $m\coloneqq\cotof{\rr}$ and $z\coloneqq\yon$, we choose the homomorphism corresponding to $r\coloneqq+1$ and write the corresponding algebra map as
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.d_potential}
\defineTerm{potd}\colon\cotof{\rr}\to\yon,\qquad\bk{\potd}{x}(*)\coloneqq +1\in T^*_x\rr.
\end{equation}
We call it $\potd$ because this is the choice for which potentials contribute their usual differentials. Indeed, for a smooth map $U\colon X\to\rr$, the composite $\potd\circ\cotof{U}\colon\cotof{X}\to\yon$ corresponds to the covector field $dU$ on $X$: tracing on directions,
\[
\bk{\potd\circ\cotof{U}}{x}(*)=(T_x U)^\top(+1)=dU|_x
\]
exactly as in \eqref{eqn.differential}.

\subsection{Forming \texorpdfstring{$\srwd$}{SRWD}}\label{sec.forming_potlens}

There is an action of $\rvect$ on $\plmfd$ from \cref{lem.parameter_lens_action} (taking $\cat A\coloneqq\rvect$, $\cat C\coloneqq\mfd$, $J\coloneqq\inc$, $\Fun T\coloneqq\rr\times\blank$) and the resulting Para construction is symmetric monoidal.

\begin{definition}[$\srwd$]\label{def.potlens}
The symmetric monoidal category $\potlens$ is defined by
\[
\defineTerm{potlens}\coloneqq\para{\rvect}{\plmfd}.
\]
We write $\defineTerm{srwd}$ for its underlying operad.
\end{definition}

A morphism $f\colon\lensob{M_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\lensob{M_K}\to\lensob{N}$ is a tuple $f=\left((V,\sharpR_V),\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f},U\right)$ with $(V,\sharpR_V):\rvect$ and smooth maps
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.srw_morphism}
\begin{gathered}
\outp f\colon V\times\prod_{i=1}^K\outp M_i\to\outp N,\qquad
\inpt f\colon V\times\prod_{i=1}^K\outp M_i\times\inpt N\to\prod_{i=1}^K\inpt M_i,\\
U\colon V\times\prod_{i=1}^K\outp M_i\times\inpt N\to\rr.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

From this point on we work in $\srwd$. Multimorphisms in $\srwd$ are called \emph{smooth rewiring diagrams}. When the parameter is the trivial reactive vector space $\rr^0$, we call such a morphism a \emph{smooth (static) wiring diagram}; when its codomain is the monoidal unit $I=\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}$, we call it a \emph{closed system}.

\[
\Lens{\finset\op}\To{\Lens{\,\rr^{\!\blank}}}\lmfd\To{\Lens{\eta}}\plmfd\To{\Para_{\rr^0}}\srwd
\]
where $\eta\colon\id_\mfd\Rightarrow\rr\times\blank$ is the unique monad map from the initial monad $\id$, and $\Para_{\rr^0}$ regards a potentialized manifold lens as a smooth rewiring diagram with unit parameter $\rr^0$. This chain gives rise to progressively richer smooth rewiring diagrams.

The simplest---those coming from the leftmost category in the chain---are static wiring diagrams, which come from ``prisms,'' i.e.\ lenses in $\finset\op$, as we saw in \cref{ex.lens_finsetop}. In this case the maps in \eqref{eqn.srw_morphism} have trivial $V=\rr^0$-factor, all the maps factor as projections and diagonals, and the potential is zero. These simplest maps give the syntax for something like ``traced symmetric monoidal semicategories," as discussed in \cref{sec.plenspot_ops}.


We can realize each such static wiring diagram as a lens between manifolds via $\Lens{\,\rr^{\!\blank}}$ (\cref{lemma.lens_pow}), but compositions coming from $\lmfd$ are more general: there are many more maps $M\times N\to M$ than just the projection. Moving to $\plmfd$ adds a potential but still no parameter space $V$: it assigns a real number to the interaction data consisting of the internal-box outputs together with the external-box input, e.g.\ a prediction error.

Finally, the general smooth rewiring diagrams are morphisms in $\srwd$: the interaction pattern itself depends smoothly on a parameter in a reactive vector space $(V,\sharpR_V)$, and under the dynamics functor that parameter is updated by the differential of the potential. Those with trivial parameter $\rr^0$ are the ones that arrive from $\plmfd$ via $\Para_{\rr^0}$. Exactly how the more general smooth rewiring dynamics work is the subject of \cref{ch.potential_driven_dynamics}.

\section{Integrator semantics}\label{sec.integrator_semantics}

In \cref{sec.org_as_para} we show how to repackage $\org_\cong$ as a Para construction over the store action of $\smsetiso$ on $\poly$. \Cref{sec.integrators} then introduces \emph{integrators} and singles out the two used in this paper: the configuration integrator $\intg_\theta$ and the phase integrator $\intg_\beta$.

\subsection{Coalgebras as Para over the store action}\label{sec.org_as_para}

Following the convention of \cref{sec.prelim}, we refer to $(2,1)$-categories simply as categories, though we do take care of the 2-dimensional isomorphisms in proofs. Recall from \cref{lem.store_monoidal} that $\Store\colon\smsetiso\to\poly$ sends $S:\smset$ to the store comonad $S\yon^S$.


\begin{proposition}\label{prop.org_as_para}
There is an isomorphism of categories
\[
\para{\Cat{Set}_{\cong}}{\poly}\To{\cong}\org_\cong.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both $\para{\Cat{Set}_{\cong}}{\poly}$ and $\org_\cong$ are $(2,1)$-categories with object set $\ob\poly$, and the mapping groupoids $p\to q$ agree
\begin{align*}
	\para{\Cat{Set}_{\cong}}{\poly}(p,q)&=
  \sum_{S:\smsetiso}\poly(\Store(S)\otimes p, q)\\&\cong
  \sum_{S:\smsetiso}\poly(S\yon^S,[p,q])\\&\cong
  \sum_{S:\smsetiso}\poly(S,[p,q]\tri S)=\org_\cong(p,q)
\end{align*}
by \cref{eqn.dirichlet_adjunction,prop.coalg_as_poly_map}. It remains to show that the identities and composites agree.

In both cases the identity is indexed by the monoidal unit $1$, and $1\To{\id}\poly(p,p)=[p,p](1)$ and $\yon\To{\id}[p,p]$ are identified as above. Given composable maps $p\To{(S,\varphi)}q\To{(T,\psi)}r$, in both cases the composite is of the form $(TS,\psi\circ\varphi)$, and the same identifications match these composites.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.poly_to_org}
Let $\cat A$ be a symmetric monoidal category and let $F\colon\cat A\to\smsetiso$ be strong symmetric monoidal. Let $\cat A$ act on $\poly$ via the strong monoidal composite $\Store\circ F\colon\cat A\to\poly$ as in \cref{lem.smc_action}. There is an identity-on-objects normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
\[
D_F\colon\para{\Store\circ F}{\poly}\to\org_\cong.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The induced action sends $(V,p)\mapsto \Store(F(V))\otimes p=F(V)\yon^{F(V)}\otimes p$. The following action square obviously commutes:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&, column sep=60pt]
\cat A\times\poly\ar[r,"\Store(F(\blank))\otimes\blank"]\ar[d,"F\times\id_\poly"', ""{name=L}]\& \poly\ar[d, equal, ""' {name=R}]\\
\smsetiso\times\poly\ar[r,"\Store(\blank)\otimes\blank"']\& \poly.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\Cref{prop.para_strong_induced,prop.para_square} gives $\para{\Store\circ F}{\poly}\to\para{\smsetiso}{\poly}$, and composing with $\para{\smsetiso}{\poly}\cong\org_\cong$ from \cref{prop.org_as_para} yields the claim. For normality, note that $F$ being strong monoidal gives $F(I_{\cat A})\cong 1$, so $D_F$ sends the unit $(I_{\cat A},\yon)$ to $\Store(F(I_{\cat A}))\otimes\yon\cong\yon$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Integrators}\label{sec.integrators}

Looking ahead, \cref{lem.potlens_to_para_poly} will assemble our constructions so far into maps of the form $\cotof{V}\otimes p\to q$, or equivalently $\cotof{V}\to[p,q]$. Our goal in this section is to turn them into $[p,q]$-coalgebras in a functorial way. To do so, \cref{prop.coalg_as_poly_map} says we just need a natural map $\Store(S)\to\cotof{V}$. Such a map can be thought of as an \emph{integrator}, such as Euler integration, which takes a gradient vector and updates the state.

In what follows we write $\cot\colon\rvect\to\poly$ for the composite $\rvect\To{\inc}\mfd\To{\cot}\poly$.

\begin{definition}[Integrator]\label{def.integrator}
Let $\cat A$ be a symmetric monoidal category and $p\colon\cat A\to\poly$ a strong symmetric monoidal functor, the \emph{parameter interface}. A \emph{$p$-integrator} is a pair $\defineTerm{intg}=(\Fun S,\upd)$
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=60pt]
	\cat A\ar[d, equal]\ar[r, "\Fun S", ""' name=top]&
	\smsetiso\ar[d, "\Store"]\\
	\cat A\ar[r, "p"', ""{name=bot}]&
	\poly
	\arrow[from=top, to=bot, Rightarrow, shorten=3pt, "\upd"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $\Fun S\colon\cat A\to\smsetiso$, the \emph{state space}, is a strong symmetric monoidal functor and $\defineTerm{upd}\colon\Store\circ\Fun S\Rightarrow p$, the \emph{update map}, is a monoidal natural transformation.
\end{definition}

By \cref{prop.coalg_as_poly_map}, the component $\upd_a\colon\Store(\Fun S(a))\to p_a$ is equivalently a $p_a$-coalgebra structure on the state set $\Fun S(a)$, and for any $a,a'$, the coalgebra associated to $a\otimes a'$ is the Dirichlet product of those associated to $a$ and $a'$, i.e.\ the two coalgebras running in parallel.

We call $\intg$ an \emph{integrator} for what its instances turn out to be. Indeed the integrators built below---configuration, phase, and dissipative---are numerical integrators of explicit-Euler type, namely Euler on configuration space, a phase-space analogue, and a dissipative variant. They differ in both the state space $\Fun S$ (configuration versus phase space) and the update $\upd$ (e.g.\ amount of friction).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.integrator_to_org}
Let $\cat A$ be a symmetric monoidal category and $p\colon\cat A\to\poly$ a strong symmetric monoidal functor. Every $p$-integrator $\intg$ induces a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
\[\Psisem{\intg}\colon\para{p}{\poly}\to\org_\cong.\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Fun S$ for the state space of $\intg=(\Fun S,\upd)$. Since $\Fun S$ is strong symmetric monoidal, \cref{lem.poly_to_org} provides a normal lax monoidal functor
\[
D_{\Fun S}\colon\para{\Store\circ\Fun S}{\poly}\to\org_\cong.
\]
By \cref{prop.para_strong_induced,prop.para_square}, the update $\upd\colon\Store\circ\Fun S\Rightarrow p$ induces a strong symmetric monoidal functor
\[
\Para_{\cat A}^{\upd}\colon\para{p}{\poly}\to\para{\Store\circ\Fun S}{\poly}.
\]
Define $\defineTerm{Psisem}_{\intg}\coloneqq D_{\Fun S}\circ\Para_{\cat A}^{\upd}$; it is normal lax monoidal because $\Para_{\cat A}^{\upd}$ is strong.
\end{proof}

We specialize to $\cat A=\rvect$ and $p=\cot$, writing $\para{\cot}{\poly}$ for the Para construction of the action $V\cdot q\coloneqq\cotof{V}\otimes q$ induced by $\cot$. By \cref{prop.integrator_to_org}, every $\cot$-integrator $\intg$ induces a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
\[\Psisem{\intg}\colon\para{\cot}{\poly}\to\org_\cong.\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.rvect_polynomial}
There is a monoidal natural transformation $\theta\colon\Store\circ\absval{\blank}\to\cot\circ\inc$ whose component $\defineTerm{thetaV}_{\rv V}\colon\Store(\absval{V})\to\cotof{V}$ at $\rv V=(V,\sharpR_V)$ is the polynomial map given on positions by the identity and on directions at $x\in V$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.directions_sharp}
T^*_xV\To{\eqref{eqn.tangent_vec}}V^*\To{\sharpR_x}V\To{x+\blank}V,
\qquad
\xi\longmapsto x+\sharpR_x(\xi).
\end{equation}
When $\sharpR$ is nondegenerate, $\thetaV{\rv V}$ is an isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On positions, both composite functors $\Store\circ\absval{\blank}$ and $\cot\circ\inc$ send $V$ to its underlying set, so we can take the on-positions component of $\thetaV{\rv V}$ to be the identity. At each $x\in V$, \eqref{eqn.directions_sharp} is the composite of the linear map $\sharpR_x$ with translation by $x$, giving a well-defined polynomial map $\Store(\absval{V})\to\cotof{V}$. When $\sharpR_x$ is an isomorphism for all $x$, the on-directions component is a bijection, so $\thetaV{\rv V}$ is an isomorphism in $\poly$.

For naturality at a $\rvect$-isomorphism $\phi\colon\rv V\to\rv W$, the on-positions square commutes by definition. On directions at $x:V$, we must check that the two backward composites $T^*_{\phi(x)}W\to V$ agree:
\begin{align*}
\xi\;&\Mapsto{\phi^*}\;\phi^*\xi\;\Mapsto{\sharpR_x}\;\sharpR_x(\phi^*\xi)\;\Mapsto{x+\blank}\;x+\sharpR_x(\phi^*\xi), && \text{[$\thetaV{\rv V}\circ\cotof{\phi}$]}\\
\xi\;&\Mapsto{\sharpR_{\phi(x)}}\;\sharpR_{\phi(x)}(\xi)\;\Mapsto{\phi(x)+\blank}\;\phi(x)+\sharpR_{\phi(x)}(\xi)\;\Mapsto{\phi^{-1}}\;x+\phi^{-1}(\sharpR_{\phi(x)}\xi) && \text{[$\Store(\phi)\circ\thetaV{\rv W}$]}.
\end{align*}
The two are equal iff $\sharpR_x\circ\phi^*=\phi^{-1}\circ\sharpR_{\phi(x)}$, which is the sharp-equivariance condition \eqref{eqn.pairing_triangle} at $x$. Monoidality follows from the direct-sum convention $\sharpR_{V_1\oplus V_2}=\sharpR_{V_1}\oplus\sharpR_{V_2}$ (\cref{prop.rvect_monoidal}).\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Generalization to manifolds]\label{rmk.rmfdc_generalization}
The dynamics functor (\cref{thm.functor}) depends on $\rvect$ only through the monoidal natural transformation $\theta$ of \cref{prop.rvect_polynomial}. Any symmetric monoidal category of manifolds admitting such a $\theta\colon\Store\circ\absval{\blank}\to\cot\circ\inc$ would serve equally well. One natural source is \emph{reactive manifolds with connection}: triples $(M,\nabla_M,\sharpR_M)$ of a smooth manifold, a geodesically complete linear connection~\cite[pp.~49, 108]{Lee:1997a}, and a smooth bundle map $\sharpR_M\colon T^*M\to TM$. The geodesic exponential $\exp^{\nabla_M}_m(\sharpR_m(\xi))$ replaces the Euler step \eqref{eqn.flow_cot}, and on flat $V$ recovers it exactly. Examples include all complete Riemannian manifolds (via Levi-Civita) and all Lie groups (via the canonical connection). We stay with reactive vector spaces for concreteness.
\end{remark}

The \emph{configuration integrator} $\intg_\theta\coloneqq(\absval{\blank},\theta)$ has state space
$\absval{\blank}$; its update $\theta$ is the natural transformation of \cref{prop.rvect_polynomial},
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.conf_integrator}
\thetaV{\rv V}\colon\Store(\absval V)\to\cotof V .
\end{equation}
Its state set is the configuration space $\absval V$, and an incoming covector $\xi\in V^*$ updates the state by $x\mapsto x+\sharpR_x(\xi)$ \eqref{eqn.directions_sharp}.

We now turn to physics, where the stored state is a phase point $(x,\xi)\in T^*V=V\oplus V^*$ \eqref{eqn.TT_def}, with \emph{position} $x$ and \emph{momentum} $\xi$.

Recall the induced monoidal natural transformations $\omega\colon\cot\circ T^*\Rightarrow\yon$ and $\chi\colon\cot\circ T^*\Rightarrow\cot$ of \cref{lem.one_form_to_poly,lem.readout_to_poly}.

\begin{definition}\label{def.one_form_lift}
For a monoidal 1-form $\omega$ and a monoidal readout $\chi$, define an integrator $\intg_{\chi,\omega}\coloneqq(\absval{T^*\blank},\upd_{\chi,\omega})$ with state space the phase space $\absval{T^*\blank}$ and update $\upd_{\chi,\omega}$ the combined transformation $u_{\chi,\omega}$ of \cref{lem.combined_update} precomposed with $\thetaV{T^*\blank}$:
\[
\upd_{\chi,\omega}\colon\quad
\Store\circ\absval{T^*\blank}
\To{\thetaV{T^*\blank}}
\cot\circ T^*
\To{u_{\chi,\omega}}
\cot.
\]
For the projection $\chi=\pi$, where $\pi(x,\xi)=x$, we write $\intg_\omega\coloneqq\intg_{\pi,\omega}$.
\end{definition}
This is an integrator: the state space $\absval{T^*\blank}$ is a composite of strong monoidal functors, hence strong monoidal; $\thetaV{T^*\blank}$ is a monoidal natural transformation by \cref{prop.rvect_polynomial}, and $u_{\chi,\omega}$ by \cref{lem.combined_update}.


\begin{proposition}\label{prop.one_form_lift}
Writing $\omega_V(x,\xi)=\bigl(a_\omega(x,\xi),\,b_\omega(x,\xi)\bigr)\in V^*\oplus V\cong T^*_{(x,\xi)}(T^*V)$, the update $\upd_{\chi,\omega}$ of \cref{def.one_form_lift} sends $(x,\xi)\mapsto\chi_V(x,\xi)$ on positions and on directions is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.one_form_update}
\absval{T^*V}\times V^*\to\absval{T^*V},\qquad
((x,\xi),\xi')\mapsto\bigl(x+b_\omega(x,\xi),\,\xi-\xi'-a_\omega(x,\xi)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem.combined_update}, $u_{\chi,\omega}$ sends $(x,\xi)\mapsto\chi_V(x,\xi)$ on positions and, on directions, $\xi'\mapsto(\xi',0)+\omega_V(x,\xi)=\bigl(\xi'+a_\omega(x,\xi),\,b_\omega(x,\xi)\bigr)$. Precomposing with $\thetaV{T^*V}$ leaves positions unchanged and, on directions, adds to the state $(x,\xi)$ the symplectic sharp \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp} of that covector \eqref{eqn.directions_sharp},
\[
\sharpS_{T^*V}\bigl(\xi'+a_\omega(x,\xi),\,b_\omega(x,\xi)\bigr)=\bigl(b_\omega(x,\xi),\,-\xi'-a_\omega(x,\xi)\bigr),
\]
which gives \eqref{eqn.one_form_update}.
\end{proof}

Recall the kinetic 1-form $\beta_V(x,\xi)=(0,\sharpR_x(\xi))$ from \cref{ex.kinetic_one_form}. The \emph{phase integrator} is the case $\chi=\pi$, $\omega=\beta$ of \cref{def.one_form_lift},
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.phase_integrator}
\intg_\beta\coloneqq\intg_{\pi,\beta}=(\absval{T^*\blank},\upd_\beta).
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eqn.one_form_update}, with $a_\beta=0$ and $b_\beta=\sharpR_x(\xi)$, the update $\upd_\beta\colon(V\oplus V^*)\yon^{V\oplus V^*}\to V\yon^{V^*}$ sends $(x,\xi)\mapsto x$ on positions, and on directions is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.phase_update}
\absval{T^*V}\times V^*\to\absval{T^*V},\qquad
((x,\xi),\xi')\mapsto(x+\sharpR_x(\xi),\,\xi-\xi').
\end{equation}
Its position component $x\mapsto x+\sharpR_x(\xi)$ is $\exp_{\rv V}$ \eqref{eqn.flow_cot}.

\section{The dynamics functors}\label{sec.dynamics_functor}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The smooth rewiring diagrams of \cref{sec.forming_potlens} become smooth rewiring dynamics by first internalizing them as parameterized polynomial maps and then applying an integrator. We begin with a preliminary construction of a functor called $\Phi'$ which will be folded into our main functor of interest $\Phi$ in \cref{thm.functor}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.potlens_to_para_poly}
There is a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
\[
\Phi'\colon\srwd\to\para{\cot}{\poly}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{def.potlens}, $\srwd$ is the underlying operad of $\para{\rvect}{\plmfd}$. We have a strong monoidal functor $\Lens\cot\colon\plmfd\to\plpoly$ from \cref{lem.cot_lifts_lens_potential} and a normal lax monoidal $\Theta_{\potd}\colon\plpoly\to\poly$ from
\cref{lem.Theta_poly_potential}. The action square for $\Lens\cot$ and the action square for $\Theta_{\potd}$ recorded in \cref{cor.parameterized_representation} induce, by \cref{prop.para_square}, the two normal lax monoidal maps below; restricting to underlying operads gives the desired $\Phi'$:
\[
\para{\rvect}{\plmfd}\To{\para{\rvect}{\Lens\cot}}
\para{\rvect}{\plpoly}\To{\para{\rvect}{\Theta_{\potd}}}
\para{\cot}{\poly}.
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

The functor $\Phi'$ acts on objects by
\[\Phi'\lensob{M}\coloneqq\cotof{\outp{M}}\otimes\ihom{\cotof{\inpt{M}},\yon}.\] 
And $\Phi'$ sends a smooth rewiring diagram $f\coloneqq\left((V,\sharpR_V),\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f},U\right)$ as in \eqref{eqn.srw_morphism}
\[\lensob{M_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\lensob{M_K}\To[\quad]{f}\lensob{N}\]
to a $\cotof{V}$-parameterized polynomial map of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.to_be_rearranged}
\cotof{V}\otimes
\Phi'\lensob{M_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi'\lensob{M_K}
\To{\Phi'(f)}
\Phi'\lensob{N}.
\end{equation}
This is the bulk of the construction. Our main functor of interest $\Phi$, which depends on an integrator (see \cref{def.integrator}), acts the same as $\Phi'$ does on objects:
\[\Phi\lensob{M}=\Phi'\lensob{M}.\]
What \cref{thm.functor} does is to first rearrange \eqref{eqn.to_be_rearranged} to
\[
\cotof{V}\to
\left[
  \Phi\lensob{M_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi\lensob{M_K},
  \Phi\lensob{N}
\right],
\]
then use the integrator to replace the $\cotof{V}$ with something of the form $S\yon^S$ for $S=F(V)$, and finally, to unfold that to a coalgebra via \cref{prop.coalg_as_poly_map}
\[
S\to\left[
  \Phi\lensob{M_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\Phi\lensob{M_K},
  \Phi\lensob{N}
\right](S).
\]
Constructing a precise relationship between $S$ and $V$ is precisely the role of the integrator: in our two main cases of interest it is the configuration space $\absval{V}$ and the phase space $\absval{T^*V}$; see \eqref{eqn.our_phis}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm.functor}
Every $\cot$-integrator $\intg$ induces an operad functor
\[
\Phi_{\intg}\colon\srwd\to\org.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define $\Phi_{\intg}$ to be the composite of the normal lax monoidal functors below, given by \cref{lem.potlens_to_para_poly,prop.integrator_to_org}:
\[
\srwd\To{\Phi'}
\para{\cot}{\poly}\To{\Psisem{\intg}}
\org_\cong\inj\org.
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

Write $\intg_\theta$ \eqref{eqn.conf_integrator} for the configuration integrator and $\intg_\beta$ \eqref{eqn.phase_integrator} for the phase integrator. Our main two functors of interest are:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.our_phis}
\defineTerm{Phiconf}\coloneqq\Phi_{\intg_\theta}
\qqand
\defineTerm{Phiphase}\coloneqq\Phi_{\intg_\beta}.
\end{equation}

\section{Configuration dynamics \texorpdfstring{$\Phiconf$}{Phi conf}}\label{sec.configuration_dynamics}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The dynamics functor $\Phiconf\colon\srwd\to\org$ is the simpler of the two dynamics functors: a state is just an element $x\in V$ of the parameterizing manifold (no additional ``momentum'' component), and each step updates $x\mapsto x+\sharpR_x(\xi_V)$, where $\xi_V\in V^*$ is a covector assembled from $f$'s backward pass and the differential of its potential. This is the functor we apply to recover Newton's method (\cref{sec.newton_warmup}) and gradient descent (\cref{sec.dl_warmup}).

Recall from \cref{thm.functor} that $\Phiconf=\Psisem{\intg_\theta}\circ\Phi'$. The functor $\Psisem{\intg_\theta}$ replaces a $\cotof{V}$-parameter via the configuration integrator's update $\thetaV{\rv V}$ (\cref{prop.rvect_polynomial}) on state set $S\coloneqq\absval{V}$:
\[
S\yon^S\To{\thetaV{\rv V}}\cotof{V}.
\]
A state consists of a position $x\in V$, and an incoming covector $\xi'\in T^*_xV$ updates that state to $x+\sharpR_x(\xi')$ by \eqref{eqn.directions_sharp}. Note that in this case $\sharpR$ can depend on $x\in V$. 

However, the majority of what takes place under $\Phiconf$ is still compressed within the $\Phi'$ factor from \cref{lem.potlens_to_para_poly}. So we now unpack $\Phiconf$ in total, tracing each term and sign in the resulting formulas back to the definitions that produced it.

Let $\inpt M,\outp M:\mfd$ be manifolds, and write
\[\Omega(M)\coloneqq\{\omega\colon M\to T^*M\mid\pi\circ\omega=\id\}\]
for the set of (not-necessarily continuous) \emph{covector fields} on $M$.%
\footnote{We take all set-theoretic sections because the category $\poly$ remembers only the underlying sets of positions and directions, not their topology.} 
A smooth map $f\colon M\to N$ induces a \emph{pullback} $f^*\colon\Omega(N)\to\Omega(M)$ defined by $(f^*\omega)(m)\coloneqq(T_m f)^\top\omega(f(m))$.

\paragraph{The action of $\Phiconf$ on objects.}
The functor $\Phiconf$ acts on objects as follows:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.Phi_on_obs}
\Phiconf\lensob M=
\cotof{\outp M}\otimes\ihom{\cotof{\inpt M},\yon}\cong
\sum_{(\outp m,\omega):\outp M\times\Omega(\inpt M)}\yon^{\left(T^*_{\outp{m}}\outp M\right)\times\inpt M}.
\end{equation}
A position in $\Phiconf\lensob M$ is a pair $(\outp m,\omega)$ where $\outp m\in\outp{M}$ is a point and $\omega\in\Omega(\inpt M)$ is a covector field. A direction at $(\outp m,\omega)$ is a pair $(\xi,\inpt m)$ where $\xi:T^*_{\outp{m}}\outp M$ is a covector and $\inpt m\in\inpt M$ is a point.

So at every time step, a box of the form
\begin{tikzpicture}[oriented WD, bb small, baseline=(M.south)]
  \node[bb={1}{1}] (M) {};
  \node[left=-1pt] at (M_in1) {\scriptsize$\inpt{M}$};
  \node[right=-1pt] at (M_out1) {\scriptsize$\outp{M}$};
\end{tikzpicture}
outputs both a point $\outp m$ of $\outp M$ and a covector field $\omega$ on $\inpt M$. It then waits for input consisting of a covector at $\outp m$ and a point of $\inpt M$.

\paragraph{The action of $\Phiconf$ on morphisms.}
Describing the action of $\Phiconf$ on morphisms will extend to the end of this section, \cref{sec.configuration_dynamics}. Let $(V,\sharpR_V):\rvect$ and let
$f\colon V\cdot\lensob M\to\lensob N$
be a morphism in $\srwd$, i.e.\ a tuple $\left(\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f},U\right)$; specializing \eqref{eqn.srw_morphism} to $K=1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.para_potential_lens_maps}
\outp f\colon V\times\outp M\to\outp N,\qquad
\inpt f\colon V\times\outp M\times\inpt N\to\inpt M,\qquad
U\colon V\times\outp M\times\inpt N\to\rr.
\end{equation}
It is sent by $\Phiconf$ to a $[\Phiconf\lensob M,\Phiconf\lensob N]$-coalgebra whose carrier is the underlying set of $V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.state_space_conf}
S\coloneqq\absval{V};
\end{equation}
we denote its points by $x\in S=|V|$. The state update below uses the (possibly basepoint-varying) sharp $\sharpR_x$ on $V$ from \cref{def.rvect}.

We now unpack the coalgebra function itself,
\[
S\To{\Phiconf(\rv V,f)}
\left[\Phiconf\lensob M,\Phiconf\lensob N\right]\tri S.
\]
Given a state $x\in S$ and a position $a\coloneqq(\outp m,\omega_M):\outp M\times\Omega(\inpt M)$, define the output point using $\outp{f}$ from \eqref{eqn.para_potential_lens_maps}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.outpn}
\outp n\coloneqq\outp f(x,\outp m)
\end{equation}
and the output covector field $\omega_N\colon\inpt N\to T^*\inpt N$ using the rest of \eqref{eqn.para_potential_lens_maps}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.omegaprime}
\omega_N(\inpt n)\coloneqq(\inpt f(x,\outp m,\blank))^*\omega_M\big|_{\inpt n}\;+\;d(U(x,\outp m,\blank))\big|_{\inpt n}.
\end{equation}
The first term is the pullback of $\omega_M$ along the partial map $\inpt f(x,\outp m,\blank)\colon\inpt N\to\inpt M$; the second is the differential of the potential $U(x,\outp m,\blank)\colon\inpt N\to\rr$ at $\inpt n$, in the sense of \eqref{eqn.differential}.%
\footnote{The $+1$ that turns the second summand into $dU$ comes from \eqref{eqn.d_potential}; the $+$ between summands is the cotangent splitting $T^*(\rr\times\inpt M)\cong T^*\rr\oplus T^*\inpt M$ (\cref{sec.manifolds_notation}).} Thus the readout position is the pair,
\[
b\coloneqq\bigl(\outp n,\,\omega_N\bigr)\;\in\;\outp N\times\Omega(\inpt N).
\]


Now given a direction $(\xi_N,\inpt n):T^*_{\outp n}\outp N\times\inpt N=\Phiconf\lensob N[b]$, the coalgebra function $\Phiconf(\rv V,f)$ at $(x,a)$ produces a direction in $T^*_{\outp m}\outp M\times\inpt M$ as follows. Let
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.inptm}
\inpt m\coloneqq\inpt f(x,\outp m,\inpt n)
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.bigtheta}
(\xi_V,\,\xi_M,\,\xi_{\inpt N})\coloneqq (T_{(x,\outp m)}\outp f)^\top\xi_N \;+\; (T_{(x,\outp m,\inpt n)}\inpt f)^\top\omega_M(\inpt m) \;+\; dU\big|_{(x,\outp m,\inpt n)}\;
\end{equation}
where $(\xi_V,\,\xi_M,\,\xi_{\inpt N}):V^*\oplus T^*_{\outp m}\outp M\oplus T^*_{\inpt n}\inpt N$.%
\footnote{Each summand is a cotangent pullback: $\xi_N$ along $\outp f$, $\omega_M(\inpt m)$ along $\inpt f$, and $+1$ along $U$ (via $\potd$, \eqref{eqn.d_potential}); the $+$'s are the cotangent splitting of $V\times\outp M\times\inpt N$ (\cref{sec.manifolds_notation}), morally the domain of the maps in \eqref{eqn.para_potential_lens_maps}.}
Note that the first summand $(T_{(x,\outp m)}\outp f)^\top\xi_N$ naturally lands in $V^*\oplus T^*_{\outp m}\outp M$ and has been extended by $0$ in the remaining $\inpt N$-factor, so by \eqref{eqn.omegaprime} we have $\omega_N(\inpt n)=\xi_{\inpt N}$.

All of the work has now been done: the coalgebra at $(x,a)$ on $(\xi_N,\inpt n)$ returns the direction
\[
(\xi_M,\,\inpt m):T^*_{\outp m}\outp M\times\inpt M=\Phiconf\lensob M[a]
\]
and updates the state (from its original $x$) to
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.state_update_gradient}
x+\sharpR_x(\xi_V),
\end{equation}
which is the on-directions component of $\thetaV{\rv V}$ \eqref{eqn.directions_sharp} applied to the covector $\xi_V\in V^*$ of \eqref{eqn.bigtheta}. In other words, the state is updated by summing three covectors pulled back along the maps in \eqref{eqn.para_potential_lens_maps}---$\xi_N$ along $\outp f$, $\omega_M(\inpt m)$ along $\inpt f$, and $+1$ along $U$ (yielding $dU$)---projecting to the $V^*$-component, sharpening to a vector via $\sharpR_x$, and adding to the original state.

All in all, one could tersely denote the coalgebra map as follows:
\[
\Phiconf(\rv V,f)\colon
x\mapsto
(\outp m,\omega_M)\mapsto
\Big(
	(\outp n,\omega_N),
	(\xi_N,\inpt n)\mapsto\\
	\big(
		(\xi_M,\inpt m),
		x+\sharpR_x(\xi_V)
	\big)
\Big),
\]
where $\outp n$, $\omega_N$, $\xi_M$, $\inpt m$, and $\xi_V$ are as in \eqref{eqn.outpn}, \eqref{eqn.omegaprime}, \eqref{eqn.inptm}, and \eqref{eqn.bigtheta}.

We can now fulfill the promise from \cref{sec.related_cls}.

\begin{proposition}[Eulerized submersion lenses]\label{prop.euler_submersion_lenses}
For $\cat R$ as in \cref{sec.related_cls}, there is a category $\cat S$, whose objects are submersions, and a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large, row sep=large]
\mathbf{OpenErg}\ar[d, "{\mathrm{collapse}\circ T^*}"'] &
\cat{R}\ar[l, "{\mathrm{wide}}"']\ar[r, "{\mathrm{ff}}"]\ar[d] &
\srwd\ar[d, "\Phiconf"]\\
\mathbf{COrg} &
\cat{S}\ar[l, "{\mathrm{wide}}"]\ar[r] &
\org
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where the middle vertical functor sends $M$ to the submersion $T^*M\surj M$, and the lower right functor sends a submersion $p\colon E\surj B$ to the polynomial $\sum_{b:B}\yon^{E_b}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Cat{SLens}$ denote the symmetric monoidal category whose objects are submersions $p\colon E\surj B$ and whose morphisms $p\to p'$, for $p'\colon E'\surj B'$, are smooth maps
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
E\ar[d, ->>, "p"']&
B\times_{B'}E'\ar[l, "\alpha"']\ar[r]\ar[d, ->>]&
E'\ar[d, ->>, "p'"]\\
B\ar[r, equal]&
B\ar[r, "r"']&
B'\ar[ul, phantom, very near end, "\lrcorner"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
There is a strong symmetric monoidal functor $P\colon\Cat{SLens}\to\poly$ given by
\[
P(E\surj B)\coloneqq\sum_{b:B}\yon^{E_b}
\]
sends a submersion lens $(r,\alpha)\colon p\to p'$ to the polynomial map with forward component $r$ and backward component $\alpha$. It is strong monoidal because the tensor product of submersions is computed by products of total and base spaces.

The strong monoidal functor
\[
\vect_\cong\to\Cat{SLens},
\qquad
V\longmapsto (TV\surj V)
\]
induces an action of $\vect_\cong$ on $\Cat{SLens}$; let $\cat{S}\coloneqq\para{\vect_\cong}{\Cat{SLens}}$. The objects of $\cat S$ are submersions, and a morphism $p\to p'$ in $\cat S$ is a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ and smooth maps
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
V\times E\ar[d, ->>, "V\times p"']&
(V\times B)\times_{B'}E'\ar[l, "{(u,\alpha)}"']\ar[r]\ar[d, ->>]&
E'\ar[d, ->>, "p'"]\\
V\times B\ar[r, equal]&
V\times B\ar[r, "r"']&
B'\ar[ul, phantom, very near end, "\lrcorner"]
\end{tikzcd}
\]

Let $\absval{\blank}\colon\vect_\cong\to\smsetiso$ be the underlying-set functor. For $V:\vect_\cong$ and $p\colon E\surj B$, define a polynomial map
\[
\theta_{V,p}\colon \Store(\absval{V})\otimes P(p)\to P\bigl((TV\surj V)\otimes p\bigr).
\]
Both sides have positions $(x,b)\in V\times B$. Using the canonical identification $TV\cong V\times V$, the map $\theta_{V,p}\colon T_xV\times E_b\to V\times E_b$ on directions at $(x,b)$ is given by
\[
(\dot x,e)\mapsto (x+\dot x,e).
\]
These maps are natural in vector-space isomorphisms and submersion lenses, and monoidal by componentwise addition in direct sums. Thus \cref{prop.para_square} induces a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
\[
\cat{S}=\para{\vect_\cong}{\Cat{SLens}}\to\para{\smsetiso}{\poly}.
\]
Composing with the identification $\para{\smsetiso}{\poly}\cong\org_\cong$ of \cref{prop.org_as_para} and the inclusion $\org_\cong\inj\org$ gives a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor $\cat S\to\org$. Unwinding this composite sends the above morphism $(V, r, u,\alpha)$ to the coalgebra with state set $V$ whose action is
\[
x\longmapsto b\longmapsto
\left(
r(x,b),\;
a'\longmapsto\bigl(\alpha(x,b,a'),\,x+u(x,b,a')\bigr)
\right).
\]

The functor $\cat S\to\mathbf{COrg}$ is more straightforward: an $\cat S$-morphism is a vector space $V$ together with a lens $(TV\surj V)\otimes p\to p'$, hence a $\mathbf{COrg}$ morphism with state manifold $V$. It is identity-on-objects, hence wide.

For $\cat{R}$ we take the category $\cat R$ from \cref{sec.related_cls}. We define $\cat R\to\cat S$ on objects by $M\mapsto T^*M\surj M$. On morphisms, send $(\rv V,w,E)\colon M\to N$ to the $\cat S$-morphism with parameter space $V$, readout $r\coloneqq w$, and for the rest of the data write
\[
dE|_{(x,m)}+(T_{(x,m)}w)^*(\xi_N)=(\xi_V,\xi_M)\in V^*\oplus T_m^*M
\]
and set $\alpha(x,m,\xi_N)\coloneqq\xi_M$ and $u(x,m,\xi_N)\coloneqq\sharpR_x(\xi_V)$. Functoriality follows from the chain rule.

The lower-right square commutes as follows. Under the fully faithful functor $\cat R\to\srwd$, the morphism $(\rv V,w,E)$ is sent to the smooth rewiring diagram with output map $w$, unique input map, and potential $E$. Since the input interface is terminal, the only nonzero terms in \eqref{eqn.bigtheta} are
\[
dE|_{(x,m)}+(T_{(x,m)}w)^*(\xi_N)=(\xi_V,\xi_M).
\]
Thus \eqref{eqn.state_update_gradient} sends the state $x$ to $x+\sharpR_x(\xi_V)$ and returns the internal covector $\xi_M$, exactly the coalgebra obtained above from the image of $(\rv V,w,E)$ in $\cat S$. The remaining compatibility with $\mathbf{OpenErg}$ is the CLS collapse construction restricted to $\cat R$.\qedhere
\end{proof}

Applications of $\Phiconf$ appear in \cref{sec.newton_warmup} (Newton's method) and \cref{sec.dl_warmup} (gradient descent and backpropagation).

\section{Phase-space dynamics \texorpdfstring{$\Phiphase$}{Phi phase}}\label{sec.phase_dynamics}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

We remind the reader of \cref{warn.prime}: primes (e.g.\ $\xi', x'$) never denote derivatives in this paper.

Recall from \cref{thm.functor} that $\Phiphase=\Psisem{\intg_\beta}\circ\Phi'$. The functor $\Psisem{\intg_\beta}$ replaces a $\cotof{V}$-parameter via the phase integrator \eqref{eqn.phase_update} on state set $S\coloneqq\absval{T^*V}$:
\[
S\yon^S\To{\upd_\beta}\cotof{V}.
\]
A state consists of a position and a momentum $(x,\xi)\in T^*V$, and an incoming covector $\xi'\in T^*_xV$ updates that state to $(x+\sharpR_x(\xi),\,\xi-\xi')$
 by \eqref{eqn.phase_update}. The readout and backward pass of the coalgebra $\Phiphase(\rv V,f)$ are identical to the configuration case---their formulas \eqref{eqn.outpn}, \eqref{eqn.omegaprime}, \eqref{eqn.inptm}, \eqref{eqn.bigtheta} depend only on the parameter position $x$, and the phase integrator maps $(x,\xi)\mapsto x$ on positions by \eqref{eqn.phase_update}. What changes is the state space and the state update; we unpack it all below.

\paragraph{The action of $\Phiphase$ on objects.}
The functor $\Phiphase$ acts on objects exactly as $\Phiconf$ in \eqref{eqn.Phi_on_obs}: a position in $\Phiphase\lensob M$ is a pair $(\outp m,\omega)$ and a direction at $(\outp m,\omega)$ is a pair $(\xi,\inpt m)$, with the same types as in the configuration case.

\paragraph{The action of $\Phiphase$ on morphisms.}
Describing the action of $\Phiphase$ on morphisms will extend to the end of this section, \cref{sec.phase_dynamics}. Let $(V,\sharpR_V):\rvect$ and let
$f\colon V\cdot\lensob M\to\lensob N$
be a morphism in $\srwd$, i.e.\ a tuple $\left(\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f},U\right)$ as in \eqref{eqn.para_potential_lens_maps}.
It is sent by $\Phiphase$ to a $[\Phiphase\lensob M,\Phiphase\lensob N]$-coalgebra whose carrier is the underlying set of $T^*V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.state_space}
S\coloneqq\absval{T^*V}\cong V\oplus V^*;
\end{equation}
we write $s=(x,\xi)\in S$ for position $x\in V$ and momentum $\xi\in V^*$. Although the coalgebra remembers only the set $S$, the state update below uses the canonical symplectic pairing on $T^*V$. Its sharp map is
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.sharp_S}
\sharpS_S\colon S^*\to S,\qquad (\xi',x')\mapsto(x',-\xi'),
\end{equation}
specializing \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp}.

We now unpack the coalgebra function itself,
\[
S\To{\Phiphase(\rv V,f)}
\left[\Phiphase\lensob M,\Phiphase\lensob N\right]\tri S.
\]
Given a state $s\coloneqq(x,\xi)\in S$ and a position $a\coloneqq(\outp m,\omega_M):\outp M\times\Omega(\inpt M)$, the output point $\outp n$, output covector field $\omega_N$, and readout position $b=(\outp n,\omega_N)\in\outp N\times\Omega(\inpt N)$ are defined by \eqref{eqn.outpn} and \eqref{eqn.omegaprime}. Given a direction $(\xi_N,\inpt n):T^*_{\outp n}\outp N\times\inpt N=\Phiphase\lensob N[b]$, the coalgebra function $\Phiphase(\rv V,f)$ at $(s,a)$ defines $\inpt m$ via \eqref{eqn.inptm} and the triple $(\xi_V,\,\xi_M,\,\xi_{\inpt N})$ via \eqref{eqn.bigtheta}, returning the direction $(\xi_M,\inpt m):T^*_{\outp m}\outp M\times\inpt M=\Phiphase\lensob M[a]$, exactly as in \cref{sec.configuration_dynamics}---the formulas depend on $x$ alone, and the phase integrator maps $(x,\xi)\mapsto x$ on positions by \eqref{eqn.phase_update}. The only difference is in the state update: starting from $s=(x,\xi)$, the state is updated to
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.state_update}
s+\sharpS_S(\xi_V,\sharpR_x(\xi))=\bigl(x+\sharpR_x(\xi),\,\xi-\xi_V\bigr).
\end{equation}
We will now break down \cref{eqn.state_update}, which is the phase integrator \eqref{eqn.phase_update} with the substitution $\xi'=\xi_V$.

Its left-hand side records where the three pieces of this update come from. The sum $s+\sharpS_S(\blank)$ is the exponential of the reactive vector space $T^*V$ on the underlying set $S$, \eqref{eqn.flow_cot}; the use of $\sharpS_S$ comes from the canonical symplectic pairing on $T^*V$, \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp}, which is constant in $s$ regardless of variation in $\sharpR_V$ (\cref{rmk.constant_canonical_sharp}); and the pair $(\xi_V,\sharpR_x(\xi))\in T^*_sS$ comes from the kinetic 1-form \eqref{eqn.kinetic_one_form} at $(x,\xi)$.

The right-hand side of \cref{eqn.state_update} is the computation: \eqref{eqn.canonical_sharp} gives $\sharpS_S(\xi_V,\sharpR_x(\xi))=(\sharpR_x(\xi),-\xi_V)$, then componentwise addition with $s=(x,\xi)$ produces $(x+\sharpR_x(\xi),\,\xi-\xi_V)$.

All in all, one could tersely denote the coalgebra map as follows:
\begin{multline*}
\Phiphase(\rv V,f)\colon
(x,\xi)\mapsto
(\outp m,\omega_M)\mapsto
\Big(
	(\outp n,\omega_N),
	(\xi_N,\inpt n)\mapsto\\
	\big(
		(\xi_M,\inpt m),
		(x+\sharpR_x(\xi),\,\xi-\xi_V)
	\big)
\Big),
\end{multline*}
where $\outp n$ is defined in \eqref{eqn.outpn}, $\omega_N$ in \eqref{eqn.omegaprime}, $\inpt m$ in \eqref{eqn.inptm}, and $\xi_M$ and $\xi_V$ in \eqref{eqn.bigtheta}.

\begin{remark}[Hamilton's equations in the constant symmetric case]\label{rmk.constant_inverse_mass_hamiltonian}
In this remark we aim to justify the Hamiltonian terminology that we allude to elsewhere in the paper. The assumptions that make the dynamics Hamiltonian are written in bold.

Suppose \textbf{the domain and codomain interfaces are closed}, i.e.\ $\inpt M=\outp M=\inpt N=\outp N=\rr^0$, so that in particular $\xi_N=0$ and $\omega_M=0$ and $U\colon V\to\rr$ depends only on $x$. Then \eqref{eqn.bigtheta} gives $\xi_V=dU|_x$. If moreover \textbf{the sharp is constant}, $\sharpR_x=\sharpR_0$, then \eqref{eqn.state_update} becomes
\[
s+\sharpS_S(dU|_x,\sharpR_0(\xi))
=
(x+\sharpR_0(\xi),\,\xi-dU|_x),
\]
which is the explicit Euler step for the vector field $(\dot x,\dot\xi)$ where
\[
\dot x\coloneqq \sharpR_0(\xi),
\qquad
\dot\xi\coloneqq-dU|_x.
\]
In the one-dimensional mass=$m$ case this reads $\dot x=\xi/m$ and $\dot\xi=-dU|_x$, the familiar (velocity, force) form of Newton's second law, with $\xi=m\dot x$ the usual momentum.

Finally, when \textbf{the sharp is symmetric}, in the sense that $\xi'(\sharpR_0(\xi))=\xi(\sharpR_0(\xi'))$ for all $\xi,\xi'\in V^*$, this vector field is \emph{Hamiltonian} meaning that it is the symplectic sharp applied to the derivative of some function, namely
\[
H(x,\xi)\coloneqq \tfrac12\xi(\sharpR_0(\xi))+U(x).
\]
This $H$ is the energy of a \emph{simple mechanical system}~\cite[\S 3.7]{abraham1978foundations}: kinetic energy $\tfrac12\xi(\sharpR_0(\xi))$ plus potential energy $U$.
Indeed, the symmetry condition and the linearity of $\sharpR$ give $dH|_{(x,\xi)}=(dU|_x,\sharpR_0(\xi))\in V^*\oplus V$, and applying the canonical symplectic sharp \eqref{eqn.sharp_S} recovers the vector field:
\[
\sharpS_S(dH|_{(x,\xi)})=(\sharpR_0(\xi),-dU|_x)=(\dot{x},\dot{\xi}).
\]
In particular the flow conserves $H$, by \cref{rmk.symplectic_perpendicular}.\qedhere
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Euler step and energy non-conservation]\label{rmk.euler_energy}
\Cref{eqn.state_update} can be read two ways. As the defining equation of a discrete dynamical system, it is exact: that's the perspective of \cref{sec.wave_equation} below, where the recurrence formula \eqref{eqn.recurrence} \emph{is} the discrete wave equation we want. But as a numerical time-stepper for the corresponding continuous phase-space dynamics---Hamiltonian in the case presented in \cref{rmk.constant_inverse_mass_hamiltonian}---it is not adequate over many timesteps: explicit Euler is not a \emph{symplectic integrator}, so the simulated trajectory accumulates energy drift and diverges from the true flow. For example, on the harmonic oscillator of \cref{sec.wave_equation}, the simulated energy grows without bound for any positive step size \cite[Ch.~I]{hairer2006geometric}.

The configuration analog \eqref{eqn.state_update_gradient} (\cref{sec.configuration_dynamics}) is also explicit Euler, but this time on a gradient flow rather than a Hamiltonian flow. So when we use Euclidean parameter spaces, the constant $\sharp$-case \eqref{eqn.learning_sharp} yields vanilla gradient descent with learning rate $\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}$, which is a standard, well-behaved update for sufficiently small $\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}$. 

The following remark sketches one possible target for such refinements.\qedhere
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Multi-stage integrators and $\org^{(K)}$]\label{rmk.org_N}
The functor $\Phi$ produces one-step coalgebras: at each macro-tick, the system emits, receives, and updates exactly once. Many useful numerical integrators are instead \emph{multi-stage}: an explicit $K$-stage Runge--Kutta method evaluates the vector field $K$-many times per step, and velocity Verlet (leapfrog) for Hamiltonian flows \cite[Ch.~VI]{hairer2006geometric} queries the force twice, at the initial and at the post-drift positions. We sketch a target bicategory that conjecturally accommodates such schemes.

For any $K:\nn$, let $\org^{(K)}$ be the bicategory with the same objects as $\org$ and hom-categories
\[
\org^{(K)}(p,q)\coloneqq\ihom{p,q}^{\tri K}\coalg,
\]
where $x^{\tri K}\coloneqq x\tri\cdots\tri x$ is the $K$-fold $\tri$-power.%
\footnote{Indeed, for any symmetric monoidal closed category $\cat{P}$, symmetric monoidal category $\cat{V}$, and lax symmetric monoidal functor $C\colon\cat{P}\to\cat{V}$, there is a symmetric monoidal $\cat{V}$-category $\org^C$ with objects given by $\ob\cat{P}$ and $\org^C(p,p')\coloneqq C([p,p'])$. Then $\org$ arises from this construction where $\cat{P}\coloneqq\poly$, $\cat{V}\coloneqq\smcat$, and $C\coloneqq\blank\coalg$. I believe I learned this abstraction from Toby St Clere Smithe. Under this same setup $\org^{(K)}$ is constructed with $C$ instead given by the composite $\poly\To{\blank^{\tri K}}\poly\To{\blank\coalg}\smcat$.
}
For example, a morphism in $\org^{(2)}$ is a coalgebra of the form $S\to q\tri q\tri S$. Given a state, it outputs a position $j_1:q(1)$ and, for every input direction $e_1:q[j_1]$, outputs another position $j_2:q(1)$ and, for every input direction $e_2:q[j_2]$, updates the state. 

We conjecture that by using a modified definition of integrator (\cref{def.integrator}) with the state space $\Fun S$ unchanged but whose update is of the form $\upd\colon\Store\circ\Fun S\Rightarrow p^{\tri K}$, one again obtains an operad functor $\srwd\to\org^{(K)}$. We do not pursue this here. See also \cite{ngotiaoco2017compositionality}.\qedhere
\end{remark}


Applications of $\Phiphase$ to harmonic-oscillator particles appear in \cref{sec.wave_equation} (recovering the discrete wave equation on a chain) and \cref{sec.graph_laplacian} (the graph Laplacian on an arbitrary finite directed graph).

\section{Newton's method}\label{sec.newton_warmup}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

A \emph{scalar} in a symmetric monoidal category is an endomorphism of the monoidal unit $I$; monoidal functors like $\Phi$ preserve scalars. In the symmetric monoidal category $\potlens$ representing $\srwd$ (\cref{def.potlens}), the monoidal unit is $I=\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}$, so for a scalar both interfaces are trivial: both $\outp f$ and $\inpt f$ in \eqref{eqn.para_potential_lens_maps} are forced to be the unique map $\bang$, and the only data is a reactive parameter space $(V,\sharpR_V)$ and a potential $U\colon V\to\rr$. Newton's method for finding critical points of a potential is such a scalar. Under $\Phiconf$, it determines a scalar in $\org$, i.e.\ a discrete dynamical system $V\to[\yon,\yon]\tri V=V$. 

Let $U\colon V\to\rr$ be a smooth map on a finite-dimensional real vector space $V$. Its derivative $dU\colon V\to V^*$ is a smooth map sending $x\in V$ to the gradient covector $dU|_x\in V^*$, and the derivative of $dU$ at $x$ is itself a linear map
\begin{equation*}
T_x(dU)\colon V\to V^*,
\end{equation*}
called the \emph{Hessian} of $U$ at $x$. In a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $V$, the linear map $T_x(dU)$ is given by the matrix of second partial derivatives
\[
\bigl(T_x(dU)\bigr)_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j U(x),
\]
which is symmetric by equality of mixed partials. Newton's method requires this matrix to have full rank at every $x\in V$, so that $T_x(dU)$ is an isomorphism. This gives a nondegenerate reactive vector space (\cref{def.rvect}): put
\[
\sharpR_x\coloneqq-\bigl(T_x(dU)\bigr)^{-1}\colon V^*\To{\cong}V,
\]
so $(V,\sharpR_V)\in\rvect$.

So we now have a scalar in $\srwd$
\[
\bigl((V,\sharpR_V),\ \outp f=\bang,\ \inpt f=\bang,\ U\bigr)\colon I\to I
\]
with potential $U$.

Applying $\Phiconf$ to $(\rv V,\bang,\bang,U)$ gives a coalgebra carried by state set $\absval V$; in \eqref{eqn.bigtheta} the $\xi_N$- and $\omega_M$-summands vanish, leaving only $dU|_x$, and the state update \eqref{eqn.state_update_gradient} reads
\[
x\longmapsto x+\sharpR_x(dU|_x)=x-\bigl(T_x(dU)\bigr)^{-1}(dU|_x),
\]
one step of \emph{Newton's method} for finding a critical point of $U$. The reactive structure $\sharpR_x=-\bigl(T_x(dU)\bigr)^{-1}$ locally approximates $U$ by a quadratic and jumps to that quadratic's critical point; for an exactly quadratic $U$, one step suffices to reach that critical point, which could be a maximum, minimum, or saddle. By contrast, gradient descent uses $\sharpR=-\epsilon\,\id$ and always moves downhill, so it can only find minima. Whether or not Newton's method converges to a critical point is a separate question.

\section{Gradient descent and backpropagation}\label{sec.dl_warmup}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

By \emph{deep learning} we mean the training of an artificial neural network by gradient descent with backpropagation~\cite{rumelhart1986learning}. Applying \cref{prop.lens_inclusion} with $\cat C=\mfd$ and $\Fun T=\rr\times\blank$ gives a strong symmetric monoidal functor $\inc\colon\mfd\to\plmfd$, $M\mapsto\binom{\rr^0}{M}$. Whiskering with $\para\rvect{\blank}$ and composing with $\Phiconf$ yields a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
\[\para{\rvect}{\mfd}\To{\para{\rvect}{\inc}}\srwd\To{\Phiconf}\org,\]
whose composite we denote by
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.lrn}
\lrn\colon\para{\rvect}{\mfd}\too\org
\end{equation} 
One could say that training a neural network is a case of our configuration dynamics construction, in the case that input manifolds and potentials are trivial: $\inpt M=\rr^0$ and $U=0$.

{\emergencystretch=2em
We now unfold this construction in coordinates, taking the source and target manifolds---the input and output layers of the neural network---to be $\rr^m$ and $\rr^n$.
\par}

\paragraph{A parameterized map as a smooth rewiring diagram.}
Suppose we are given a smooth parameterized map
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.model_map}
F\colon V\times\rr^m\to\rr^n,
\end{equation}
  thought of as a feedforward neural network with parameter space $(V,\sharpR_V):\rvect$---i.e., $V$ comes equipped with a smooth map $\sharpR_V\colon V\to\vect(V^*,V)$ (\cref{def.rvect}), whose value $\sharpR_x\colon V^*\to V$ at $x\in V$ will act as a position-dependent learning-rate. This $F$ is regarded by $\para{\rvect}{\inc}$ as a $\srwd$-morphism
\[
f\colon\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^m}\to\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^n}
\]
with parameter $V$, by taking $\outp f=F\colon V\times \rr^m\to\rr^n$, taking $\inpt f\colon V\times\rr^m\times\rr^0\to\rr^0$ to be the unique map, and taking the potential to be $0$. 

\paragraph{The interfaces.}
On objects, $\lrn(\rr^m)$ is given by
\[
\lrn(\rr^m)=\Phiconf\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^m}
=\cotof{\rr^m}\otimes\ihom{\cotof{\rr^0},\yon}
\cong\cotof{\rr^m}=\sum_{z:\rr^m}\yon^{T^*_z\rr^m},
\]
because $\cotof{\rr^0}\cong\yon$ and $\ihom{\yon,\yon}\cong\yon$. Hence $\Phiconf(f)$ is a $[\cotof{\rr^m},\cotof{\rr^n}]$-coalgebra.

\paragraph{The coalgebra.}
Because we are using the configuration integrator, the state set is $\absval{V}$ by \eqref{eqn.state_space_conf}; an element is a vector of the ``weights and biases'' of the artificial neural network. We unpack the coalgebra step at fixed $x\in V$, given an input position $z\in\rr^m$ from upstream and a direction $\xi\in T^*_{F(x,z)}\rr^n$ from downstream; where these come from during the training process is taken up below.

At state $x\in V$ and input data $z\in\rr^m$, the readout point is
\begin{equation*}
\outp n\coloneqq\outp f(x,z)=F(x,z),
\end{equation*}
which is \eqref{eqn.outpn} in this special case.

Now we feed the external interface a direction $\xi\in T^*_{F(x,z)}\rr^n$. Since $\inpt f$ is the unique map to $\rr^0$ and $U=0$, the second and third summands in \eqref{eqn.bigtheta} vanish. Thus the only nonzero contribution is the cotangent pullback along $F$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.dl_pullback}
(\xi_V,\xi_M)=(T_{(x,z)}F)^\top\xi\in V^*\oplus T^*_z\rr^m.
\end{equation}
Here $\xi_V$ is the parameter covector and $\xi_M$ is the covector returned to the $\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^m}$ interface. In partial-map notation,
\[
\xi_V=\bigl(T_x(F(\blank,z))\bigr)^\top\xi
\qqand
\xi_M=\bigl(T_z(F(x,\blank))\bigr)^\top\xi.
\]
By \eqref{eqn.state_update_gradient}, the parameter update is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.dl_gradient_update}
x\longmapsto x+\sharpR_x(\xi_V).
\end{equation}
Altogether, suppressing the unique $\rr^0$-components, the coalgebra is
\[
\Phiconf(F)(x)(z)
=\Bigl(F(x,z),\;\xi\mapsto\bigl(\xi_M,\,x+\sharpR_x(\xi_V)\bigr)\Bigr),
\]
where $(\xi_V,\xi_M)$ is defined by \eqref{eqn.dl_pullback}.

\paragraph{Losses and backpropagation.}
The zero-potential condition does not mean there is no learning signal; it means the learning signal is supplied as an external cotangent direction. If $\xi=dU|_{F(x,z)}$ for a loss $U\colon\rr^n\to\rr$, then by the chain rule
\[
d(U\circ F)|_{(x,z)}=(T_{(x,z)}F)^\top dU|_{F(x,z)}=(\xi_V,\xi_M).
\]
Thus $\xi_V=d_x(U(F(\blank,z)))$ is the parameter-gradient covector, while $\xi_M=d_z(U(F(x,\blank)))$ is the covector propagated backward to the previous interface, if any. For a composite network, this repeated production of the covector $\xi_M$ is the usual backpropagated gradient signal. On Euclidean parameter spaces, for the constant sharp \eqref{eqn.learning_sharp}, \eqref{eqn.dl_gradient_update} is vanilla gradient descent with learning rate $\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}$. This presupposes a single fixed loss $U$ applied at every step; in actual training, $U$ varies per datum (typically through a label), and the source of $z$ varies too. We package both as a single coalgebra next.

\paragraph{Training data as an environment.}
The preceding discussion focuses on $\xi=dU|_{F(x,z)}$ for a single fixed loss function $U\colon\rr^n\to\rr$ and a single input $z\in\rr^m$. Genuine training involves a stream of such pairs, which we package as an $\org$-map $f_{\mathrm{gen}}\colon\yon\to\Phiconf\binom{\rr^n}{\rr^m}$, i.e.\ a coalgebra on
\[
\cotof{\rr^m}\otimes\ihom{\cotof{\rr^n},\yon}
\]
whose positions are pairs $(z,\omega)\in\rr^m\times\Omega(\rr^n)$: an input point together with a covector field on the output (typically $\omega=dU$ for some per-datum loss $U\colon\rr^n\to\rr$). Such a coalgebra is, in general, not just a stream of $(z,\omega)$'s, but can branch after each step based on a pair $(\xi_M,y)\in T^*_z\rr^m\times\rr^n$. These are exactly what the network gives back when we wire it in, below: $y=F(x,z)$ is the predicted output and $\xi_M$ is the backpropagated input-gradient. So the training-data block emits $(z,\omega)$ and may transition based on what the network replies, namely the prediction $y$ and the backpropagated input-gradient $\xi_M$. When the transition of the data generator depends on these replies, the data presented to the model-in-training is adapted to that model's behavior; when it ignores them, the data presentation is simply an externally specified supervised stream.

We can combine $f_{\mathrm{gen}}$ with the network $\lrn(F)\colon\cotof{\rr^m}\to\cotof{\rr^n}$ from \eqref{eqn.lrn} and \eqref{eqn.model_map} as follows. By closedness of $\org$ (\cref{rmk.org_closed}), $\lrn(F)$ corresponds equivalently to a map
\[
\overline{\lrn(F)}\colon\yon\to[\cotof{\rr^m},\cotof{\rr^n}]
\]
in $\org$. Tensoring with $f_{\mathrm{gen}}$ and applying the two evaluation pairings of $\poly$ closes the system:
\[
\yon\To{f_{\mathrm{gen}}\otimes\overline{\lrn(F)}}\bigl(\cotof{\rr^m}\otimes\ihom{\cotof{\rr^n},\yon}\bigr)\otimes[\cotof{\rr^m},\cotof{\rr^n}]\To{\ev_1}\cotof{\rr^n}\otimes\ihom{\cotof{\rr^n},\yon}\To{\ev_2}\yon.
\]
The result is a $\yon$-coalgebra on $\absval{V}\times\absval{V_{\mathrm{gen}}}$, where $V_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is the carrier of $f_{\mathrm{gen}}$, i.e.\ a discrete dynamical system on $V\times V_{\mathrm{gen}}$ that trains the neural network as it moves through training data. In each step, starting from the current network parameter $x\in V$ and dataset state $s\in V_{\mathrm{gen}}$: $f_{\mathrm{gen}}$ at $s$ produces $(z,\omega)$; $\ev_1$ pairs $\cotof{\rr^m}$ (carrying $z$) with $[\cotof{\rr^m},\cotof{\rr^n}]$ to run the network, producing $y=F(x,z)$; $\ev_2$ pairs $[\cotof{\rr^n},\yon]$ (carrying $\omega$) with the resulting $\cotof{\rr^n}$, sampling $\xi=\omega(y)$ and triggering the backward pass; update $x\mapsto x+\sharpR_x(\xi_V)$, and advance $f_{\mathrm{gen}}$'s state via its coalgebra map applied to $(\xi_M,\,y)$.

\paragraph{Momentum via damping.}
The same machinery accommodates damping. Since $\beta$ and $\zeta$ (\cref{ex.kinetic_one_form,ex.dissipation_one_form}) are monoidal 1-forms, so is $\beta+c\,\zeta$ for a damping coefficient $c\in[0,1]$ (\cref{prop.one_forms_monoid}); the resulting \emph{dissipative phase integrator} $\intg_{\pi,\beta+c\,\zeta}$ has update map
\[
((x,\xi),\xi')\mapsto(x+\sharpR_x(\xi),\,(1-c)\xi-\xi'),
\]
recovering the phase integrator $\intg_\beta$ at $c=0$. Fed the gradient signal $\xi'=dU|_x$, this is a damped, momentum-style update, of the kind underlying classical momentum methods such as the heavy-ball method~\cite{polyak1964momentum}; reading the gradient at an extrapolated point via a lookahead readout $\exp^\alpha$ (\cref{lem.readout_examples}) is similarly reminiscent of Nesterov's accelerated gradient~\cite{nesterov1983method}. We do not pin down the precise correspondences.

\section{The wave equation}\label{sec.wave_equation}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

We illustrate $\Phiphase\colon\srwd\to\org$ by deriving the wave equation. We describe a single particle (with harmonic potential) as a $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram $\Part\colon I\to\boxob$ and arrange particles into a chain as a $K$-ary smooth (static) wiring diagram $\fun{wire}_K\colon\boxob\tpow{K}\to\boxob$, and then audit the computation via functoriality. In \cref{sec.spring_first_pass} we compose the particles and the wiring in $\srwd$, apply $\Phiphase$, and recover the discrete wave equation. In \cref{sec.spring_second_pass} we apply $\Phiphase$ to the particle and wiring separately, compose the resulting coalgebras in $\org$, and verify term-by-term agreement as a check on signs, indices, and routing.

That is, we begin with a single particle whose state is a transverse displacement and momentum, which we put in a ``box'' that delivers the displacement to its neighbors and updates its state based on some fixed parameters $\kappa,m>0$ and the displacements of its neighboring particles. We wire these boxes together in series
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.wave_wd}
\begin{tikzpicture}[oriented WD, bb port length=4pt, bb port sep=1.5, bb min width=.35cm, bbx=.4cm, bby=.8ex, baseline=(lbox1), particle/.style={path picture={\fill (path picture bounding box.center) circle (2.4pt);}}]
  \def\N{7}
  \node[bb={1}{1}, particle] (lbox1) {};
  \foreach \i [evaluate=\i as \prev using int(\i-1)] in {2,...,\N} {
    \node[bb={1}{1}, particle, right=.7 of lbox\prev] (lbox\i) {};
    \draw (lbox\prev_out1) -- (lbox\i_in1);
  }
  \node[right=.4 of lbox\N] (dots) {$\cdots$};
  \draw (lbox\N_out1) -- (dots.west);
  \node[bb={1}{1}, particle, right=.4 of dots] (rbox1) {};
  \draw (dots.east) -- (rbox1_in1);
  \foreach \i [evaluate=\i as \prev using int(\i-1)] in {2,...,\N} {
    \node[bb={1}{1}, particle, right=.7 of rbox\prev] (rbox\i) {};
    \draw (rbox\prev_out1) -- (rbox\i_in1);
  }
  \node[bb={0}{0}, fit={(lbox1) (rbox\N)}] (Y) {};
  \node[coordinate] at (Y.west|-lbox1_in1) (Y_in1) {};
  \node[coordinate] at (Y.east|-rbox\N_out1) (Y_out1) {};
  \draw (Y_in1) -- (lbox1_in1);
  \draw (rbox\N_out1) -- (Y_out1);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{equation}
and calculate that the resulting state dynamics evolves according to the discrete wave equation:%
\footnote{Taking the continuum limit gives the usual wave equation.}
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.discrete_wave}
m\ddot x_i=\kappa(x_{i-1}-2x_i+x_{i+1}),
\end{equation}
where $\ddot x_i(t)=x_i(t+2)-2x_i(t+1)+x_i(t).$

We will work in the full suboperad of $\srwd$ (in the sense of \cref{sec.wd_operads}) spanned by a single object $\boxob\coloneqq\binom{\rr}{\rr}$, corresponding to a box whose input port and output port are both $\rr$. That is, we'll consider morphisms of the form
\[
\varphi\in\srwd(\boxob\tpow{n},\boxob)
\]
for varying $n$. Note that $\boxob\tpow{0}=\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}$ is the monoidal unit.

We derive the wave equation, then audit the derivation via functoriality. First, we describe the particle and the wiring in $\srwd$, compose them there, and apply $\Phiphase$ to the resulting $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram to read off the dynamics. Then, as a bookkeeping check, we apply $\Phiphase$ to the particle and to the wiring separately, compose in $\org$, and verify that the same coalgebra is obtained.

\subsection{First pass: composing in \texorpdfstring{$\srwd$}{SRWD}}\label{sec.spring_first_pass}

First we give a $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram $\Part\colon\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}\to\binom{\rr}{\rr}$, for the dynamical system that goes in each internal box, representing the particle of mass $m>0$. Then we give a $K$-ary smooth (static) wiring diagram for each $K>0$ that describes the (static) wiring diagram shown in \eqref{eqn.wave_wd}. Then we calculate what happens when we compose.

\paragraph{The particle.}

To define a particle $\Part\colon\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}\to\binom{\rr}{\rr}$ as a $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram in $\srwd$ \eqref{eqn.srw_morphism}, we need a reactive vector space $(V,\sharpR_V)$, a smooth output map $\outp f\colon V\to\rr$, and a smooth potential $U\colon V\times\rr\to\rr$. We take the parameter space to be $\rr\in\rvect$ with constant sharp $\sharpR_x(p)\coloneqq p/m$ (independent of $x\in\rr$); we read the value $x\in\rr$ as the particle's transverse displacement from equilibrium. We take the output map to be the identity, $\outp f(x)\coloneqq x$, and the potential to be
\[U(x,y)\coloneqq\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(x-y)^2,\]
the \emph{harmonic} potential, assigning energy to the relative displacement of the particle and its neighbor.

\paragraph{The wiring diagram.}
For the wiring diagram, we start in $\Lens{\finset\op}$; see \cref{ex.lens_finsetop}. The $K$-ary series composition shown in \eqref{eqn.wave_wd} corresponds to a lens $\varphi_K\colon\binom{K}{K}=\binom{1}{1}+\cdots+\binom{1}{1}\to\binom{1}{1}$ given by
\[
K\From{\outp f}1
\qqand
K+1\From{\inpt f} K,
\]
where we identify $K+1$ with the disjoint union of the $K$ internal-box outputs and the external-box input, the external-box input last. Then $\outp f$ picks out the last wire, $\outp{f}(*)=K$, and $\inpt f$ picks out the preceding wire for each internal input: the first internal input is fed by the external input, and input $n>1$ is fed by output $n-1$.

Applying $\rr^\blank$ from \cref{lemma.lens_pow}, we obtain the lens $\binom{\rr^K}{\rr^K}\to\binom{\rr}{\rr}$ given by
\[
\outp f(x_1,\ldots,x_K)\coloneqq x_K
\qqand
\inpt f\bigl((x_1,\ldots,x_K),\,x_0\bigr)\coloneqq(x_0,\,x_1,\,\ldots,\,x_{K-1}).
\]
{\emergencystretch=2em
Lenses without potential and without parameterization are special cases of smooth rewiring diagrams in $\srwd$, i.e.\ taking $V'\coloneqq\rr^0$ and the $0$-potential $U'\coloneqq0\colon\rr^K\times\rr\to\rr$, we have the wiring as a smooth (static) wiring diagram
\par}
\[
\fun{wire}_K\colon\boxob\tpow{K}\to\boxob,\qquad \left(\binom{\inpt f}{\outp f},0\right)
\]

Composing, we obtain a $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram, $\fun{wire}_K(\Part,\ldots,\Part)$, with parameter $V\coloneqq V'\oplus\rr^{\oplus K}=\rr^K$ (one $\rr$ per particle) and constant sharp
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.sharp_chain}
\sharpR_{\vec x}\colon V^*\to V,\qquad p\mapsto p/m,
\end{equation}
acting per-particle on each $\rr$-summand (the inverse-mass pairing), independent of $\vec x\in V$. The defining maps are
\[
\outp f(x_1,\ldots,x_K)\coloneqq x_K
\qqand
U\bigl((x_1,\ldots,x_K),\,x_0\bigr)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{K}\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(x_i-x_{i-1})^2,
\]
with $\inpt f$ vacuous. The sum in $U$ comes from the productor \eqref{eqn.monoid_productor} of the monoidal monad $\rr\times\blank$ on $\Lens\mfd$, where $(\rr,0,+)$ is the additive monoid. Composition with $\fun{wire}_K$ then substitutes $y_i\mapsto x_{i-1}$ via $\inpt f$ (with $x_0$ the external input).

\paragraph{The discrete dynamics.}

The discrete dynamics appear when we apply the functor $\Phiphase\colon\srwd\to\org$. The object $\boxob$ is sent to
\[
\Phiphase(\boxob)=\sum_{(x,\omega):\rr\times\Omega(\rr)}\yon^{T^*_x\rr\times \rr}.
\]
The map $\fun{wire}_K(\Part,\ldots,\Part)$ is sent to a coalgebra
\[
\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K(\Part,\ldots,\Part))\colon S\To{}\Phiphase(\boxob)\tri S
\]
with state set $S\coloneqq T^*\rr^K$, with elements written $s=((x_1,\ldots,x_K),(p_1,\ldots,p_K))$ for positions and momenta (so $\xi=\vec p$ in terms of \eqref{eqn.state_update}), and with the function given by
\begin{equation*}
(\vec x,\vec p)\mapsto\Bigl(\bigl(x_K,\,\omega_\boxob\bigr),\;(\xi_N,x_0)\mapsto\bigl(\vec x+\tfrac{1}{m}\vec p,\,\vec p-\xi_V\bigr)\Bigr),
\end{equation*}
where each piece is read off from the unpacking in \cref{sec.phase_dynamics}:
\begin{itemize}
\item The output position $x_K$ comes from $\outp f$ via \eqref{eqn.outpn}.
\item The \emph{harmonic spring} covector field on the left-hand side,
\[\omega_\boxob(x_0)=d\bigl[\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(x_1-x_0)^2\bigr]\big|_{x_0}=-\kappa(x_1-x_0),\]
comes from \eqref{eqn.omegaprime} (with $\inpt f$ and $\omega_M$ vacuous, and $dx_0=+1$ via \eqref{eqn.d_potential}).
\item The pullback covector $\xi_V\in T^*_{\vec x}V$ comes from \eqref{eqn.bigtheta}. It is given component-wise (via \eqref{eqn.tangent_vec} identifying $T^*_{\vec x}V\cong V^*=(\rr^K)^*$) by
\begin{align*}
(\xi_V)_i&=
\bigl((T_{\vec x}\outp f)^\top\xi_N\bigr)_i+\partial_{x_i}U(\vec x,x_0)\\&=
\begin{cases}\partial_{x_i}U(\vec x,x_0)+0&1\leq i<K\\ \partial_{x_K}U(\vec x,x_0)+\xi_N&i=K\end{cases}\\&=
\begin{cases}\kappa(2x_i-x_{i-1}-x_{i+1})&1\leq i<K\\ \kappa(x_K-x_{K-1})+\xi_N&i=K\end{cases}
\end{align*}
\item The state update applied to $s=(\vec x,\vec p)$ comes from \eqref{eqn.state_update}: using our (constant) sharp $\sharpR_{\vec x}(\vec p)=\vec p/m$ from \eqref{eqn.sharp_chain} and the symplectic sharp $\sharpS_S\colon(\xi,x)\mapsto(x,-\xi)$ from \eqref{eqn.sharp_S}, we get
\[
s'\coloneqq s+\sharpS_S(\xi_V,\sharpR_{\vec x}(\vec p))=(\vec x+\vec p/m,\,\vec p-\xi_V).
\]
In other words, writing $s'=(x_1',\ldots,x_K',p_1',\ldots,p_K')$, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.potlens_composite_final}
x_i'\coloneqq x_i+p_i/m
\qqand
p_i'\coloneqq\begin{cases}p_i+\kappa(x_{i-1}+x_{i+1}-2x_i)&1\leq i<K\\ p_i+\kappa(x_{K-1}-x_K)-\xi_N&i=K.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Recovering the wave equation.}
To recover the discrete wave equation everywhere, we pin both ends of the chain by fixing the phantom neighbor displacements outside the chain to zero:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.wave_pin}
  x_0\coloneqq 0
  \qqand
  x_{K+1}\coloneqq 0.
\end{equation}
Supplying these via the external direction-input means setting
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.wave_bc}
x_0\coloneqq 0\qqand\xi_N\coloneqq \kappa\,x_K.
\end{equation}
With these choices, the boundary case at $i=K$ becomes $p_K'=p_K+\kappa(x_{K-1}-2x_K)$, so the above state update formula reduces to this:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.state_update_explicit}
x_i'\coloneqq x_i+p_i/m
\qqand
p_i'\coloneqq p_i+\kappa(x_{i-1}+x_{i+1}-2x_i).
\end{equation}

Following \cref{ex.y_coalgebra_trajectory}, fix any initial $s(0)\coloneqq(\vec{x}(0),\vec{p}(0))$ and write $s(t)$ for the resulting trajectory under \eqref{eqn.state_update_explicit}; in particular $s(t+1)=s(t)'$. We then rewrite the first equation as $p_i(t)=mx_i(t+1)-mx_i(t)$ and substitute it into the second:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.recurrence}
mx_i(t+2)-mx_i(t+1)=mx_i(t+1)-mx_i(t)+\kappa(x_{i-1}(t)+x_{i+1}(t)-2x_i(t))
\end{equation}
Rearranging, we obtain the wave equation formula \eqref{eqn.discrete_wave} with the same notation as used there.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.wave_equation}
\leavevmode\par\nobreak
With $\Part$, $\fun{wire}_K$, and the boundary choices \eqref{eqn.wave_pin} and \eqref{eqn.wave_bc} as above, the $\Phiphase(\boxob)$-coalgebra
\[
\Phiphase\bigl(\fun{wire}_K(\Part,\ldots,\Part)\bigr)
\]
recovers the discrete wave equation \eqref{eqn.discrete_wave}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is the calculation of \eqref{eqn.state_update_explicit}: writing the state as a function of time and eliminating the momenta gives \eqref{eqn.discrete_wave}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Adaptive spring material]\label{rmk.adaptive_spring}
The above chain uses a constant coupling $\kappa$, but the $\srwd$ formalism supports adaptive ones, as mentioned in \cref{sec.intro_smooth_rewiring}. Replace $\fun{wire}_K$ with a $K$-ary smooth rewiring diagram $\fun{wire}^{\mathrm{adapt}}_K\colon\boxob\tpow{K}\to\boxob$ whose parameter space $(V,\sharpR_V)$ is $\rr^{K-1}$ (one stiffness $\kappa_j$ per bond) with variable sharp $\sharpR_\kappa(\xi)_j\coloneqq-\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}\kappa_j\xi_j$, whose output and input maps $(\outp f,\inpt f)$ are the same as $\fun{wire}_K$, and whose potential is
\[
U\bigl((\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_{K-1}),(x_1,\ldots,x_K),x_0\bigr)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{K-1}\tfrac{\kappa_j}{2}(x_{j+1}-x_j)^2.
\]
The resulting composite $\fun{wire}^{\mathrm{adapt}}_K(\Part,\ldots,\Part)$ is a $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram whose parameter space is $\rr^K\oplus\rr^{K-1}$ (displacements plus stiffnesses). Under $\Phiconf$, the differential of $U$ with respect to $\kappa_j$ contributes a learning signal $\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{j+1}-x_j)^2$, and the variable sharp makes the stiffness update multiplicative: $\kappa_j\mapsto\kappa_j\bigl(1-\eta_{\mathrm{LR}}\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{j+1}-x_j)^2\bigr)$. This is an instance of natural gradient descent~\cite{amari1998natural}. We do not unpack the full coupled dynamics here.\qedhere
\end{remark}

\subsection{Functoriality audit: composing in \texorpdfstring{$\org$}{Org}}\label{sec.spring_second_pass}

The wave equation was derived in \cref{sec.spring_first_pass}; operad-functoriality of $\Phiphase$ forces equality of the two sides below:
\[
\Phiphase\bigl(\fun{wire}_K(\Part,\ldots,\Part)\bigr)=\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K)\bigl(\Phiphase(\Part),\ldots,\Phiphase(\Part)\bigr),
\]
We now work out the right-hand side term by term---applying $\Phiphase$ to the particle and to the wiring separately, then composing in $\org$---as an explicit check on signs, indices, and the routing of inputs through the formulas.

\paragraph{The particle dynamics.}

We first apply $\Phiphase$ to $\Part\colon\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}\to\boxob$ to obtain a coalgebra on
\[
  \left[\Phiphase\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0},\Phiphase(\boxob)\right]=
  \left[\yon,\Phiphase(\boxob)\right]\cong\Phiphase(\boxob)=
  \sum_{(x,\omega):\rr\times\Omega(\rr)}\yon^{T^*_x\rr\times\rr}
\]
We took the parameter space to be $\rr$, so by \eqref{eqn.state_space} the state set of $\Phiphase(\Part)$ is $S\coloneqq T^*\rr$, with elements written $s=(x,p)$ for position and momentum. The map
\[
S\To{\Phiphase(\Part)}
\sum_{(x,\omega):\rr\times\Omega(\rr)}S^{T^*_x\rr\times\rr}
\]
is given by
\[
(x,p)\mapsto\Bigl(\bigl(x,\,x_{\fun{prev}}\mapsto\kappa(x_{\fun{prev}}-x)\bigr),\;(\xi_{\fun{next}},x_{\fun{prev}})\mapsto\bigl(x+p/m,\,p-\xi_{\fun{next}}+\kappa(x_{\fun{prev}}-x)\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
In other words, starting at position $x$ and momentum $p$, it outputs $x$ to the right and the covector field $x_{\fun{prev}}\mapsto\kappa(x_{\fun{prev}}-x)$, which ``pulls'' $x_{\fun{prev}}$ toward $x$. Then, receiving a covector $\xi_{\fun{next}}$ from the right and a point $x_{\fun{prev}}$ from the left, it updates its position by adding velocity (momentum divided by mass) and updates its momentum by adding the spring force $\kappa(x_{\fun{prev}}-x)$, which ``pulls'' $x$ toward $x_{\fun{prev}}$, and subtracting the received covector $\xi_{\fun{next}}$.
 
\paragraph{The static wiring.}

Apply $\Phiphase$ to $\fun{wire}_K\colon\boxob^{\otimes K}\to\boxob$. With trivial parameter $\rr^0$, the state space $T^*\rr^0$ is a single point, so $\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K)$ is \emph{static}: a function $1\to[\Phiphase(\boxob)^{\otimes K},\allowbreak\Phiphase(\boxob)](1)$ is equivalently (see \eqref{eqn.dirichlet_hom}) a polynomial map $\Phiphase(\boxob)^{\otimes K}\to\allowbreak\Phiphase(\boxob)$, i.e.\
\[
\sum_{(\vec x,\vec\omega):\rr^K\times\Omega(\rr)^K}\yon^{T^*_{\vec x}\rr^K\times\rr^K}
\To{\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K)}
\sum_{(x,\omega):\rr\times\Omega(\rr)}\yon^{T^*_x\rr\times\rr}.
\]
To each $(\vec x,\vec \omega)$, the vector of outputs of the internal boxes, we assign the external output $(x_K,\omega_1)$ (the rightmost particle's position with the leftmost box's covector field); and to each external input $(\xi_N,x_0)$ at the external box, we assign the internal inputs $(\omega_2(x_1),x_0)$, $(\omega_3(x_2),x_1)$, $\ldots$, $(\omega_K(x_{K-1}),x_{K-2})$, $(\xi_N,x_{K-1})$---each box $i<K$ receives its right neighbor's covector field evaluated at $x_i$ in its $\xi$-component, box $K$ gets the external $\xi_N$, and each box receives its left neighbor's position. In our concise format,
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.org_wiring_concise}
(\vec x,\vec \omega)\mapsto\Big((x_K,\omega_1),\;(\xi_N,x_0)\mapsto\bigl((\omega_2(x_1),\ldots,\omega_K(x_{K-1}),\xi_N),\,(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{K-1})\bigr)\Big).
\end{equation}
The routing $\omega_{i+1}(x_i)$ into box $i$'s $\xi$-component is how the wiring transmits the spring force from box $i+1$ to box $i$; the only external forcing is via $x_0$ (the leftmost neighbor position, fed to box $1$) and $\xi_N$ (the covector at $x_K$, fed to box $K$) in \eqref{eqn.wave_wd}.

\paragraph{Composition.}

The $\org$-composite $\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K)\bigl(\Phiphase(\Part),\ldots,\Phiphase(\Part)\bigr)$ is a $0$-ary morphism $\yon\to\Phiphase(\boxob)$ in $\org$, i.e.\ a $\Phiphase(\boxob)$-coalgebra. By \cref{prop.org_as_para}, composition in $\org$ multiplies state spaces and composes the underlying polynomial maps; here $\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K)$ has trivial state space and each $\Phiphase(\Part)$ has state space $T^*\rr$, so the composite has state space $1\times(T^*\rr)^K=T^*\rr^K$. Concretely, the $K$-many single-particle coalgebra maps $\Phiphase(\Part)\colon T^*\rr\to\Phiphase(\boxob)\tri T^*\rr$ combine via the lax monoidal structure of $\blank\coalg$ (\cref{prop.org}) into a single $\Phiphase(\boxob)^{\otimes K}$-coalgebra with states $T^*\rr^K$, and the static polynomial map $\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K)\colon\Phiphase(\boxob)^{\otimes K}\to\Phiphase(\boxob)$ then post-composes:
\[
T^*\rr^K\To{\Phiphase(\Part)^{\otimes K}}\Phiphase(\boxob)^{\otimes K}\tri T^*\rr^K\To{\Phiphase(\fun{wire}_K)\tri T^*\rr^K}\Phiphase(\boxob)\tri T^*\rr^K.
\]
Given a tuple $(\vec x,\vec p):T^*\rr^K$, the first arrow sends it to
\begin{equation*}
\Bigl(\bigl(x_i,\,x_i'\mapsto\kappa(x_i'-x_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^K,\;(\vec\xi_{\fun{next}},\vec x_{\fun{prev}})\mapsto\bigl(\vec x+\vec p/m,\,\vec p-\vec\xi_{\fun{next}}+\kappa(\vec x_{\fun{prev}}-\vec x)\bigr)\Bigr).
\end{equation*}
When applying the second arrow \eqref{eqn.org_wiring_concise} to the result, the threading goes as follows. 

Beginning with $(\vec x,\vec p):T^*\rr^K$, the coalgebras output $(\vec x,\vec\omega)\coloneqq\bigl(x_i,\,x_i'\mapsto\kappa(x_i'-x_i)\bigr)_{i=1}^K$. The wiring takes that, outputs the last point and the first covector field, $(x_K,\omega_1)$, and asks for $(\xi_N,x_0)$. Upon receiving it, it sends $\bigl((\omega_2(x_1),\ldots,\omega_K(x_{K-1}),\xi_N),\,(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{K-1})\bigr)$ back to the coalgebras. In other words, it sends $(\vec\xi_{\fun{next}},\vec x_{\fun{prev}})$ where $(\xi_{\fun{next}})_i\coloneqq\omega_{i+1}(x_i)=\kappa(x_i-x_{i+1})$ for $1\leq i<K$ and $(\xi_{\fun{next}})_K\coloneqq\xi_N$, and $(x_{\fun{prev}})_i=x_{i-1}$. This in turn is sent to $(\vec x+\vec p/m,\,\vec p-\vec\xi_{\fun{next}}+\kappa(\vec x_{\fun{prev}}-\vec x))$. This is exactly \eqref{eqn.potlens_composite_final}: the two routes through the functoriality square agree term by term, confirming the signs, indices, and covector-field evaluations in the formulas above.

\section{Graph Laplacian on a directed graph}\label{sec.graph_laplacian}

The chain of \cref{sec.spring_first_pass} is one instance of a more general construction: again using the harmonic particle, any finite directed graph $G=(E\tto V)$ yields a $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram whose image under $\Phiphase$ is the discrete dynamics of the \emph{graph Laplacian} of $G$. Hopefully this serves as a sanity check on the formalism of this paper.

\paragraph{From directed graph to static wiring diagram.}

Let $G\coloneqq(V,E,\src,\tgt)$ be a finite directed graph: i.e.\ $V$ is a finite vertex set, $E$ is a finite edge set, and $\src,\tgt\colon E\to V$ are functions assigning a source and target vertex to each edge. For each $v\in V$ write
\[
\outp{v}\coloneqq\src^{-1}(v)\subseteq E\qqand \inpt{v}\coloneqq\tgt^{-1}(v)\subseteq E
\]
for the sets of out-edges and in-edges at $v$. Note that the maps $\tgt$ and $\src$ supply canonical bijections $\sum_{v\in V}\inpt{v}\cong E\cong\sum_{v\in V}\outp{v}$. Denote the composite by
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.prism_f}
\inpt{f}\colon\sum_{v\in V}\inpt{v}\too\sum_{v\in V}\outp{v}
\end{equation}

Working in $\Lens{\finset\op}$ (\cref{ex.lens_finsetop}), define the $|V|$-ary \emph{prism wiring} of $G$ to be the lens
\[
\varphi_G\colon\sum_{v\in V}\binom{\inpt{v}}{\outp{v}}\to\binom{0}{0}
\]
with $\outp{f}\coloneqq\bang\colon 0\to\sum_{v}\outp{v}$ unique and $\inpt{f}\colon\sum_{v}\inpt{v}\to\sum_{v}\outp{v}$ as in \eqref{eqn.prism_f}.%
\footnote{
In fact finite directed graphs are in bijection with $|V|$-ary prisms in $\Lens{\finset\op}$ whose codomain is $\binom{0}{0}$ (hence $\outp f=\bang$) and whose $\inpt f$ is a bijection.
}
Concretely, each $v$ becomes a box with as many output and input ports as the out-degree and in-degree of $v$; the edges connect these ports one-to-one.

Applying $\rr^{\blank}$ (\cref{lemma.lens_pow}) gives a lens
\[
\rr^{\varphi_G}\colon\prod_{v\in V}\binom{\rr^{\inpt{v}}}{\rr^{\outp{v}}}\to\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}
\]
whose input map $\prod_{v}\rr^{\outp{v}}\to\prod_{v}\rr^{\inpt{v}}$ is again the identity on $\rr^E$. As in \cref{sec.spring_first_pass}, we treat $\rr^{\varphi_G}$ as a parameter-free, potential-free smooth (static) wiring diagram in $\srwd$ by taking $V'\coloneqq\rr^0$ and $U'\coloneqq 0$.

\paragraph{The particle at a vertex.}

For each $v\in V$, fix a mass $m_v>0$, and fix a stiffness $\kappa_e>0$ for each $e\in\inpt{v}$. The \emph{harmonic particle at $v$} is the $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram
\[
\Part_v\colon\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}\to\binom{\rr^{\inpt{v}}}{\rr^{\outp{v}}}
\]
defined by:
\begin{itemize}
\item parameter space $V_v\coloneqq\rr\in\rvect$ with constant sharp $\sharpR_x(p)\coloneqq p/m_v$ (independent of $x\in\rr$);
\item output map $\outp{f_v}\colon V_v\to\rr^{\outp{v}}$ the broadcast diagonal $x\mapsto(x)_{e\in\outp{v}}$, sending the particle's position to each of its $|\outp{v}|$-many out-edges;
\item input map $\inpt{f_v}\coloneqq\bang\colon V_v\times\rr^{\inpt{v}}\to\rr^0$, uniquely determined;
\item potential $U_v\colon V_v\times\rr^{\inpt{v}}\to\rr$ given by
\[
U_v\bigl(x,(n_e)_{e\in\inpt{v}}\bigr)\coloneqq\sum_{e\in\inpt{v}}\tfrac{\kappa_e}{2}\,(x-n_e)^2.
\]
\end{itemize}
The particle $\Part$ of \cref{sec.spring_first_pass} is the case where $|\outp{v}|=|\inpt{v}|\coloneqq 1$ with $\kappa_e\coloneqq\kappa$ and $m_v\coloneqq m$ for a single $\kappa,m>0$.

\paragraph{The composite.}

Composing $\rr^{\varphi_G}$ with the family $(\Part_v)_{v\in V}$ in $\srwd$ produces a $0$-ary smooth rewiring diagram
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.psi_graph_lap}
\psi_G\coloneqq\rr^{\varphi_G}\bigl((\Part_v)_{v\in V}\bigr)\colon\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}\to\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}
\end{equation}
with composite parameter space $V_G\coloneqq\bigoplus_{v\in V}V_v=\rr^V$, constant sharp $\sharpR_{\vec x}(\vec p)\coloneqq(p_v/m_v)_{v\in V}$, vacuous output and input maps, and composite potential $\rr^V\to\rr$ calculated as
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.graph_potential}
U(\vec x)\;=\;\sum_{v\in V}U_v\bigl(x_v,(x_{\src(e)})_{e\in\inpt{v}}\bigr)\;=\;\sum_{e\in E}\tfrac{\kappa_e}{2}\,(x_{\tgt(e)}-x_{\src(e)})^2.
\end{equation}
The first equality comes from $\outp f_v$ and $\inpt f_v$ for each $v\in V$; the second equality uses the partition $E=\bigsqcup_{v\in V}\inpt{v}$.

\paragraph{The discrete dynamics.}

Next we will apply $\Phiphase$ to $\psi_G\colon\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}\to\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}$. By \cref{sec.phase_dynamics}, since $\Phiphase\binom{\rr^0}{\rr^0}=\yon$, we have that $\Phiphase(\psi_G)$ is a $[\yon,\yon]=\yon$-coalgebra, i.e.\ a function $T^*\rr^V\to T^*\rr^V$. For $(\vec x,\vec p)\in T^*\rr^V$, it is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.graph_state_update}
\Phiphase(\psi_G)(\vec x,\vec p)\coloneqq\bigl(\vec x+(p_v/m_v)_{v\in V},\,\vec p-\xi_{V_G}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\xi_{V_G}\in(\rr^V)^*$ is as in \eqref{eqn.bigtheta}. With $\outp f$ and $\inpt f$ both vacuous, the pullback terms $(T_{\vec x}\outp f)^\top\xi_N$ and $(T_{\vec x}\inpt f)^\top\omega_M(\inpt m)$ vanish, leaving $\xi_{V_G}=dU(\vec x)$, i.e.\ the differential of \eqref{eqn.graph_potential}. Component-wise (via \eqref{eqn.tangent_vec}):
\begin{align}\label{eqn.no_self}
(\xi_{V_G})_v
&=\partial_{x_v}\sum_{e\in E}\tfrac{\kappa_e}{2}(x_{\tgt(e)}-x_{\src(e)})^2\\\nonumber
&=\sum_{e\in E}\kappa_e\,(x_{\tgt(e)}-x_{\src(e)})\cdot\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\tgt(e)=v}-\mathbf{1}_{\src(e)=v}\bigr)\\\nonumber
&=\sum_{e\in\inpt{v}}\kappa_e(x_v-x_{\src(e)})\;+\;\sum_{e\in\outp{v}}\kappa_e(x_v-x_{\tgt(e)}).
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{1}_{\tgt(e)=v}$ and $\mathbf{1}_{\src(e)=v}$ are indicator functions. We may assume that $\src(e)\neq \tgt(e)$ for all $e$, because self-loops contribute nothing to the sum \eqref{eqn.no_self}. Define a $(V\times V)$-matrix $L_G$ to be the linear function that sends $\vec{x}$ to the vector with the following components:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.graph_laplacian}
(L_G\,\vec x)_v\;\coloneqq\;\sum_{e\in\inpt{v}}\kappa_e(x_v-x_{\src(e)})\;+\;\sum_{e\in\outp{v}}\kappa_e(x_v-x_{\tgt(e)});
\end{equation}
One can check that $L_G$ has diagonal and off-diagonal entries given by
\[
(L_G)_{vv}=\sum_{e\in\inpt{v}\cup\outp{v}}\kappa_e
\qqand
(L_G)_{vw}=-\sum_{\{\src(e),\tgt(e)\}=\{v,w\}}\kappa_e
\]
This is the (weighted, symmetrized) \emph{graph Laplacian} of $G$~\cite[\S 1.2--1.4]{chung1997spectral}.

Fixing an initial state $(\vec x(0),\vec p(0))\in T^*\rr^V$ and iterating $\Phiphase(\psi_G)$ yields, by \cref{ex.y_coalgebra_trajectory}, a trajectory $\bigl(\vec x(t),\vec p(t)\bigr)_{t\in\nn}$. As a notational convenience, write $\ddot{x}_v(t)\coloneqq x_v(t+2)-2x_v(t+1)+x_v(t)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.graph_laplacian}
For $\psi_G$ as in \eqref{eqn.psi_graph_lap}, every trajectory of $\Phiphase(\psi_G)\colon T^*\rr^V\to T^*\rr^V$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eqn.graph_laplacian_dynamics}
m_v\,\ddot{x}_v(t)\;=\;-\bigl(L_G\,\vec x(t)\bigr)_v
\qquad(v\in V,\ t\in\nn),
\end{equation}
where $L_G$ is the graph Laplacian \eqref{eqn.graph_laplacian} of $G$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From the state update equation \eqref{eqn.graph_state_update}, the position update is $x_v(t+1)=x_v(t)+p_v(t)/m_v$; solving for momentum we get $p_v(t)=m_v\bigl(x_v(t+1)-x_v(t)\bigr)$. And the momentum update is $p_v(t+1)=p_v(t)-(\xi_{V_G})_v=p_v(t)-(L_G\vec x(t))_v$, using \eqref{eqn.graph_laplacian} for the last equality. Substituting the first into the second gives
\[
m_v\bigl(x_v(t+2)-x_v(t+1)\bigr)\;=\;m_v\bigl(x_v(t+1)-x_v(t)\bigr)-(L_G\vec x(t))_v,
\]
and collecting the position terms on the left yields \eqref{eqn.graph_laplacian_dynamics}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Heat equation from the configuration integrator]\label{rmk.graph_heat}
Applying $\Phiconf$ rather than $\Phiphase$ to the same graph wiring and potential, but with the opposite-sign reaction $\sharpR^{\mathrm{heat}}_{\vec x}(\vec p)\coloneqq-(p_v/m_v)_v$, recovers the discrete \emph{heat} equation on $G$. The state set is now $V_G=\rr^V$ (no momentum), and using the same $\xi_{V_G}=L_G\vec x$ from \eqref{eqn.graph_laplacian}, the update $\vec x(t+1)=\vec x(t)+\sharpR^{\mathrm{heat}}_{\vec x(t)}(\xi_{V_G})$ reads component-wise as
\[
m_v\bigl(x_v(t+1)-x_v(t)\bigr)\;=\;-\bigl(L_G\,\vec x(t)\bigr)_v
\qquad(v\in V,\ t\in\nn),
\]
i.e.\ explicit-Euler diffusion on $G$ with vertex masses as heat capacities. Thus the phase integrator with the positive inverse-mass reaction gives wave dynamics, while the configuration integrator with the opposite-sign reaction gives heat dynamics on the same graph wiring and potential.
\end{remark}

Both wave and heat dynamics on $\psi_G$ exhibit superposition: both $\Phiphase(\psi_G)$ and $\Phiconf(\psi_G)$ are linear endomaps on their respective state spaces. This is because $\psi_G$ has constant sharp, linear output map, vacuous input map, and quadratic potential---conditions that force the resulting state-update map to be linear for either integrator.

\section*{Conclusion}

The operad $\srwd$ of smooth rewiring diagrams provides a single compositional syntax in which gradient-based learning and Hamiltonian-style mechanics coexist as functorial semantics $\Phiconf,\Phiphase\colon\srwd\to\org$. The key mediating construction is Moore internalization, which converts lens interfaces into polynomial interfaces, and integrators, which determine how stored state relates to the parameter space.

Moreover, the two integrators interact with the neural-network example in a suggestive way. Applying $\Phiconf$ to the parameterized map $F\colon V\times\rr^m\to\rr^n$ of \cref{sec.dl_warmup} recovers gradient descent; we expect that applying $\Phiphase$ to the same $\srwd$-morphism, with a constant inverse-mass sharp $\sharpR_x(p)=\frac{1}{m}p$, yields momentum-based weight dynamics---the undamped heavy-ball method---placing standard and momentum-based training as two integrators of the same smooth rewiring syntax, just as the wave and heat equations arise as two integrators of the same graph wiring (\cref{rmk.graph_heat}).

Several directions remain open. Replacing reactive vector spaces with reactive manifolds equipped with connections (\cref{rmk.rmfdc_generalization}) would extend the framework beyond flat parameter spaces. The multi-stage bicategory $\org^{(K)}$ sketched in \cref{rmk.org_N} is a natural target for multi-step (e.g.\ symplectic) integrators such as leapfrog, which would address the energy drift inherent in the explicit-Euler step (\cref{rmk.euler_energy}). Substituting the stochastic polynomial $\mathrm{rand}$ for $\yon$ in the potential monoid (\cref{rmk.alpha_general}) would give stochastic update rules, opening a path toward compositional stochastic dynamics. Finally, it may be that the superposition principle exhibited by the wave and heat examples can be generalized to a ``linear stratum'' suboperad $\srwd^{\tn{lin}}\ss\srwd$ by restricting to constant sharps, linear lens maps, and quadratic potentials.
