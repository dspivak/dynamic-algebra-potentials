\title{Harmonic Chains, Characteristic Queues, and Associative Strings}

\begin{abstract}
The CDLM framework of adaptive arrangements is parametric in an
adaptive-arrangement datum.  We assemble a \emph{Hilbert arrangement datum}
$\Hb$---spaces are real Hilbert spaces with smooth maps, parameters are
reactive Hilbert spaces, potentials accumulate in $\R$---and, following the
framework, obtain the operad $\harr$ of Hilbert arrangements, its polynomial
interpretation, and a phase integrator, hence an operad functor
$\Phiphase\colon\harr\to\pc$.

The homogeneous harmonic chain lives in $\harr$: masses and bonds are
arrangements, and finite chains are their operadic composites.  Finite
arity forbids an infinite composite, but the biinfinite lattice is the
colimit of the clamped chains in the category of closed harmonic
systems, and the semantics sends this colimit to a strong limit.  On
closed harmonic systems the phase semantics lifts to linear dynamics,
and the harmonic system of any unitary $\Z$-representation $(X,u)$
carries a characteristic morphism to the shift pair $u\oplus u^{-1}$;
at the shift representation and the characteristic tuning of step
scaling to cell duration, this extracts two oppositely shifting
characteristic fields.  The shift system so obtained factors, in the
hom-categories of the semantics, as a standard queue composed with its
environment; a finite segment thus carries two standard queues.

The standard queue is terminal among bounded linear realizations of exact
delay.  Its subdivision is governed by time change along multiplication
by $r$ on the monoid of discrete times.  This identifies the categorical
reason that one coarse update equals two refined updates.  Hilbert
completion of the resulting dyadic system is the weighted space
$L^2([0,d),Z\,dx)$.

Weighted interval spaces form a strong algebra for ordered interval
concatenation.  Any such algebra canonically determines lossless delay lines;
the finite queues embed into this construction and determine it densely.
Translation of the two completed characteristic fields is equivalent to the
weak wave equation, modulo constant displacement---so every lattice
trajectory samples a weak solution of the wave equation.  Finally, the same
interval algebra determines an associative serial composition of two-ended
strings.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Categorical background}
\label{sec.background_categories}

We assume familiarity with symmetric monoidal categories; with monoids and
comonoids in them; with lax, strong, and normal (unit-preserving) monoidal
functors and monoidal natural transformations; with monads, monoidal monads,
and adjunctions; and with the writer monad $R\otimes\blank$ of a commutative
monoid object $(R,0,+)$ \cite[Lem.~3.2.1]{cdlm}.%lem.monoid_to_monad
We write $\defineTerm{smsetiso}$ for the category of sets and bijections.

\paragraph{Operads.}
By an \emph{operad} we mean a symmetric colored operad, i.e.\ a symmetric
multicategory \cite{leinster2004higher}: a class of \emph{colors}; for every
finite list of colors $c_1,\ldots,c_K$ and every color $c$ a set of
\emph{operations} $\cat O(c_1,\ldots,c_K;c)$; identities; substitution; and
symmetric-group actions, subject to associativity, unit, and equivariance
laws.  Every symmetric monoidal category $(\cat C,I,\otimes)$ has an
underlying operad with
$\cat O(c_1,\ldots,c_K;c)\coloneqq\cat C(c_1\otimes\cdots\otimes c_K,c)$,
and every lax symmetric monoidal functor induces an \emph{operad functor}
between the underlying operads.  A \emph{full suboperad} is spanned by a
subclass of colors.

For a nonsymmetric colored operad $\cat O$ and a symmetric monoidal category
$(\cat C,I,\otimes)$, an \emph{$\cat O$-algebra in $\cat C$} assigns an
object $A(c):\cat C$ to each color and a map
$A(\varphi)\colon A(c_1)\otimes\cdots\otimes A(c_K)\to A(c)$ to each
operation $\varphi\in\cat O(c_1,\ldots,c_K;c)$, respecting identities and
substitution.  We denote such an algebra as a map $A\colon\cat O\to\cat C$.
We call the maps $A(\varphi)$ the \emph{structure maps} of $A$, and we call
$A$ \emph{strong} if every structure map is an isomorphism.

\paragraph{Semigroupal categories.}
A \emph{semigroupal category} is a category with a tensor product and an
associator satisfying the pentagon, with no unit assumed---the nonunital
version of a monoidal category.  A \emph{strong semigroupal functor} is a
functor equipped with natural isomorphisms
$F(c)\otimes F(c')\cong F(c\otimes c')$ satisfying the associativity
coherence.  A commutative semigroup, such as $(\R_{\geq h},+)$, is a
discrete semigroupal category; a \emph{strong semigroupal representation}
of it in a semigroupal category $\cat D$ is a strong semigroupal functor
into $\cat D$.

\paragraph{Polynomial functors.}
We use the category $\defineTerm{poly}$ of polynomial functors; see
\cite{niu2025polynomial} for a full treatment and \cite[\S2]{cdlm} for the
fragment used here.  A polynomial is a formal coproduct of representables
\[
p=\sum_{i\in p(1)}\yon^{p[i]},
\]
where the elements of the set $p(1)$ are the \emph{positions} of $p$ and the
elements of each set $p[i]$ are the \emph{directions} at $i$.  A morphism
$p\to p'$ is a function forward on positions together with, at each
$i\in p(1)$, a function backward on directions
$p'[i']\to p[i]$.  We write $\defineTerm{yon}$ for the identity polynomial
(one position, one direction), so $\yon$ is the monoidal unit for the
Dirichlet product $\otimes$, which multiplies position sets and direction
sets.  The Dirichlet product is closed; we write $[p,p']$ for the internal
hom.  For a set $S$, we write $\defineTerm{Store}(S)\coloneqq S\yon^S$.  A
\emph{$p$-coalgebra} is a set $S$ with a function $f\colon S\to p(S)$;
equivalently \cite[Prop.~2.2.3]{cdlm},%prop.coalg_as_poly_map
a polynomial map $\Store(S)\to p$.

\paragraph{Polynomial coalgebras.}
We use the $2$-category $\defineTerm{pc}$ of \cite[\S2.2.4]{cdlm}:%sec.org
its objects are polynomials; its hom-category $\pc(p,p')$ is the category of
$[p,p']$-coalgebras and coalgebra morphisms; composition and the monoidal
structure (with unit $\yon$) are by Dirichlet product of coalgebras.  We
refer to objects of $\pc(p,p')$ as \emph{$1$-cells} and to their morphisms
as \emph{$2$-cells}.  Since $[\yon,\yon]\cong\yon$, a $1$-cell
$\yon\to\yon$ is a $\yon$-coalgebra, i.e.\ a set $S$ with a function
$S\to S$---a discrete dynamical system---and a $2$-cell between two such is
a function commuting with the two endofunctions.

\paragraph{The CDLM framework.}
The framework of \cite[Ch.~4]{cdlm} is parametric in an
\emph{adaptive-arrangement datum} $D=(\cat M,\cat Q,J,\Fn R)$: a cartesian
monoidal category $\cat M$ of \emph{spaces}, a symmetric monoidal category
$\cat Q$ of \emph{parameters}, a strong monoidal functor
$J\colon\cat Q\to\cat M$, and a monoidal monad $\Fn R$ on $\cat M$, the
\emph{potentials monad} \cite[Def.~4.1.1]{cdlm}.%def.rewiring_datum
Out of $D$ the framework builds an operad $\mathbb{A}\Cat{rr}_D$ of
\emph{adaptive arrangements}; a \emph{polynomial interpretation datum} for
$D$ \cite[Def.~4.2.1]{cdlm}%def.polynomial_interpretation
then yields a lax symmetric monoidal functor into parameterized polynomials
\cite[Thm.~4.2.2]{cdlm},%thm.poly_interpretation
and an \emph{integrator} \cite[Def.~4.3.4]{cdlm}%def.integrator
turns the result into an operad functor
$\mathbb{A}\Cat{rr}_D\to\pc$ \cite[Thm.~4.3.6]{cdlm}.%thm.dynamics_functor
We recall the relevant definitions at their points of use in
\cref{sec.hilbert_datum,sec.hilbert_semantics}, where we instantiate each of
them in Hilbert spaces.

\subsection{Hilbert-space background}
\label{sec.background_hilbert}

All vector spaces are real.  Standard references for this material are
\cite{conway1990functional} for Hilbert-space theory,
\cite{cartan1971differential} for differential calculus on Banach spaces,
and \cite{brezis2011functional} for Sobolev spaces and distributions.

\paragraph{Pre-Hilbert and Hilbert spaces.}
A \emph{pre-Hilbert space} is a vector space with an inner product
$\langle\blank,\blank\rangle$; a \emph{Hilbert space} is a pre-Hilbert space
that is complete for the induced norm.  A linear map $f\colon V\to W$ is
\emph{bounded} if $\lVert f\rVert\coloneqq\sup_{\lVert v\rVert\leq1}\lVert
f(v)\rVert_W<\infty$; we call $\lVert f\rVert$ the \emph{operator norm} of
$f$.  We write $\defineTerm{prehilb}$ for the category of
pre-Hilbert spaces and bounded linear maps, and $\defineTerm{hilb}$ for its
full subcategory of Hilbert spaces.  A bounded linear map is an
\emph{isometry} if it preserves norms, and \emph{unitary} if it is a
surjective isometry.  In any category, a morphism is a \emph{split
epimorphism} if it admits a section.  For $c\in\R_{>0}$, we write $V[c]$
for the same vector space with the \emph{rescaled} inner product
$c\langle\blank,\blank\rangle$; it is again a pre-Hilbert space, complete
if $V$ is.

\paragraph{Completion.}
The inclusion $\Hilb\hookrightarrow\PreHilb$ has a left adjoint, the
\emph{Hilbert completion} $V\mapsto\widehat V$: the unit $V\to\widehat V$ is
an isometry with dense image, and every bounded linear map $f\colon V\to Y$
with $Y$ complete extends uniquely to $\widehat f\colon\widehat V\to Y$ with
$\lVert\widehat f\rVert=\lVert f\rVert$.

\paragraph{Increasing unions.}
Given pre-Hilbert spaces $H_n$ and isometric embeddings
$\rho_n\colon H_n\hookrightarrow H_{n+1}$ for $n\in\N$, the \emph{increasing
union} $\bigcup_n H_n$ is the union of the images in the colimit of the
underlying vector spaces, a pre-Hilbert space in which each $H_n$ embeds
isometrically and every element lies in some $H_n$.

\begin{lemma}[Isometric colimits]\label{lem.isometric-colimits}
Let $(H_n,\rho_n)_{n\in\N}$ be as above and let $Y\in\Hilb$.  Restriction
along the embeddings $H_n\hookrightarrow\widehat{\bigcup_nH_n}$ is a
bijection
\begin{equation}\label{eq.completion-universal-property}
\Hilb\Bigl(\widehat{\textstyle\bigcup_nH_n},\ Y\Bigr)
\cong
\left\{
(f_n)_{n\in\N}
\ \middle|\
\begin{array}{l}
f_n\colon H_n\to Y\text{ is bounded},\\
f_{n+1}\rho_n=f_n,\
\sup_n\lVert f_n\rVert<\infty
\end{array}
\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Restriction sends a bounded $f$ to a compatible family with
$\lVert f_n\rVert\leq\lVert f\rVert$.  It is injective because
$\bigcup_nH_n$ is dense in the completion.  For surjectivity, let $(f_n)$
be a compatible family with $s\coloneqq\sup_n\lVert f_n\rVert<\infty$.
Every element $x\in\bigcup_nH_n$ lies in some $H_n$; setting
$F(x)\coloneqq f_n(x)$ is well defined by compatibility, linear, and
bounded with $\lVert F\rVert\leq s$.  By the completion adjunction, $F$
extends uniquely to a bounded map on $\widehat{\bigcup_nH_n}$, which
restricts to the given family.
\end{proof}

Restricted to contractions, \cref{eq.completion-universal-property} says
exactly that $\widehat{\bigcup_nH_n}$ is the colimit of $(H_n,\rho_n)$ in
the category of Hilbert spaces and linear contractions.

\paragraph{Duals, Riesz representation, adjoints.}
The \emph{dual} $V^*$ of a pre-Hilbert space $V$ is the space of bounded
linear functionals with the operator norm.  For $V$ a Hilbert space, $V^*$
is a Hilbert space, and the map $V\to V^*$,
$v\mapsto\langle v,\blank\rangle$, is a unitary isomorphism---this is the
Riesz representation theorem \cite{conway1990functional}.  We define the
\emph{Riesz map} $\defineTerm{riesz}_V\colon V^*\To{\cong}V$ to be its inverse.
Consequently every Hilbert space is \emph{reflexive}: the evaluation map
$\iota_V\colon V\to V^{**}$, $\iota_V(v)(\xi)\coloneqq\xi(v)$, is a unitary
isomorphism, and we use it silently to identify $V^{**}$ with $V$.  A
bounded linear map $f\colon V\to W$ has a \emph{transpose}
$f^*\colon W^*\to V^*$, $f^*(\eta)\coloneqq\eta\circ f$, with
$\lVert f^*\rVert=\lVert f\rVert$, $(g\circ f)^*=f^*\circ g^*$, and
$(f^{-1})^*=(f^*)^{-1}$ when $f$ is invertible.  Under reflexivity,
$f^{**}=f$.

For Hilbert spaces, conjugating the transpose by the Riesz maps gives the
\emph{adjoint}
\[
f^\dagger\coloneqq \riesz_V\circ f^*\circ\riesz_W^{-1}\colon W\to V,
\qquad
\langle f(v),w\rangle_W=\langle v,f^\dagger(w)\rangle_V,
\]
and $(\blank)^\dagger$ is a \emph{dagger structure} on $\Hilb$
\cite{heunen2019categories}: an identity-on-objects contravariant
involution, i.e.
\[
\id_V^\dagger=\id_V,
\qquad
(g\circ f)^\dagger=f^\dagger\circ g^\dagger,
\qquad
f^{\dagger\dagger}=f.
\]
Rearranging the definition of the adjoint gives the \emph{Riesz
identities}: for bounded $f\colon V\to W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq.riesz-identities}
\riesz_V\circ f^*=f^\dagger\circ\riesz_W
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\riesz_W\circ(f^\dagger)^*=f\circ\riesz_V,
\end{equation}
the second by applying the first to $f^\dagger$ in place of $f$.
In dagger terms the isometries and unitaries of the previous paragraph are
the standard notions: $f$ is an isometry if and only if
$f^\dagger\circ f=\id$ (by polarization), and unitary if and only if it is
a \emph{dagger isomorphism}, $f^\dagger\circ f=\id$ and
$f\circ f^\dagger=\id$.

\paragraph{Direct sums.}
The direct sum $V\oplus W$ of pre-Hilbert spaces carries the inner product
$\langle(v,w),(v',w')\rangle\coloneqq\langle v,v'\rangle+\langle
w,w'\rangle$.  It is the categorical product (and coproduct) in $\PreHilb$
and in $\Hilb$---a biproduct---and the zero space $0$ is a zero object.
On $\Hilb$ it is moreover a \emph{dagger biproduct}: each projection is
the dagger of the corresponding injection
\cite{heunen2019categories}.  The
canonical map $V^*\oplus W^*\to(V\oplus W)^*$, sending $(\xi,\eta)$ to
$(v,w)\mapsto\xi(v)+\eta(w)$, is a unitary isomorphism, and we use it
silently.  For a Hilbert space $V$, we write
\[
T^*V\coloneqq V\oplus V^*,
\]
call it the \emph{phase space} of $V$, and call $V^*$ the \emph{cotangent
space} $T^*_vV\coloneqq V^*$ at any point $v\in V$.  Combining the two
identifications above with reflexivity gives
$(T^*V)^*=(V\oplus V^*)^*\cong V^*\oplus V$, which we also use silently.

\paragraph{Sequence and function spaces.}
For a Hilbert space $B$, define
\[
\ell^2(\Z,B)
\coloneqq
\left\{
a\colon\Z\to B
\ \middle|\
\sum_{i\in\Z}\lVert a_i\rVert_B^2<\infty
\right\},
\qquad
\langle a,b\rangle
\coloneqq
\sum_{i\in\Z}\langle a_i,b_i\rangle_B,
\]
in which the sum defining $\langle a,b\rangle$ converges absolutely by the
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.  This inner product space is complete, hence a
Hilbert space \cite{conway1990functional}.  We write
$\ell^2(\Z)\coloneqq\ell^2(\Z,\R)$; for a finite set $\Lambda$ we write
$\ell^2(\Lambda)\coloneqq\R^\Lambda$ with the standard inner product.  We write
$e_i\in\ell^2(\Z)$ for the $i$th standard basis vector and
$\xi_i\coloneqq\xi(e_i)$ for the components of a functional
$\xi\in\ell^2(\Z)^*$, so that $\riesz_{\ell^2(\Z)}(\xi)=(\xi_i)_{i\in\Z}$.  The
two \emph{shift} operators $(\sigma^{\leftarrow}a)_i\coloneqq a_{i+1}$ and
$(\sigma^{\rightarrow}a)_i\coloneqq a_{i-1}$ are unitary on $\ell^2(\Z,B)$,
and are inverse to each other.

For a measure space $(X,\mu)$ we write $L^2(X,\mu)$ for the Hilbert space
of \emph{square-integrable functions}: measurable functions
$f\colon X\to\R$ with $\int_X|f|^2\,d\mu<\infty$, taken modulo equality
$\mu$-almost everywhere, with inner product
$\langle f,g\rangle\coloneqq\int_Xfg\,d\mu$; completeness is the
Riesz--Fischer theorem \cite{brezis2011functional}.  These spaces
illustrate the Riesz representation theorem concretely: every bounded
functional on $L^2([0,1],dx)$ is $f\mapsto\int_0^1fg\,dx$ for a unique
$g\in L^2([0,1],dx)$---for instance $g=1$ gives the mean of $f$, and
$g=\mathbf1_{[0,1/2]}$ the mass on the left half---whereas point
evaluation $f\mapsto f(\tfrac12)$ is not a bounded functional, being
undefined on almost-everywhere classes.  For $Z\in\R_{>0}$ and an interval $J\subseteq\R$, we write $Z\,dx$ for $Z$
times Lebesgue measure on $J$; thus the \emph{weighted space}
$L^2(J,Z\,dx)$ consists of the square-integrable functions on $J$ with
inner product $\langle f,g\rangle=Z\int_Jfg\,dx$.  The case
$J=[0,d)$ appears throughout.

\paragraph{Dynamics and time change.}
For a monoid $(M,+,0)$, write $\Cat BM$ for the category with one object
whose endomorphism monoid is $M$.  Define
\[
\defineTerm{dynh}_M\coloneqq[\Cat BM,\Hilb]:
\]
an object is a Hilbert space with an action of $M$ by bounded operators,
and a morphism is a bounded linear map commuting with the two actions.
Every monoid homomorphism $\mu\colon M\to M'$ induces a functor
$\Cat B\mu\colon\Cat BM\to\Cat BM'$, and restriction along it is a
functor
\[
\mu^*\coloneqq[\Cat B\mu,\Hilb]\colon\Dyn_{M'}\longrightarrow\Dyn_M,
\]
the \emph{time change} along $\mu$.  We write $\Dyn\coloneqq\Dyn_\N$,
where $\N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$: an object of $\Dyn$ is a pair $(X,F)$ of a
Hilbert space and a bounded endomorphism $F\colon X\to X$, its
\emph{transition}, and a morphism $\alpha\colon(X,F)\to(Y,G)$ is a
bounded linear map $\alpha\colon X\to Y$ satisfying
$\alpha\circ F=G\circ\alpha$.

\paragraph{Unitary representations.}
For a group $G$, the category $\Cat BG$ is a dagger category with
$g^\dagger\coloneqq g^{-1}$, and a \emph{unitary representation} of $G$
is a dagger functor $\Cat BG\to\Hilb$ \cite{heunen2019categories}: an
object of $\Dyn_G$ whose action is by unitaries.  A unitary
representation of $\Z$ is thus a pair $(X,u)$ of a Hilbert space and a
unitary $u\colon X\to X$; a morphism of unitary representations is a
morphism of the underlying objects of $\Dyn_G$.  The \emph{difference
operator} of a unitary representation $(X,u)$ of $\Z$ is
\[
D_u\coloneqq\id_X-u^{-1}\colon X\to X,
\]
bounded with $\lVert D_u\rVert\leq2$ and with adjoint
$D_u^\dagger=\id_X-u$.  The shift $\sigma^{\leftarrow}$ makes
$\ell^2(\Z,B)$ a unitary representation of $\Z$, for which we abbreviate
\[
D\coloneqq D_{\sigma^{\leftarrow}}=\id-\sigma^{\rightarrow}
\colon\ell^2(\Z,B)\to\ell^2(\Z,B),
\]
so that $(Da)_i=a_i-a_{i-1}$ and $(D^\dagger a)_i=a_i-a_{i+1}$.

Every morphism $\alpha\colon(X,u)\to(Y,v)$ of $\Dyn_\Z$ satisfies
$\alpha\circ D_u=D_v\circ\alpha$, since $\alpha u=v\alpha$ implies
$\alpha u^{-1}=v^{-1}\alpha$: the difference operator is a natural
transformation
\[
D\colon\iota^*\Longrightarrow\iota^*,
\qquad
\iota^*\colon\Dyn_\Z\to\Dyn_0=\Hilb,
\]
of the time change along the unique homomorphism $\iota\colon0\to\Z$.
We write $\mathrm U(G)$ for the groupoid of unitary representations of
$G$ and unitary intertwiners, a subcategory of $\Dyn_G$.

\paragraph{Smooth maps.}
Let $V,W$ be Hilbert spaces and $f\colon V\to W$ a function.  The
\emph{Fr\'echet derivative} of $f$ at $v\in V$, when it exists, is the
bounded linear map $D_vf\colon V\to W$ satisfying
\[
\lim_{v'\to0}
\frac{\lVert f(v+v')-f(v)-D_vf(v')\rVert_W}{\lVert v'\rVert_V}=0.
\]
At most one linear map satisfies this: the difference $L$ of two
satisfying maps has $\lVert L(v')\rVert/\lVert v'\rVert\to0$ as $v'\to0$,
and by linearity this ratio is invariant under $v'\mapsto tv'$ for
$t>0$, so it vanishes for every $v'\neq0$, i.e.\ $L=0$.  The map $f$ is
\emph{smooth} if it has Fr\'echet derivatives of all orders, each depending
continuously on the basepoint \cite{cartan1971differential}.  Smooth maps
compose, and the chain rule $D_v(g\circ f)=D_{f(v)}g\circ D_vf$ holds.
Every bounded linear map $f$ is smooth with $D_vf=f$, as is every constant
map and every bounded affine map.  The pairing of smooth maps into a direct
sum is smooth.  For a smooth $U\colon V\to\R$ we write
$dU|_v\coloneqq D_vU\in V^*$, the derivative regarded as a bounded
functional, and call it the \emph{differential} of $U$ at $v$.

A \emph{continuous quadratic form} on a Hilbert space $V$ is a function
$U(v)\coloneqq\tfrac12 B(v,v)$ for a bounded symmetric bilinear
$B\colon V\times V\to\R$.  Every continuous quadratic form is smooth, with
differential the bounded linear map
\[
dU|_v=B(v,\blank)\in V^*.
\]
Equivalently, by Riesz, $U(v)=\tfrac12\langle A(v),v\rangle$ for the
bounded operator $A\coloneqq \riesz_V\circ dU\colon V\to V$, and symmetry of
$B$ says exactly that $A$ is \emph{self-adjoint}: $A^\dagger=A$.  We
define quadratic forms through bounded bilinear maps rather than---as one
may in finite dimensions \cite[Def.~5.3.3]{cdlm}%def.arrangement_terminology
---as linear functionals on a tensor square: on an infinite-dimensional
$V$, functionals on the Hilbert-space tensor square represent only the
\emph{Hilbert--Schmidt} forms, those with
$\sum_{i,j}\langle A(e_i),e_j\rangle^2<\infty$ for an orthonormal basis
$(e_i)$ \cite{conway1990functional}, and already
$U(v)=\tfrac12\lVert v\rVert^2$, whose operator is $A=\id$, is excluded.
The lattice potential \cref{eq.lattice-potential} is likewise not
Hilbert--Schmidt: its operator has the diagonal entry $\tfrac{2Z}h$ at
every site.

\paragraph{Strong convergence.}
A sequence of functions $g_n\colon X\to W$ into a Hilbert space
\emph{converges strongly} to $g$ if $g_n(x)\to g(x)$ in norm for every
$x\in X$.  Strong convergence does not require---and below will not
have---convergence in operator norm.

\begin{lemma}[Strong limits]\label{lem.strong-limits}
Let $X,Y,W$ be Hilbert spaces, and let $F_n,F\colon X\to Y$ and
$G_n,G\colon Y\to W$ be bounded linear maps with
$\sup_n\lVert F_n\rVert<\infty$ and $\sup_n\lVert G_n\rVert<\infty$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item.dense-limit} If $F_nx\to Fx$ for every $x$ in a dense
subset of $X$, then $F_n\to F$ strongly.
\item\label{item.composed-limit} If $F_n\to F$ and $G_n\to G$ strongly,
then $G_n\circ F_n\to G\circ F$ strongly.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\cref{item.dense-limit}: put $s\coloneqq\sup_n\lVert F_n\rVert$.  Let
$x\in X$ and $\varepsilon>0$, and choose $x'$ in the dense subset with
$\lVert x-x'\rVert<\varepsilon$.  For every $n$,
\[
\lVert F_nx-Fx\rVert
\leq
\lVert F_n(x-x')\rVert+\lVert F_nx'-Fx'\rVert+\lVert F(x'-x)\rVert
\leq
\bigl(s+\lVert F\rVert\bigr)\varepsilon+\lVert F_nx'-Fx'\rVert,
\]
and the last term tends to $0$ by hypothesis.
\cref{item.composed-limit}: for every $x\in X$, writing
$G_nF_nx-GFx=G_n(F_nx-Fx)+\bigl(G_n(Fx)-G(Fx)\bigr)$,
\[
\lVert G_nF_nx-GFx\rVert
\leq
\sup_n\lVert G_n\rVert\cdot\lVert F_nx-Fx\rVert
+\lVert G_n(Fx)-G(Fx)\rVert,
\]
and both terms tend to $0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The Hilbert arrangement datum}
\label{sec.hilbert_datum}

We now instantiate the framework.  Recall from
\cite[Def.~4.1.1]{cdlm}%def.rewiring_datum
that an adaptive-arrangement datum consists of a cartesian monoidal category
of spaces, a symmetric monoidal category of parameters, a strong monoidal
functor from parameters to spaces, and a monoidal potentials monad on
spaces.  The smooth datum of \cite[Def.~5.3.1]{cdlm}%def.potlens
takes finite-dimensional manifolds as spaces; here we take Hilbert spaces.

\subsection{Spaces}
\label{sec.hilbert_spaces}

\begin{definition}[Hilbert spaces with smooth maps]\label{def.hsm}
We define the category $\defineTerm{hsm}$ to have real Hilbert spaces as
objects and smooth maps (\cref{sec.background_hilbert}) as morphisms.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.hsm-cartesian}
$(\hsm,1,\oplus)$ is cartesian monoidal, where $1\coloneqq\R^0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The zero space $1=\R^0$ is terminal: the unique map $V\to1$ is constant,
hence smooth.  For Hilbert spaces $W_1,W_2$, the projections
$\pi_i\colon W_1\oplus W_2\to W_i$ are bounded linear, hence smooth.  Given
smooth $f_i\colon V\to W_i$, the pairing
$\langle f_1,f_2\rangle\colon V\to W_1\oplus W_2$ is smooth
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}) and is the unique map whose composites with
the projections are the $f_i$.  Thus $\oplus$ is the categorical product,
and the monoidal structure it induces is cartesian.
\end{proof}

The pair $(\R,0,+)$ is a commutative monoid object in $\hsm$: addition
$\R\oplus\R\to\R$ is bounded linear and the unit $1\to\R$ is constant, so
both are smooth.  By \cite[Lem.~3.2.1]{cdlm},%lem.monoid_to_monad
it induces the monoidal \emph{writer monad} $\R\oplus\blank$ on $\hsm$,
which we abbreviate by its carrier $\R$.  This is our potentials monad: a
backward pass valued in $\R\oplus X$ is an ordinary backward pass valued in
$X$ together with a scalar potential.

\subsection{Parameters}
\label{sec.reactive_hilbert}

\begin{definition}[Reactive Hilbert space]\label{def.rhilb}
A \emph{reactive Hilbert space} is a pair $\rv V\coloneqq(V,\sharpR_V)$ of a
real Hilbert space $V$ and a bounded linear map
$\defineTerm{sharpR}_V\colon V^*\to V$, its \emph{reaction} or \emph{sharp}.  We call
$\rv V$ \emph{nondegenerate} if $\sharpR_V$ is an isomorphism, and
\emph{symmetric} if $\xi'(\sharpR_V\xi)=\xi(\sharpR_V\xi')$ for all
$\xi,\xi'\in V^*$.

We define the category $\defineTerm{rhilb}$ to have reactive Hilbert spaces
as objects and, as morphisms $\rv V\to\rv W$, the bounded linear
isomorphisms $\phi\colon V\To{\cong}W$ that are \emph{sharp-equivariant}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq.sharp-equivariance}
\phi\circ\sharpR_V\circ\phi^*=\sharpR_W
\end{equation}
as maps $W^*\to W$.
\end{definition}

The reactive vector spaces of \cite[Def.~5.2.1]{cdlm}%def.rvect
allow the reaction to vary smoothly with a basepoint $v\in V$; the harmonic
systems of this paper need only basepoint-independent reactions, so we
develop the constant case.  \Cref{eq.sharp-equivariance} is the constant
instance of the sharp-equivariance condition there.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.rhilb-monoidal}
Direct sum makes $\rhilb$ symmetric monoidal, with unit $\rv 0\coloneqq(0,0)$
and
\[
\sharpR_{V\oplus W}\coloneqq\sharpR_V\oplus\sharpR_W
\colon V^*\oplus W^*\to V\oplus W
\]
under the identification $(V\oplus W)^*\cong V^*\oplus W^*$ of
\cref{sec.background_hilbert}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A direct sum of bounded maps is bounded, so $\sharpR_{V\oplus W}$ is a
reaction.  If $\phi\colon\rv V\to\rv W$ and $\phi'\colon\rv V'\to\rv W'$ are
sharp-equivariant, then so is $\phi\oplus\phi'$, because transposes,
composites, and \cref{eq.sharp-equivariance} are all computed summandwise.
The coherence isomorphisms of $(\Hilb,0,\oplus)$ are sharp-equivariant for
the direct-sum reactions, again summandwise, so they lift to $\rhilb$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.rhilb-forgetful}
The forgetful functor $\inc\colon\rhilb\to\hsm$, $(V,\sharpR_V)\mapsto V$, is
strong symmetric monoidal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On both sides the tensor is $\oplus$ with unit the zero space
(\cref{prop.hsm-cartesian,prop.rhilb-monoidal}), and $\inc$ preserves both
on the nose; morphisms of $\rhilb$ are bounded linear, hence smooth.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The datum and its operad}
\label{sec.datum_operad}

\begin{definition}[Hilbert arrangement datum]\label{def.hilbert-datum}
The \emph{Hilbert arrangement datum} is
\[
\defineTerm{Hb}\coloneqq(\hsm,\rhilb,\inc,\R),
\]
an adaptive-arrangement datum in the sense of
\cite[Def.~4.1.1]{cdlm}:%def.rewiring_datum
$\hsm$ is cartesian monoidal (\cref{prop.hsm-cartesian}), $\rhilb$ is
symmetric monoidal (\cref{prop.rhilb-monoidal}), $\inc$ is strong symmetric
monoidal (\cref{prop.rhilb-forgetful}), and $\R$ abbreviates the writer
potentials monad $\R\oplus\blank$ (\cref{sec.hilbert_spaces}).  We write
$\defineTerm{harr}$ for its adaptive-arrangement operad.
\end{definition}

We unwind what \cite[Def.~4.1.1]{cdlm}%def.rewiring_datum
provides.  An object of $\harr$ is an \emph{interface} $\binom{A}{B}$ with
\emph{input space} $A:\hsm$ and \emph{output space} $B:\hsm$; the monoidal
unit is $I\coloneqq\binom{1}{1}$, and the tensor $\ltens$ direct-sums inputs
and outputs.  A morphism
$f\colon\bigl(\binom{A_1}{B_1},\ldots,\binom{A_K}{B_K}\bigr)\to\binom{A}{B}$,
which we call a \emph{Hilbert arrangement}, is a tuple
$f=(\rv Q,\outp f,\inpt f,U)$: a reactive Hilbert space
$\rv Q=(Q,\sharpR_Q)$, the \emph{parameter}, and smooth maps
\begin{equation}\label{eq.harr-morphism}
\begin{gathered}
\outp f\colon Q\oplus B_1\oplus\cdots\oplus B_K\to B,
\qquad
\inpt f\colon Q\oplus B_1\oplus\cdots\oplus B_K\oplus A
\to A_1\oplus\cdots\oplus A_K,\\
U\colon Q\oplus B_1\oplus\cdots\oplus B_K\oplus A\to\R,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
the \emph{output map}, the \emph{input map}, and the \emph{potential}.
(Products in $\hsm$ are direct sums, and in the writer instance the
backward pass splits into the input map and the potential.)  Composition
substitutes forward data into forward data and backward data into backward
data, tensors the parameters, and \emph{adds} the potentials of the
constituents after substitution; \cite[\S4.1]{cdlm}%rem.huge_wiring_diagram
draws the composite of two unary arrangements explicitly.

\begin{definition}[Vocabulary]\label{def.vocabulary}
Following \cite[Def.~5.3.3]{cdlm},%def.arrangement_terminology
we say a Hilbert arrangement $f=(\rv Q,\outp f,\inpt f,U)$ is:
\emph{stateless} if $Q=\R^0$; \emph{potential-free} if $U=0$;
\emph{static} if both; \emph{closed} if its codomain is $I$; a
\emph{system} if its arity is $K=0$; a \emph{closed system} if it is a
morphism $I\to I$; and \emph{harmonic} if its reaction $\sharpR_Q$ is
symmetric and its potential $U$ is a continuous quadratic form
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}).
\end{definition}

For a closed system, the source and target contribute nothing:
\cref{eq.harr-morphism} reduces to the parameter $\rv Q$ and the potential
$U\colon Q\to\R$.

\section{Interpretation and integration}
\label{sec.hilbert_semantics}

Following the framework, semantics for $\harr$ takes two further
ingredients: a polynomial interpretation datum
\cite[Def.~4.2.1]{cdlm},%def.polynomial_interpretation
which casts spaces as polynomial interfaces and fixes what potentials do,
and an integrator \cite[Def.~4.3.4]{cdlm},%def.integrator
which turns the resulting effects into dynamics.  We build the Hilbert
instances of both and obtain the phase dynamics functor
$\Phiphase\colon\harr\to\pc$ (\cref{thm.phase-functor}), together with
its lift $\Phih$ to linear dynamics on the closed harmonic systems
(\cref{prop.harmonic-lift}).

\subsection{The cotangent functor}
\label{sec.cot_hilbert}

\begin{definition}[Cotangent functor]\label{def.cot-hilbert}
The \emph{cotangent functor} $\defineTerm{cot}\colon\hsm\to\poly$ sends a
Hilbert space $V$ to
\[
\cotof V\coloneqq\sum_{v\in V}\yon^{T^*_vV},
\]
where $T^*_qV=V^*$ (\cref{sec.background_hilbert}).  It sends a smooth map
$f\colon V\to W$ to the polynomial map $\cotof f\colon\cotof V\to\cotof W$
whose position map is $f$ and whose direction map at $v\in V$ is the
transpose of the Fr\'echet derivative,
$(D_vf)^*\colon T^*_{f(v)}W\to T^*_vV$.
\end{definition}

This is functorial: $D_v(\id_V)=\id_V$, whose transpose is the identity,
and for smooth $f\colon V\to W$, $g\colon W\to X$ the chain rule gives
$D_v(g\circ f)=D_{f(v)}g\circ D_vf$, so
$(D_v(g\circ f))^*=(D_vf)^*\circ(D_{f(v)}g)^*$, which is the direction map
of $\cotof g\circ\cotof f$.  This is the construction of
\cite[Def.~6.1.1]{cdlm}%def.cot
with manifolds replaced by Hilbert spaces, tangent maps by Fr\'echet
derivatives, and dual spaces by continuous duals; note that $\poly$ sees
only the underlying sets of the cotangent spaces.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.cot-monoidal-hilbert}
The cotangent functor
$\cot\colon(\hsm,1,\oplus)\to(\poly,\yon,\otimes)$ is strong symmetric
monoidal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The zero space has $1^*=0$, so $\cotof1=\sum_{\{0\}}\yon^{\{0\}}\cong\yon$.
For Hilbert spaces $V,W$, the identification
$(V\oplus W)^*\cong V^*\oplus W^*$ of \cref{sec.background_hilbert} gives
\[
\cotof{V\oplus W}
=\sum_{(v,w)\in V\oplus W}\yon^{(V\oplus W)^*}
\cong
\Bigl(\sum_{v\in V}\yon^{V^*}\Bigr)\otimes\Bigl(\sum_{w\in W}\yon^{W^*}\Bigr)
=\cotof V\otimes\cotof W
\]
by the definition of the Dirichlet product.  This isomorphism is natural:
for smooth $f\colon V\to V'$ and $g\colon W\to W'$, the product map
$f\oplus g$ has block-diagonal derivative
$D_{(v,w)}(f\oplus g)=D_vf\oplus D_wg$, whose transpose is
$(D_vf)^*\oplus(D_wg)^*$.  Coherence follows from the corresponding
coherence of $(1,\oplus)$ in $\hsm$, as in \cite[Prop.~6.1.2]{cdlm}.%prop.cot_monoidal
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Hilbert polynomial interpretation datum}
\label{sec.hilbert_interpretation}

Recall from \cite[Def.~4.2.1]{cdlm}%def.polynomial_interpretation
that a polynomial interpretation datum for a datum $D$ consists of an
interface functor (strong symmetric monoidal from spaces to $\poly$), a
semantic effect (a monoidal monad on $\poly$), an effect comparison
(a monoidal monad morphism carrying the syntactic effect to the semantic
one), and a potential algebra (a monoid with an algebra structure for the
semantic effect, which applies the effects).  For a writer potentials
monad, the canonical choices of semantic effect and comparison come with
the interface functor: the writer monad of the image of the carrier, and
the strong-monoidality comparison.

For the potential algebra we use the same object as the smooth instance.
The space $\R$ is finite-dimensional and $\R^*\cong\R$ by the standard
pairing, so the polynomial $\cotof\R=\sum_{a\in\R}\yon^{T^*_a\R}$ and its
commutative monoid structure (image of $(\R,0,+)$ under the strong monoidal
$\cot$) are literally those of \cite[\S6.2]{cdlm}.  Hence
\cite[Lem.~6.2.1]{cdlm}%lem.alpha_constant
applies unchanged: the $(\cotof\R\otimes\blank)$-monoid structures on
$\yon$ are exactly the constant covector fields $r\in\R$.  As there, we
take the canonical choice $r\coloneqq+1$ and write
$\defineTerm{potd}\colon\cotof\R\to\yon$ for the corresponding algebra map.  It is the
choice for which potentials contribute their differentials: for a smooth
map $U\colon V\to\R$ with $V:\hsm$, the composite
$\potd\circ\cotof U\colon\cotof V\to\yon$ is trivial on positions, and on
directions at $v\in V$ it selects
\begin{equation}\label{eq.potential-differential}
(D_vU)^*(+1)=dU|_v\in T^*_vV,
\end{equation}
so under the identification of maps $\cotof V\to\yon$ with covector fields
on $V$, it is the differential $dU$.

\begin{definition}[Hilbert polynomial interpretation datum]
\label{def.interp-hilbert}
The \emph{Hilbert polynomial interpretation datum} for $\Hb$ is
\[
\defineTerm{interph}\coloneqq
\bigl(\cot,\ \cotof\R\otimes\blank,\ \thetacomp,\ (\yon,\potd)\bigr),
\]
where $\cot$ is the cotangent functor
(\cref{def.cot-hilbert,prop.cot-monoidal-hilbert}), the semantic effect is
the writer monad of the commutative monoid $\cotof\R$, the comparison
$\defineTerm{theta}\colon\cotof{\R\oplus\blank}\cong\cotof\R\otimes\cotof\blank$ is
induced by the strong monoidality of $\cot$, and the potential algebra is
$(\yon,\potd)$ as in \cref{eq.potential-differential}.
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor.interpretation-functor}
There is a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
\[
\defineTerm{Phip}_{\interph}\colon\harr\longrightarrow\para{\cot}{\poly},
\]
acting on interfaces by
$\Phip\interph\binom{A}{B}=\cotof B\otimes[\cotof A,\yon]$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is \cite[Thm.~4.2.2]{cdlm}%thm.poly_interpretation
applied to the datum $\Hb$ (\cref{def.hilbert-datum}) and the
interpretation datum $\interph$ (\cref{def.interp-hilbert}); it is normal
because the potential-algebra carrier is $\yon$.
\end{proof}

Here $\para{\cot}{\poly}$ denotes the Para construction for the action
$\rv Q\cdot p\coloneqq\cotof Q\otimes p$ of $\rhilb$ on $\poly$: an object
is a polynomial, and a morphism $p\to p'$ is a pair of a reactive Hilbert
space $\rv Q$ and a polynomial map $\cotof Q\otimes p\to p'$.

\subsection{The phase integrator}
\label{sec.phase_integrator}

Recall from \cite[Def.~4.3.4]{cdlm}%def.integrator
that for a strong symmetric monoidal $p\colon\cat Q\to\poly$, a
\emph{$p$-integrator} is a pair $(\Fn S,\fun{dyn})$ of a strong symmetric
monoidal state-space functor $\Fn S\colon\cat Q\to\smsetiso$ and a monoidal
natural transformation $\fun{dyn}\colon\Store\circ\Fn S\Rightarrow p$, and
that every $p$-integrator induces a normal lax symmetric monoidal functor
$\para{p}{\poly}\to\pc$ \cite[Prop.~4.3.5]{cdlm}.%prop.integrator_to_org
We build the phase integrator for $p\coloneqq\cot\circ\inc$, in three
steps: the phase-space endofunctor $T^*$, the Euler step $\chiE$, and the
readout $\nuro$.  Throughout we use the identifications
\[
\cotof V=\lvert V\rvert\,\yon^{V^*}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\cotof{T^*V}=\lvert V\oplus V^*\rvert\,\yon^{V^*\oplus V}
\]
of \cref{sec.background_hilbert}, where $\lvert\blank\rvert$ denotes the
underlying set.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop.TT-hilbert}
The assignments
\[
T^*\rv V\coloneqq\bigl(V\oplus V^*,\ \sharpS_{T^*V}\bigr),
\qquad
T^*\phi\coloneqq\phi\oplus(\phi^*)^{-1},
\]
where the \emph{canonical symplectic sharp} is the bounded map
\begin{equation}\label{eq.symplectic-sharp}
\defineTerm{sharpS}_{T^*V}\colon V^*\oplus V\to V\oplus V^*,
\qquad
(\xi,v)\mapsto(v,-\xi),
\end{equation}
define a strong symmetric monoidal endofunctor $T^*\colon\rhilb\to\rhilb$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first check that $T^*\phi$ is a morphism of $\rhilb$ for every
$\phi\colon\rv V\to\rv W$.  It is a bounded linear isomorphism because
$\phi$ and $(\phi^*)^{-1}$ are.  For sharp-equivariance
\cref{eq.sharp-equivariance} we must show
$T^*\phi\circ\sharpS_{T^*V}\circ(T^*\phi)^*
=\sharpS_{T^*W}$.  Under the identifications of
\cref{sec.background_hilbert}, the transpose of
$T^*\phi=\phi\oplus(\phi^*)^{-1}$ is
$(T^*\phi)^*=\phi^*\oplus\phi^{-1}$ as a map
$W^*\oplus W\to V^*\oplus V$, using reflexivity and
$((\phi^*)^{-1})^*=(\phi^{**})^{-1}=\phi^{-1}$.  So for
$(\eta,w)\in W^*\oplus W$:
\[
(\eta,w)
\;\xmapsto{(T^*\phi)^*}\;
(\phi^*\eta,\ \phi^{-1}w)
\;\xmapsto{\sharpS_{T^*V}}\;
(\phi^{-1}w,\ -\phi^*\eta)
\;\xmapsto{T^*\phi}\;
\bigl(w,\ -\eta\bigr)
=\sharpS_{T^*W}(\eta,w).
\]
Note that the sharp-equivariance of $\phi$ itself was not needed: the
symplectic sharp is the same formula for every reactive Hilbert space, as
in \cite[Prop.~6.3.4]{cdlm}.%prop.TT_endofunctors
Preservation of identities and composites is inherited from $\Hilb$ and
from $(g\circ f)^*=f^*\circ g^*$.

For strong monoidality, the unitor is the identity, since
$T^*\rv 0=0$, and the productor is the coordinate swap
\[
(T^*)_2\colon T^*V\oplus T^*W\To{\cong}T^*(V\oplus W),
\qquad
\bigl((v,\xi),(w,\eta)\bigr)\mapsto\bigl((v,w),(\xi,\eta)\bigr).
\]
By \cref{eq.symplectic-sharp}, the symplectic sharp of $T^*(V\oplus W)$
sends $(\xi,\eta,v,w)\mapsto(v,w,-\xi,-\eta)$; conjugating by $(T^*)_2$
gives $(\xi,v,\eta,w)\mapsto(v,-\xi,w,-\eta)$, which is
$\sharpS_{T^*V}\oplus\sharpS_{T^*W}$, the direct-sum
sharp of \cref{prop.rhilb-monoidal}.  Symmetry and coherence are inherited
from $(\Hilb,0,\oplus)$, as in \cite[Prop.~6.3.5]{cdlm}.%prop.TT_monoidal
\end{proof}

Since every morphism of $\rhilb$ is an isomorphism, the underlying-set
functors $\lvert\blank\rvert$ and $\lvert T^*\blank\rvert$ land in
$\smsetiso$, and both are strong symmetric monoidal:
$\lvert V\oplus W\rvert\cong\lvert V\rvert\times\lvert W\rvert$.

\begin{proposition}[Euler step]\label{prop.euler-step-hilbert}
There is a monoidal natural transformation
$\defineTerm{chi}\colon\Store\circ\lvert\blank\rvert\Rightarrow\cot\circ\inc$ of
functors $\rhilb\to\poly$ whose component
$\chiE_{\rv V}\colon\Store(\lvert V\rvert)\to\cotof V$ is the identity on
positions and, on directions at $v\in V$, is
\[
T^*_vV=V^*\to\lvert V\rvert,
\qquad
\xi\mapsto v+\sharpR_V(\xi).
\]
When $\rv V$ is nondegenerate, $\chiE_{\rv V}$ is an isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both composites send $\rv V$ to a polynomial with position set
$\lvert V\rvert$, so the identity is a valid position map, and the stated
direction maps assemble into a polynomial map
$\Store(\lvert V\rvert)=\lvert V\rvert\,\yon^{\lvert V\rvert}\to
\lvert V\rvert\,\yon^{V^*}$.  For naturality at
$\phi\colon\rv V\to\rv W$, the position squares commute by definition; on
directions at $v\in V$, the two composites
$T^*_{\phi(v)}W\to\lvert V\rvert$ are
\[
\xi\;\xmapsto{\phi^*}\;\phi^*\xi\;\xmapsto{}\;v+\sharpR_V(\phi^*\xi)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\xi\;\xmapsto{}\;\phi(v)+\sharpR_W(\xi)\;\xmapsto{\phi^{-1}}\;
v+\phi^{-1}(\sharpR_W\xi),
\]
along $\chiE_{\rv V}\circ\cotof\phi$ and
$\Store(\phi)\circ\chiE_{\rv W}$ respectively.  These agree if and only if
$\sharpR_V\circ\phi^*=\phi^{-1}\circ\sharpR_W$, which is sharp-equivariance
\cref{eq.sharp-equivariance}.  Monoidality holds because
$\sharpR_{V\oplus W}=\sharpR_V\oplus\sharpR_W$
(\cref{prop.rhilb-monoidal}) and translation acts coordinatewise.  When
$\sharpR_V$ is an isomorphism, so is each direction map, hence so is
$\chiE_{\rv V}$.  This is \cite[Prop.~6.3.2]{cdlm}%prop.rvect_polynomial
with constant reactions and bounded sharps.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Phase readout]\label{prop.nu-hilbert}
There is a monoidal natural transformation
$\defineTerm{nu}\colon\cot\circ\inc\circ\,T^*\Rightarrow\cot\circ\inc$ of functors
$\rhilb\to\poly$ whose component
$\nuro_{\rv V}\colon\cotof{T^*V}\to\cotof V$ has position map the
\emph{presented position}
\begin{equation}\label{eq.presented-position-abstract}
(v,\xi)\mapsto\tilde v\coloneqq v+\sharpR_V(\xi)
\end{equation}
and direction map at $(v,\xi)$ given by
\[
V^*\to V^*\oplus V,
\qquad
\xi'\mapsto\bigl(\xi',\ \sharpR_V(\xi)\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The position map is bounded linear, and the direction maps land in
$V^*\oplus V\cong T^*_{(v,\xi)}(T^*V)$, so the components are polynomial
maps.  For naturality at $\phi\colon\rv V\to\rv W$ we compare
$\cotof\phi\circ\nuro_{\rv V}$ with $\nuro_{\rv W}\circ\cotof{T^*\phi}$.  On
positions, using sharp-equivariance \cref{eq.sharp-equivariance} in the
middle step,
\[
\phi\bigl(v+\sharpR_V\xi\bigr)
=\phi(v)+\phi\bigl(\sharpR_V\xi\bigr)
=\phi(v)+\sharpR_W\bigl((\phi^*)^{-1}\xi\bigr),
\]
which is the presented position of
$T^*\phi(v,\xi)=(\phi v,(\phi^*)^{-1}\xi)$.  On directions at $(v,\xi)$,
take $\xi'\in W^*$.  Along $\cotof\phi\circ\nuro_{\rv V}$ it maps to
$\bigl(\phi^*\xi',\ \sharpR_V\xi\bigr)$.  Along
$\nuro_{\rv W}\circ\cotof{T^*\phi}$ it maps first to
$\bigl(\xi',\sharpR_W(\phi^*)^{-1}\xi\bigr)$ and then, along the transpose
$(T^*\phi)^*=\phi^*\oplus\phi^{-1}$ (proof of \cref{prop.TT-hilbert}), to
$\bigl(\phi^*\xi',\ \phi^{-1}\sharpR_W(\phi^*)^{-1}\xi\bigr)$; and
$\phi^{-1}\circ\sharpR_W\circ(\phi^*)^{-1}=\sharpR_V$ by
\cref{eq.sharp-equivariance}.  Monoidality holds coordinatewise, as in
\cref{prop.euler-step-hilbert}.  This transformation is the composite
readout $\nuro_{\exp,\beta}$ of \cite[Lem.~6.3.16]{cdlm},%lem.combined_update
assembled from the exponential readout \cite[Lem.~6.3.9]{cdlm}%lem.readout_examples
and the kinetic $1$-form \cite[Ex.~6.3.13]{cdlm};%ex.kinetic_one_form
we have verified it directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Phase integrator]\label{def.phase-integrator-hilbert}
The \emph{phase dynamics} is the monoidal natural transformation
\[
\defineTerm{dynphase}\colon
\Store\circ\lvert T^*\blank\rvert
\xRightarrow{\ \chiE_{T^*\blank}\ }
\cot\circ\inc\circ\,T^*
\xRightarrow{\ \nuro\ }
\cot\circ\inc,
\]
the Euler step on phase space (\cref{prop.euler-step-hilbert} whiskered by
the strong monoidal $T^*$ of \cref{prop.TT-hilbert}) followed by the phase
readout (\cref{prop.nu-hilbert}).  The \emph{phase integrator} is the
$(\cot\circ\inc)$-integrator
\[
\phase\coloneqq\bigl(\lvert T^*\blank\rvert,\ \dynphase\bigr)
\]
in the sense of \cite[Def.~4.3.4]{cdlm}.%def.integrator
\end{definition}

We record the resulting update.  At $\rv V$, the direction map of
$\chiE_{T^*\rv V}$ adds the symplectic sharp of the covector assembled by
$\nuro$: by \cref{eq.symplectic-sharp},
$\sharpS_{T^*V}(\xi',\sharpR_V\xi)=(\sharpR_V\xi,\,-\xi')$, so on
directions
\begin{equation}\label{eq.phase-update}
\lvert T^*V\rvert\times V^*\to\lvert T^*V\rvert,
\qquad
\bigl((v,\xi),\xi'\bigr)\mapsto
\bigl(v+\sharpR_V(\xi),\ \xi-\xi'\bigr),
\end{equation}
while the position reported is the presented position
\cref{eq.presented-position-abstract}.

\begin{theorem}[Phase dynamics functor]\label{thm.phase-functor}
There is a normal operad functor
\[
\defineTerm{Phiphase}\coloneqq\Phi_{\interph,\phase}
\colon\harr\longrightarrow\pc,
\]
with
$\Phiphase\binom{A}{B}=\cotof B\otimes[\cotof A,\yon]$ on interfaces and
$\Phiphase(I)\cong\yon$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is \cite[Thm.~4.3.6]{cdlm}%thm.dynamics_functor
applied to the interpretation datum $\interph$
(\cref{def.interp-hilbert}) and the integrator $\phase$
(\cref{def.phase-integrator-hilbert}); normality, hence
$\Phiphase(I)\cong\yon$, holds because the potential-algebra carrier is
$\yon$.
\end{proof}

In particular $\Phiphase$ sends a closed system $f\colon I\to I$ to a
$1$-cell $\yon\to\yon$ in $\pc$, i.e.\ to a discrete dynamical system
(\cref{sec.background_categories}).

\begin{proposition}[Closed systems]\label{prop.closed-step}
Let $f\colon I\to I$ be a closed system in $\harr$ with parameter
$\rv Q=(Q,\sharpR_Q)$ and potential $U\colon Q\to\R$.  Then
$\Phiphase(f)\in\pc(\yon,\yon)$ is the discrete dynamical system with state
set $\lvert T^*Q\rvert$ and transition
\begin{equation}\label{eq.closed-step}
(q,\xi)\longmapsto
\bigl(\tilde q,\ \xi-dU|_{\tilde q}\bigr),
\qquad
\tilde q=q+\sharpR_Q(\xi).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a closed system the interfaces contribute nothing, and the backward
pass of $f$ is the potential alone.  Under $\Phip\interph$ the potential
enters through the potential algebra as the covector field
$\potd\circ\cotof U=dU$ \cref{eq.potential-differential}, evaluated at the
parameter position that the integrator presents; by
\cref{eq.presented-position-abstract} that position is $\tilde q$.  The
integrator then performs the update \cref{eq.phase-update} with incoming
covector $\xi'\coloneqq dU|_{\tilde q}$, giving \cref{eq.closed-step}.
This unwinding is that of \cite[\S6.6, Eq.~(63)]{cdlm},%eqn.closed_update
which depends only on the component formulas of $\chiE$, $\nuro$, and
$\potd$; these are shared verbatim by the Hilbert instance
(\cref{prop.euler-step-hilbert,prop.nu-hilbert,eq.potential-differential}).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The harmonic lift}
\label{sec.harmonic_lift}

\begin{remark}[From syntax to hom-categories]\label{rmk.syntax-to-homs}
An operad supplies operations and substitution, nothing else: $\harr$
has no morphisms \emph{between} arrangements, so a comparison of two
built systems cannot be stated in $\harr$.  It is stated in the
hom-categories of $\pc$, where $\Phiphase$ lands.  This subsection
refines $\pc(\yon,\yon)$ by $\Dyn$ on the closed harmonic systems, and
\cref{sec.harmonic_segments} computes hom-categories at monomial
interfaces together with their composition.
\end{remark}

Forgetting the Hilbert structure of an object of $\Dyn$ leaves a set with
an endofunction, i.e.\ a $1$-cell $\yon\to\yon$ in $\pc$
(\cref{sec.background_categories}), and forgetting the linearity of a
morphism leaves a $2$-cell.  This gives a faithful functor
\begin{equation}\label{eq.dyn-forgetful}
\defineTerm{Ufun}\colon\Dyn\longrightarrow\pc(\yon,\yon).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Harmonic lift]\label{prop.harmonic-lift}
Let $f\in\harr(I;I)$ be a closed harmonic system with parameter
$\rv Q=(Q,\sharpR_Q)$ and potential $U$, and let
$A\coloneqq\riesz_Q\circ dU\colon Q\to Q$ be the operator of $U$
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}).  Then
\[
\defineTerm{Phih}(f)\coloneqq\bigl(T^*Q,\ F_f\bigr):\Dyn,
\qquad
F_f(q,\xi)\coloneqq\bigl(\tilde q,\ \xi-\riesz_Q^{-1}(A\tilde q)\bigr),
\quad
\tilde q\coloneqq q+\sharpR_Q(\xi),
\]
satisfies $\Ufun\bigl(\Phih(f)\bigr)=\Phiphase(f)$ and
$\Phih(f\ltens f')=\Phih(f)\oplus\Phih(f')$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Here $\tilde q$ is the presented position
\cref{eq.presented-position-abstract} of the phase integrator at
$(q,\xi)$.  Both components of $F_f$ are composites of the bounded
linear maps $\sharpR_Q$, $A$, $\riesz_Q^{-1}$, identities, and sums, so
$F_f$ is a bounded endomorphism of $T^*Q$ and $(T^*Q,F_f):\Dyn$.

By \cref{prop.closed-step}, $\Phiphase(f)$ is the discrete dynamical
system with state set $\lvert T^*Q\rvert$ and transition
$(q,\xi)\mapsto(\tilde q,\ \xi-dU|_{\tilde q})$ \cref{eq.closed-step}.
Since $A=\riesz_Q\circ dU$, we have
$dU|_{\tilde q}=\riesz_Q^{-1}(A\tilde q)$, so this transition is the
underlying function of $F_f$; hence
$\Ufun(\Phih(f))=\Phiphase(f)$.

For the last claim, let $f'$ be closed harmonic with parameter
$\rv Q'=(Q',\sharpR_{Q'})$ and potential $U'$.  The tensor $f\ltens f'$
is the closed system with parameter $\rv Q\oplus\rv Q'$ and potential
$U''\colon Q\oplus Q'\to\R$, $U''(q,q')=U(q)+U'(q')$
(\cref{sec.datum_operad}).  It is harmonic:
its sharp is the symmetric $\sharpR_Q\oplus\sharpR_{Q'}$
(\cref{prop.rhilb-monoidal}), and $U''$ is the continuous quadratic
form of the bounded symmetric bilinear map
$B\oplus B'$, with operator $A''=A\oplus A'$.  Under the coordinate
identification $T^*(Q\oplus Q')\cong T^*Q\oplus T^*Q'$ of
\cref{prop.TT-hilbert}, both components of $F_{f\ltens f'}$ therefore
act summandwise, so $F_{f\ltens f'}=F_f\oplus F_{f'}$.
\end{proof}

Harmonicity is what makes the lift exist: for a continuous quadratic
potential, the differential $dU|_q=B(q,\blank)$ is bounded linear in $q$
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}), and this is the linearity of $F_f$ used
above.  For a general smooth potential, the closed phase step
\cref{eq.closed-step} is merely a smooth self-map of
$\lvert T^*Q\rvert$: still an object of $\pc(\yon,\yon)$, but with no
reason to lie in the image of $\Ufun$.

\subsection{Step scaling}
\label{sec.step_scaling}

The phase integrator takes one unit-size step per tick of $\pc$-time.  The
step size is not a parameter of the functor $\Phiphase$; instead it is a
rescaling of the syntax.

\begin{definition}[Step scaling]\label{def.scale}
For $\Delta\tau\in\R_{>0}$, define
$\defineTerm{scale}_{\Delta\tau}\colon\harr\to\harr$ to be the identity on
interfaces and, on a Hilbert arrangement
$f=\bigl((Q,\sharpR_Q),\outp f,\inpt f,U\bigr)$,
\[
\scale_{\Delta\tau}(f)\coloneqq
\bigl((Q,\Delta\tau\,\sharpR_Q),\ \outp f,\ \inpt f,\ \Delta\tau\,U\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem.scale-functor}
$\scale_{\Delta\tau}$ is an operad endofunctor of $\harr$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A bounded isomorphism $\phi$ satisfies sharp-equivariance
\cref{eq.sharp-equivariance} for $\sharpR$ if and only if it does for
$\Delta\tau\,\sharpR$, so $(Q,\sharpR_Q)\mapsto(Q,\Delta\tau\,\sharpR_Q)$ is
well defined and preserves the direct-sum reactions of
\cref{prop.rhilb-monoidal}.  Identity arrangements are static, hence fixed.
For composition and tensor: composites and tensors of arrangements have
parameter the tensor of the parameters, forward and input data obtained
from the constituents' forward and input data by substitution, and
potential the \emph{sum} of the constituents' potentials precomposed with
(unscaled) forward data (\cref{sec.datum_operad}).  Scaling every
constituent potential by $\Delta\tau$ therefore scales the composite
potential by $\Delta\tau$, and scaling every reaction scales the tensored
reaction, so $\scale_{\Delta\tau}$ preserves composition, tensor, and
identities.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Execution at time step $\Delta\tau$]\label{rmk.scale-execution}
For a closed system $f$ with parameter $(Q,\sharpR_Q)$ and potential $U$,
\cref{prop.closed-step} gives
\[
\Phiphase\bigl(\scale_{\Delta\tau}f\bigr)\colon
(q,\xi)\longmapsto
\bigl(q+\Delta\tau\,\sharpR_Q(\xi),\ \xi-\Delta\tau\,dU|_{\tilde q}\bigr),
\qquad
\tilde q=q+\Delta\tau\,\sharpR_Q(\xi),
\]
the size-$\Delta\tau$ symplectic Euler step of the Hamiltonian vector field
of reaction $\sharpR_Q$ and potential $U$.  One tick of $\pc$-time thus
represents $\Delta\tau$ units of physical time.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Reciprocal scaling is invisible]\label{rmk.reciprocal-scaling}
One might instead expect the reaction and the potential to scale by
reciprocal factors.  The reciprocal variant of \cref{def.scale}---sending
$(Q,\sharpR_Q)$ to $(Q,\Delta\tau\,\sharpR_Q)$ but $U$ to
$U/\Delta\tau$---changes nothing.  Indeed, let $f$ be a closed system and
$g$ its reciprocal scaling, and let
$\Lambda\colon T^*Q\to T^*Q$ be the bounded isomorphism
$\Lambda(q,\xi)\coloneqq(q,\xi/\Delta\tau)$.  Writing
$\tilde q=q+\sharpR_Q(\xi)$ and using \cref{eq.closed-step} for both
systems,
\[
\Phiphase(g)\bigl(\Lambda(q,\xi)\bigr)
=
\Bigl(
q+\Delta\tau\,\sharpR_Q\bigl(\tfrac{\xi}{\Delta\tau}\bigr),\
\tfrac{\xi}{\Delta\tau}-\tfrac{1}{\Delta\tau}\,dU|_{\tilde q}
\Bigr)
=
\Lambda\bigl(\Phiphase(f)(q,\xi)\bigr),
\]
so $\Lambda$ is an isomorphism
$\Phiphase(f)\cong\Phiphase(g)$ in $\pc(\yon,\yon)$---and an isomorphism
$\Phih(f)\cong\Phih(g)$ in $\Dyn$ when $f$ is harmonic
(\cref{prop.harmonic-lift}).  Reciprocal scaling merely rescales the
unit of momentum.
The two combinations separate cleanly in the harmonic chain of
\cref{sec.harmonic}: the product of the reaction coefficient
$\tfrac1{Zh}$ and the stiffness coefficient $\tfrac Zh$ is $1/h^2$, the
clock that $\scale_{\Delta\tau}$ moves
(\cref{rmk.characteristic-tuning}), while their ratio is $Z^2$, the
impedance that reciprocal scaling moves.  Both the reaction and the
potential enter the step as rates, and elapsed time multiplies rates;
that is why $\scale_{\Delta\tau}$ scales both by the same factor.
\end{remark}

\section{Harmonic chains and characteristic factorization}
\label{sec.harmonic}

\subsection{Masses, bonds, and chains}
\label{sec.mass_bond}

Fix an impedance $Z\in\R_{>0}$ and a cell duration $h\in\R_{>0}$.

\begin{definition}[Mass and bond]\label{def.mass-bond}
The \emph{mass} is the system
$\defineTerm{mass}_{Z,h}\colon I\to\binom{1}{\R}$ with parameter
$(\R,\sharpR_{Z,h})$, output map $q\mapsto q$, and zero potential, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq.mass-reaction}
\sharpR_{Z,h}\colon\R^*\to\R,
\qquad
\sharpR_{Z,h}(\eta)\coloneqq\frac{\eta}{Zh},
\end{equation}
with $\R^*\cong\R$ by the standard pairing.  The \emph{bond} is the
stateless system
$\defineTerm{bond}_{Z,h}\colon I\to\binom{\R^2}{1}$ with potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq.bond-potential}
U_{Z,h}\colon\R^2\to\R,
\qquad
U_{Z,h}(a,b)\coloneqq\frac{Z}{2h}(a-b)^2.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Thus the cell mass is $Zh$ and the bond stiffness is $Z/h$.  The mass
outputs its state and reacts to incoming covectors; the bond is pure
potential, reading the pair delivered to its input port.  Both are
harmonic (\cref{def.vocabulary}).

\begin{definition}[Graph wiring]\label{def.wire}
A \emph{finite graph} $\Gamma$ is a pair of finite sets $V$ of
\emph{vertices} and $E$ of \emph{edges} with endpoint maps
$\partial_0,\partial_1\colon E\to V\sqcup\{\star\}$, where $\star$ is the
\emph{wall}.  The \emph{graph wiring} of $\Gamma$ is the static
arrangement
\[
\defineTerm{wire}_\Gamma\colon
\binom{1}{\R}^{\ltens V}\ltens\binom{\R^2}{1}^{\ltens E}
\longrightarrow I
\]
whose output map is the unique map to $1$, whose potential is zero, and
whose input map
\[
\R^V\longrightarrow(\R^2)^{E},
\qquad
q\longmapsto
\bigl(q_{\partial_0e},\,q_{\partial_1e}\bigr)_{e\in E},
\qquad
q_\star\coloneqq0,
\]
feeds each bond the outputs of the masses at its endpoints, the wall
outputting $0$.  (The mass inputs and bond outputs are trivial, and the
input map is bounded linear, hence smooth.)
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Incidence operator]\label{def.incidence}
The \emph{incidence operator} of a finite graph $\Gamma$ is
\[
d_\Gamma\colon\ell^2(V)\to\ell^2(E),
\qquad
(d_\Gamma q)_e\coloneqq q_{\partial_0e}-q_{\partial_1e},
\qquad
q_\star\coloneqq0.
\]
Reversing an edge's endpoints changes the sign of the corresponding
component, so $\lVert d_\Gamma q\rVert$ is independent of orientation.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Graph chains]\label{prop.graph-chain}
For every finite graph $\Gamma$, the composite
\[
\defineTerm{chain}_\Gamma
\coloneqq
\Wire_\Gamma\bigl(
\Mass_{Z,h},\ldots,\Mass_{Z,h},\
\Bond_{Z,h},\ldots,\Bond_{Z,h}
\bigr)
\in\harr(I;I),
\]
one mass per vertex and one bond per edge, is the closed harmonic system
with parameter $\bigl(\ell^2(V),\ \riesz/(Zh)\bigr)$ and potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq.graph-potential}
U_\Gamma(q)=\frac{Z}{2h}\,\lVert d_\Gamma q\rVert^2,
\end{equation}
whose operator is
$\frac Zh\,d_\Gamma^\dagger d_\Gamma\colon\ell^2(V)\to\ell^2(V)$---the
graph Laplacian of $\Gamma$, scaled by $\frac Zh$.  The wiring diagram
is the graph.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We unwind the composition described in \cref{sec.datum_operad}.  The
tensor of the masses and bonds is a system
$I\to\binom{1}{\R}^{\ltens V}\ltens\binom{\R^2}{1}^{\ltens E}$ with
parameter the direct sum of the constituents' parameters, which is
$(\R^V,\sharpR_{Z,h}^{\oplus V})=(\ell^2(V),\riesz/(Zh))$ by
\cref{prop.rhilb-monoidal,eq.mass-reaction} since the bonds are
stateless, and with potential the sum of the constituents' potentials:
the masses contribute zero and the bond at $e$ contributes
$U_{Z,h}(a_e,b_e)$ \cref{eq.bond-potential} as a function of the pair
$(a_e,b_e)$ arriving at its input port.  Composing with $\Wire_\Gamma$
tensors on the trivial parameter, adds the zero potential of the wiring,
and substitutes the wiring's input map into the backward data: by
\cref{def.wire} the bond at $e$ receives
$(a_e,b_e)=(q_{\partial_0e},q_{\partial_1e})$, where $q\in\R^V$ is the
tuple of mass outputs, i.e.\ the parameter states of the masses, and
$q_\star=0$.  The total potential is therefore
\[
U_\Gamma(q)
=\sum_{e\in E}\frac{Z}{2h}\bigl(q_{\partial_0e}-q_{\partial_1e}\bigr)^2
=\frac{Z}{2h}\lVert d_\Gamma q\rVert^2.
\]
The reaction is symmetric, and $U_\Gamma$ is the continuous quadratic
form of the bounded symmetric bilinear map
$(q,q')\mapsto\frac Zh\langle d_\Gamma q,d_\Gamma q'\rangle$, with
operator $\frac Zh\,d_\Gamma^\dagger d_\Gamma$
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}); so the system is harmonic.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite chains]\label{prop.chain}
Let $P_K$ ($K\geq2$) be the path graph: vertices $\{1,\ldots,K\}$ and
edges $\{1,\ldots,K-1\}$ with $\partial_0i=i$ and $\partial_1i=i+1$.
Then $\Chain_K^{Z,h}\coloneqq\Chain_{P_K}$ is the closed harmonic system
with parameter $\bigl(\R^K,\sharpR_{Z,h}^{\oplus K}\bigr)$ and potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq.chain-potential}
U(q_1,\ldots,q_K)=\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}\frac{Z}{2h}(q_i-q_{i+1})^2.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is \cref{prop.graph-chain} at $\Gamma=P_K$; it is the computation
of \cite[\S7.3]{cdlm}%sec.wave_equation
in the Hilbert instance.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Clamped chains]\label{prop.clamped-chain}
Let $P^{\mathrm{cl}}_K$ ($K\geq2$) be the path graph with wall ends:
$P_K$ with two further edges $e_0,e_K$, where $\partial_0e_0=1$,
$\partial_0e_K=K$, and $\partial_1e_0=\partial_1e_K=\star$.  Then
$\Chain^{\mathrm{cl}}_K\coloneqq\Chain_{P^{\mathrm{cl}}_K}$ is the
closed harmonic system with parameter
$\bigl(\R^K,\sharpR_{Z,h}^{\oplus K}\bigr)$ and potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq.clamped-potential}
U^{\mathrm{cl}}(q_1,\ldots,q_K)
=
\frac{Z}{2h}
\Bigl(
q_1^2+\sum_{i=1}^{K-1}(q_i-q_{i+1})^2+q_K^2
\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is \cref{prop.graph-chain} at $\Gamma=P^{\mathrm{cl}}_K$: the wall
edges contribute $(q_1-0)^2$ and $(q_K-0)^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The harmonic system of a unitary representation}
\label{sec.unitary_harmonic}

\begin{definition}[Harmonic system of a representation]\label{def.lat-rep}
Let $(X,u)$ be a unitary representation of $\Z$
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}).  Define the closed system
\[
\defineTerm{lat}_{Z,h}(X,u)\in\harr(I;I)
\]
with parameter $\bigl(X,\ \riesz_X/(Zh)\bigr)$ and potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq.rep-potential}
U_u\colon X\to\R,
\qquad
U_u(q)\coloneqq\frac{Z}{2h}\,\lVert D_uq\rVert^2.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The reaction $\riesz_X/(Zh)$ is symmetric, and $U_u$ is the continuous
quadratic form of the bounded symmetric bilinear map
$(q,q')\mapsto\frac Zh\langle D_uq,D_uq'\rangle$, with operator
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert})
\begin{equation}\label{eq.rep-operator}
A_u=\frac Zh\,D_u^\dagger D_u
=\frac Zh\,\bigl(2\,\id_X-u-u^{-1}\bigr)
\colon X\to X.
\end{equation}
So $\Lat_{Z,h}(X,u)$ is a closed harmonic system, and the harmonic lift
(\cref{prop.harmonic-lift}) applies to it.

\begin{definition}[The harmonic lattice]\label{def.lattice}
The \emph{harmonic lattice} is the shift instance
\[
\Lat_{Z,h}\coloneqq\Lat_{Z,h}\bigl(\ell^2(\Z),\ \sigma^{\leftarrow}\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

Writing $Q\coloneqq\ell^2(\Z)$: the parameter of $\Lat_{Z,h}$ is
$\bigl(Q,\ \riesz_Q/(Zh)\bigr)$---in components
$(\sharpR\xi)_i=\xi_i/(Zh)$---and, since $(Dq)_i=q_i-q_{i-1}$, the
potential $U_{\Lat}\coloneqq U_{\sigma^{\leftarrow}}$ and its
differential are
\begin{equation}\label{eq.lattice-potential}
U_{\Lat}(q)
=
\frac{Z}{2h}\sum_{i\in\Z}(q_i-q_{i-1})^2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq.lattice-covector}
\bigl(dU_{\Lat}|_q\bigr)_i
=
\frac{Z}{h}
\bigl(
2q_i-q_{i-1}-q_{i+1}
\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Finite arity]\label{rmk.finite-arity}
Operations of an operad have finite arity, so there is no countably-ary
wiring in $\harr$ placing one mass at every $i\in\Z$, and the lattice is
\emph{not} an operadic composite of masses and bonds.  It is a single
closed arrangement, defined directly by its parameter and potential.

Nor is finite arity a restriction one could simply relax.  A
countably-ary operad of arrangements would need countable tensors of
interfaces, and the candidate tensor---the $\ell^2$-direct sum
$\widehat{\bigoplus}_{i\in\Z}B_i$, whose elements are the tuples with
$\sum_i\lVert b_i\rVert^2<\infty$---is not cartesian: pairing countably
many bounded maps requires square-summability of their values, and there
is no diagonal $B\to\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\N}B$, so $\Hilb$ has no
countable products.  Moreover the underlying set of
$\widehat{\bigoplus}_iB_i$ is a proper subset of the product
$\prod_i\lvert B_i\rvert$, so the semantics of a countable tensor could
not be the countable parallel product of the semantics---the state-space
functor $\lvert T^*\blank\rvert$ would fail its monoidality exactly at
arity $\omega$.  The discrepancy between the $\ell^2$-sum and the product
is the finite-energy condition itself: the lattice's states form a proper
subspace of $\R^\Z$ precisely because their energies are summable.

The sense in which the lattice is assembled from masses and bonds is
therefore a colimit rather than a composite:
\cref{thm.lattice-colimit} exhibits $\Lat_{Z,h}$ as the colimit, in the
category $\Harm$ of closed harmonic systems, of the clamped
chains---which \emph{are} operadic composites
(\cref{prop.clamped-chain})---and \cref{prop.chain-limit} shows that
the phase semantics converges along with the syntax.
\end{remark}

Not every instance of \cref{def.lat-rep} lies beyond the operad's reach:
the cyclic representations are realized by operadic composites.

\begin{corollary}[Cyclic chains]\label{prop.cyclic-chain}
Let $C_K$ ($K\geq1$) be the cycle graph: vertices $\Z/K$ and edges
$\Z/K$ with $\partial_0i=i$ and $\partial_1i=i-1$.  Then, with cyclic
shift $(\sigma^{\leftarrow}a)_i=a_{i+1}$ on $\ell^2(\Z/K)$,
\[
\Chain_{C_K}
=
\Lat_{Z,h}\bigl(\ell^2(\Z/K),\ \sigma^{\leftarrow}\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop.graph-chain}, both sides are the closed system whose
parameter is $(\ell^2(\Z/K),\riesz/(Zh))$ and whose potential is
\[
\frac{Z}{2h}\sum_{i\in\Z/K}(q_i-q_{i-1})^2
=\frac{Z}{2h}\lVert D_{\sigma^{\leftarrow}}q\rVert^2
=U_{\sigma^{\leftarrow}}(q),
\]
and for a closed system the parameter and the potential are the whole
datum (\cref{sec.datum_operad}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Incidence and difference]\label{rmk.incidence-difference}
The incidence operator generalizes the difference operator.  For the
cycle $C_K$, the endpoint map $\partial_0\colon E\To{\cong}V$ is a
bijection, and the induced unitary $\ell^2(V)\To{\cong}\ell^2(E)$
carries $D_{\sigma^{\leftarrow}}$ to $d_{C_K}$; likewise---though
outside the operad---the lattice potential \cref{eq.lattice-potential}
is $\frac{Z}{2h}\lVert Dq\rVert^2$ with $D$ the incidence operator of
the biinfinite path.  For a general graph no such identification
exists: $d_\Gamma\colon\ell^2(V)\to\ell^2(E)$ has genuinely different
domain and codomain, and the identity
$D_u-D_u^\dagger D_u=u-\id$ behind the characteristic factorization
(\cref{thm.characteristic-factorization}) is unavailable.  The
factorization into characteristics is exactly the presence of this
identification---the one-dimensionality of the medium.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Discrete linear dynamics}
\label{sec.dyn}

For a trajectory $x\colon\N\to X$ in a Hilbert space $X$---a sequence
with $x(t+1)=F(x(t))$ for the transition $F$ at hand---we write,
following \cite[\S7.3]{cdlm},%eqn.doubledot
\[
\dot x(t)\coloneqq x(t+1)-x(t)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\ddot x(t)\coloneqq x(t+1)-2x(t)+x(t-1)
\quad(t\geq1)
\]
for the discrete velocity and the discrete acceleration; note
$\ddot x(t)=\dot x(t)-\dot x(t-1)$.

\begin{proposition}[Characteristic recurrence]\label{prop.recurrence}
Let $(X,u)$ be a unitary representation of $\Z$ and $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$, and
put
\begin{equation}\label{eq.rep-transition}
(T^*X,\ F_u)\coloneqq\Phih\bigl(\scale_h\,\Lat_{Z,h}(X,u)\bigr),
\end{equation}
suppressing $Z,h$ from the notation $F_u$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq.rep-transition-formula}
F_u(q,\xi)
=\bigl(\widetilde q,\ \xi-Z\,\riesz_X^{-1}(D_u^\dagger D_u\widetilde q)\bigr),
\qquad
\widetilde q=q+\riesz_X(\xi)/Z,
\end{equation}
and every trajectory of $F_u$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq.momentum-position}
\riesz_X\bigl(\xi(t)\bigr)=Z\,\dot q(t)
\end{equation}
and the discrete wave equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq.discrete-wave}
\ddot q(t)=-D_u^\dagger D_u\,q(t)
\qquad(t\geq1).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The arrangement $\scale_h\,\Lat_{Z,h}(X,u)$ is the closed system with
reaction $h\cdot\riesz_X/(Zh)=\riesz_X/Z$ and potential $h\,U_u$
(\cref{def.scale,def.lat-rep}), whose operator is
$h\,A_u=Z\,D_u^\dagger D_u$ \cref{eq.rep-operator}.  Substituting this
reaction and this operator into the harmonic lift
(\cref{prop.harmonic-lift}) gives \cref{eq.rep-transition-formula}, in
which $\widetilde q$ is the presented position.

Let $(q(t),\xi(t))_{t\in\N}$ be a trajectory.  The position component of
\cref{eq.rep-transition-formula} gives
$q(t+1)=\widetilde q(t)=q(t)+\riesz_X(\xi(t))/Z$, which is
\cref{eq.momentum-position}.  The momentum component of
\cref{eq.rep-transition-formula} at time $t-1$, together with
$\widetilde q(t-1)=q(t)$, gives
$\riesz_X(\xi(t))-\riesz_X(\xi(t-1))=-Z\,D_u^\dagger D_u\,q(t)$.
Substituting \cref{eq.momentum-position} at times $t$ and $t-1$ on the
left and dividing by $Z$ gives
$\dot q(t)-\dot q(t-1)=-D_u^\dagger D_u\,q(t)$, which is
\cref{eq.discrete-wave}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Phase semantics of the lattice]\label{prop.phase-semantics}
Put $Q\coloneqq\ell^2(\Z)$ and
$(T^*Q,\ F_{Z,h})\coloneqq\Phih\bigl(\scale_h\,\Lat_{Z,h}\bigr)$.  Then
$\Phiphase\bigl(\scale_h\,\Lat_{Z,h}\bigr)=\Ufun(T^*Q,F_{Z,h})$; in
components,
\begin{equation}\label{eq.phase-transition}
F_{Z,h}(q,\xi)
=
\Bigl(
\bigl(q_i+\tfrac{\xi_i}{Z}\bigr)_{i\in\Z},\
\bigl(\xi_i+Z(\widetilde q_{i-1}-2\widetilde q_i+\widetilde q_{i+1})
\bigr)_{i\in\Z}
\Bigr),
\qquad
\widetilde q=q+\riesz_Q(\xi)/Z,
\end{equation}
and every trajectory satisfies
$\ddot q_i(t)=q_{i-1}(t)-2q_i(t)+q_{i+1}(t)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is \cref{prop.recurrence} at the shift representation
$(\ell^2(\Z),\sigma^{\leftarrow})$ of \cref{def.lattice}, together with
$\Ufun\circ\Phih=\Phiphase$ on closed harmonic systems
(\cref{prop.harmonic-lift}).  For the components, use
$(D^\dagger Da)_i=(Da)_i-(Da)_{i+1}=2a_i-a_{i-1}-a_{i+1}$: the momentum
update of \cref{eq.rep-transition-formula} has $i$th component
$\xi_i-Z(2\widetilde q_i-\widetilde q_{i-1}-\widetilde q_{i+1})$, and
\cref{eq.discrete-wave} reads componentwise as stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Characteristic tuning]\label{rmk.characteristic-tuning}
For a general step $\Delta\tau\in\R_{>0}$, the computation of
\cref{prop.recurrence} applied to
$\scale_{\Delta\tau}\Lat_{Z,h}(X,u)$ gives
$\riesz_X(\xi(t))=\tfrac{Zh}{\Delta\tau}\,\dot q(t)$ and hence
\[
\ddot q(t)
=
-\Bigl(\frac{\Delta\tau}{h}\Bigr)^{2}D_u^\dagger D_u\,q(t):
\]
the coefficient $(\Delta\tau/h)^2$ multiplies the spatial second
difference.  The \emph{characteristic tuning} is $\Delta\tau=h$, at which
\cref{eq.discrete-wave} holds; we use it throughout.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The lattice as a colimit of chains}
\label{sec.lattice_colimit}

\begin{definition}[The category of closed harmonic systems]\label{def.harm}
The category $\defineTerm{harmcat}$ has as objects the closed harmonic
systems of $\harr(I;I)$ and as morphisms
$(\rv Q,U)\to(\rv Q',U')$ the isometries $\iota\colon Q\to Q'$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq.harm-morphism}
U'\circ\iota=U
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sharpR_Q=\iota^\dagger\circ\sharpR_{Q'}\circ(\iota^\dagger)^*,
\end{equation}
with composition that of $\Hilb$.
\end{definition}

Composites of such isometries again satisfy \cref{eq.harm-morphism}, so
the definition is valid.  For unitary $\iota$ the sharp condition is
sharp-equivariance \cref{eq.sharp-equivariance}, so the isomorphisms of
$\Harm$ are the potential-preserving unitary isomorphisms of $\rhilb$.
When both sharps are multiples $c\,\riesz$ of the Riesz maps---as for
every closed system in this paper---the sharp condition holds for
\emph{every} isometry: by the Riesz identities
\cref{eq.riesz-identities},
$\iota^\dagger\circ(c\,\riesz_{Q'})\circ(\iota^\dagger)^*
=c\,\iota^\dagger\iota\riesz_Q=c\,\riesz_Q$.  Finally,
$\scale_{\Delta\tau}$ (\cref{def.scale}) scales the potentials and the
sharps on both sides of \cref{eq.harm-morphism} by $\Delta\tau$, so it
lifts to an endofunctor of $\Harm$ that is the identity on morphisms.

\begin{lemma}[The lift on isomorphisms]\label{lem.lift-iso}
An isomorphism $\phi\colon f\To{\cong}f'$ in $\Harm$ is unitary, and
\[
T^*\phi=\phi\oplus(\phi^*)^{-1}
\colon\Phih(f)\To{\cong}\Phih(f')
\]
is an isomorphism in $\Dyn$; the assignment $\phi\mapsto T^*\phi$ makes
$\Phih$ a functor on the groupoid of $\Harm$-isomorphisms.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An invertible isometry is unitary.  Write $f=(\rv Q,U)$ and
$f'=(\rv Q',U')$, with operators $A,A'$ of the potentials.  Since
$\phi$ is unitary, the sharp condition of \cref{eq.harm-morphism} is
sharp-equivariance: $\sharpR_{Q'}=\phi\circ\sharpR_Q\circ\phi^*$.
Differentiating $U'\circ\phi=U$ at $q\in Q$ gives
$dU'|_{\phi q}\circ\phi=dU|_q$, i.e.\
$dU'|_{\phi q}=dU|_q\circ\phi^\dagger=(\phi^\dagger)^*(dU|_q)$, so by
the second Riesz identity \cref{eq.riesz-identities}
\[
A'(\phi q)
=\riesz_{Q'}\bigl((\phi^\dagger)^*(dU|_q)\bigr)
=\phi\bigl(\riesz_Q(dU|_q)\bigr)
=\phi(Aq).
\]
Now compare $T^*\phi\circ F_f$ with $F_{f'}\circ T^*\phi$ at
$(q,\xi)\in T^*Q$, writing $\tilde q=q+\sharpR_Q(\xi)$.  On positions,
\[
\phi q+\sharpR_{Q'}\bigl((\phi^*)^{-1}\xi\bigr)
=\phi q+\phi\,\sharpR_Q\,\phi^*(\phi^*)^{-1}\xi
=\phi(\tilde q).
\]
On momenta, note that for unitary $\phi$ one has
$(\phi^\dagger)^*=(\phi^*)^{-1}$, so inverting the second Riesz
identity gives
$\riesz_{Q'}^{-1}\circ\phi=(\phi^*)^{-1}\circ\riesz_Q^{-1}$; hence
\[
(\phi^*)^{-1}\xi-\riesz_{Q'}^{-1}\bigl(A'\phi\tilde q\bigr)
=(\phi^*)^{-1}\xi-\riesz_{Q'}^{-1}\bigl(\phi A\tilde q\bigr)
=(\phi^*)^{-1}\bigl(\xi-\riesz_Q^{-1}(A\tilde q)\bigr).
\]
So $T^*\phi$ intertwines the transitions.  It is invertible with
inverse $T^*(\phi^{-1})$, and functoriality is that of the endofunctor
$T^*$ (\cref{prop.TT-hilbert}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Functoriality in the representation]
\label{prop.lat-functor}
The assignment $(X,u)\mapsto\Lat_{Z,h}(X,u)$, $\phi\mapsto\phi$, is a
functor
\[
\Lat_{Z,h}(\blank)\colon\mathrm U(\Z)\longrightarrow\Harm
\]
sending every morphism to an isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\phi\colon(X,u)\to(Y,v)$ be a unitary intertwiner.  It is an
isometry; the sharp condition of \cref{eq.harm-morphism} holds because
both sharps are the multiple $\riesz/(Zh)$ of the Riesz maps; and the
potentials \cref{eq.rep-potential} agree because $D$ is natural
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}) and $\phi$ isometric:
\[
U_v(\phi q)
=\frac{Z}{2h}\lVert D_v\phi q\rVert^2
=\frac{Z}{2h}\lVert\phi D_uq\rVert^2
=\frac{Z}{2h}\lVert D_uq\rVert^2
=U_u(q).
\]
Composition and identities are those of $\Hilb$, and every $\phi$ is
invertible with inverse again a unitary intertwiner, so its image is an
isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The lattice is a colimit of clamped chains]
\label{thm.lattice-colimit}
Fix $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$.  For $n\geq1$ put $\Lambda_n\coloneqq\{-n,\ldots,n\}$
and write $\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n}$ for $\Chain^{\mathrm{cl}}_{2n+1}$
(\cref{prop.clamped-chain}) with its sites relabelled by $\Lambda_n$.
The extensions by zero
$\rho_n\colon\ell^2(\Lambda_n)\hookrightarrow\ell^2(\Lambda_{n+1})$ and
$\iota_n\colon\ell^2(\Lambda_n)\hookrightarrow\ell^2(\Z)$ are morphisms
\[
\rho_n\colon\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n}\to\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n+1},
\qquad
\iota_n\colon\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n}\to\Lat_{Z,h}
\quad\text{in }\Harm,
\]
and the $\iota_n$ exhibit $\Lat_{Z,h}$ as the colimit
$\operatorname{colim}_n\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n}$: for every $g\in\Harm$,
composition with them is a bijection
\begin{equation}\label{eq.lattice-colimit-up}
\Harm\bigl(\Lat_{Z,h},\ g\bigr)
\cong
\bigl\{
(f_n)_{n\geq1}
\ \big|\
f_n\colon\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n}\to g\text{ in }\Harm,\
f_{n+1}\circ\rho_n=f_n
\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All sharps in sight are multiples of Riesz maps, so the sharp condition
of \cref{eq.harm-morphism} holds for every isometry among these systems,
and only the potential conditions need checking.  For $\rho_n$:
evaluating $U^{\mathrm{cl}}_{n+1}$ \cref{eq.clamped-potential} at an
extension by zero, the wall terms at $\pm(n+1)$ vanish, the bonds from
$\pm n$ to $\pm(n+1)$ contribute $\frac{Z}{2h}q_{\pm n}^2$---the wall
terms of $U^{\mathrm{cl}}_n$---and the interior bonds match; the total
is $U^{\mathrm{cl}}_n(q)$.  For $\iota_n$: evaluating $U_{\Lat}$
\cref{eq.lattice-potential} at an extension by zero, all summands with
$\lvert i\rvert>n+1$ vanish, the summands at $i=\pm n$ or $i=n+1$
adjacent to the window boundary contribute $\frac{Z}{2h}q_{\pm n}^2$,
and the interior summands match; the total is again
$U^{\mathrm{cl}}_n(q)$.

For the bijection, note first that $\ell^2(\Z)$ is the completed
increasing union of the $\ell^2(\Lambda_n)$: the union is the space of
finitely supported sequences, which is dense.  Restriction along the
$\iota_n$ sends a morphism $F\colon\Lat_{Z,h}\to g$ to a compatible
family, and is injective by density.  Conversely, let $(f_n)$ be a
compatible family, with $g=(\rv Y,U_g)$.  Each $f_n$ is an isometry,
hence a contraction, so by \cref{lem.isometric-colimits} the family
extends uniquely to a bounded $F\colon\ell^2(\Z)\to Y$, which is
isometric because it is isometric on a dense subspace.  The sharp
condition again comes for free, and $U_g\circ F$ and $U_{\Lat}$ are
continuous quadratic forms agreeing on the dense union---for
$q\in\ell^2(\Lambda_n)$,
$U_g(F\iota_nq)=U_g(f_nq)=U^{\mathrm{cl}}_n(q)=U_{\Lat}(\iota_nq)$---so
they agree everywhere, and $F$ is a morphism of $\Harm$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Strong continuity of the harmonic lift]
\label{thm.lift-continuity}
Let $f=(\rv Q,U)$ and $f_n=(\rv Q_n,U_n)$ be closed harmonic systems and
let $\iota_n\colon f_n\to f$ be morphisms in $\Harm$ whose images
increase with dense union.  Writing $\Phih(f)=(T^*Q,F_f)$ and
$\Phih(f_n)=(T^*Q_n,F_{f_n})$, put
\[
E_n\coloneqq\iota_n\oplus(\iota_n^\dagger)^*
\colon T^*Q_n\to T^*Q,
\qquad
R_n\coloneqq\iota_n^\dagger\oplus\iota_n^*
\colon T^*Q\to T^*Q_n.
\]
Then $E_n\circ F_{f_n}\circ R_n\to F_f$ strongly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $S\coloneqq\sharpR_Q\circ\riesz_Q^{-1}\colon Q\to Q$ and
$A\coloneqq\riesz_Q\circ dU\colon Q\to Q$ for the operators of the sharp
and the potential of $f$, and $S_n,A_n\colon Q_n\to Q_n$ for those of
$f_n$.  The two conditions
of \cref{eq.harm-morphism} say exactly that $S_n$ and $A_n$ are the
compressions of $S$ and $A$:
\[
S_n=\iota_n^\dagger S\iota_n,
\qquad
A_n=\iota_n^\dagger A\iota_n.
\]
For $A_n$: evaluating $U\circ\iota_n=U_n$ at $q\in Q_n$ gives
$U_n(q)=\tfrac12\langle A\iota_nq,\iota_nq\rangle
=\tfrac12\langle\iota_n^\dagger A\iota_nq,q\rangle$, and
$\iota_n^\dagger A\iota_n$ is self-adjoint.  For $S_n$: by the Riesz
identities \cref{eq.riesz-identities},
$\riesz_Q\circ(\iota_n^\dagger)^*=\iota_n\circ\riesz_{Q_n}$ and
$\riesz_{Q_n}\circ\iota_n^*=\iota_n^\dagger\circ\riesz_Q$, so
conjugating the sharp condition by the Riesz maps gives
$S_n=\iota_n^\dagger S\iota_n$.

Conjugate everything by the unitaries
$\id\oplus\riesz\colon T^*V\to V\oplus V$: the lift transitions become
$\hat F_f\colon Q\oplus Q\to Q\oplus Q$ and
$\hat F_{f_n}\colon Q_n\oplus Q_n\to Q_n\oplus Q_n$,
\[
\hat F_f(q,p)=\bigl(q+Sp,\ p-A(q+Sp)\bigr),
\qquad
\hat F_{f_n}(x,z)=\bigl(x+S_nz,\ z-A_n(x+S_nz)\bigr),
\]
and, by the Riesz identities, $E_n$ and $R_n$ become
$\iota_n\oplus\iota_n$ and $\iota_n^\dagger\oplus\iota_n^\dagger$.
Put $P_n\coloneqq\iota_n\iota_n^\dagger\colon Q\to Q$, the orthogonal
projection onto the image of $\iota_n$.  Substituting the compressions,
the conjugate of $E_nF_{f_n}R_n$ is
\[
(q,p)\longmapsto
\bigl(P_nq+P_nSP_np,\ \ P_np-P_nA(P_nq+P_nSP_np)\bigr).
\]
Since the images increase with dense union and
$\lVert P_n\rVert\leq1$, \cref{lem.strong-limits}\cref{item.dense-limit}
gives $P_n\to\id_Q$ strongly.  Each coordinate above is a sum of
composites of the fixed bounded maps $S$, $A$, $\id$ with the uniformly
bounded, strongly convergent $P_n$; sums of strong limits are strong
limits, and composites are by
\cref{lem.strong-limits}\cref{item.composed-limit}, so the display
converges strongly to
$(q,p)\mapsto\bigl(q+Sp,\ p-A(q+Sp)\bigr)=\hat F_f(q,p)$.  Conjugating
back by the fixed unitaries preserves strong convergence, so
$E_nF_{f_n}R_n\to F_f$ strongly.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Chains converge to the lattice]\label{prop.chain-limit}
Fix $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$, put $Q\coloneqq\ell^2(\Z)$, and let $E_n,R_n$ be as
in \cref{thm.lift-continuity} for the extensions by zero
$\iota_n\colon\ell^2(\Lambda_n)\to Q$.  Write
$\bigl(T^*\ell^2(\Lambda_n),F^{\mathrm{cl},n}\bigr)
\coloneqq\Phih\bigl(\scale_h\,\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n}\bigr)$
and, for the free chains $\Chain^{(n)}\coloneqq\Chain^{Z,h}_{2n+1}$ of
\cref{prop.chain} relabelled by $\Lambda_n$,
$\bigl(T^*\ell^2(\Lambda_n),F^{(n)}\bigr)
\coloneqq\Phih\bigl(\scale_h\,\Chain^{(n)}\bigr)$.  Then, strongly,
\[
E_n\circ F^{\mathrm{cl},n}\circ R_n\longrightarrow F_{Z,h}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
E_n\circ F^{(n)}\circ R_n\longrightarrow F_{Z,h}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Applying the endofunctor $\scale_h$ of $\Harm$ to
\cref{thm.lattice-colimit} gives morphisms
$\scale_h\Chain^{\mathrm{cl},n}\to\scale_h\Lat_{Z,h}$ along the same
$\iota_n$, whose images increase with dense union;
\cref{thm.lift-continuity} gives the clamped case.

For the free case, \cref{prop.chain,prop.clamped-chain} give
$U^{\mathrm{cl}}-U=\frac{Z}{2h}(q_{-n}^2+q_n^2)$ on $\Lambda_n$, so
after $\scale_h$ the two lifts differ only in the momentum update, by
the differential of $\frac Z2(q_{-n}^2+q_n^2)$ at the presented
position:
\[
\bigl(F^{(n)}-F^{\mathrm{cl},n}\bigr)(x,\zeta)
=\Bigl(0,\ Z\,\riesz_{\ell^2(\Lambda_n)}^{-1}
\bigl(\widetilde x_{-n}e_{-n}+\widetilde x_ne_n\bigr)\Bigr),
\qquad
\widetilde x=x+\riesz_{\ell^2(\Lambda_n)}(\zeta)/Z.
\]
At $(x,\zeta)=R_n(q,\xi)$ the presented positions agree,
$\widetilde x_{\pm n}=\widetilde q_{\pm n}$, and the momentum leg of
$E_n$ is an isometry, so
\[
\bigl\lVert E_n\bigl(F^{(n)}-F^{\mathrm{cl},n}\bigr)R_n(q,\xi)
\bigr\rVert^2
=Z^2\bigl(\widetilde q_{-n}^{\,2}+\widetilde q_n^{\,2}\bigr)
\longrightarrow0,
\]
being a tail of the $\ell^2$-vector $\widetilde q$.  Hence the free
compressions have the same strong limit.
\end{proof}

The convergence is strong but not uniform: for unit states concentrated
at the sites $\pm n$, the boundary terms in the proof have norm
independent of $n$.

\subsection{The characteristic morphism}
\label{sec.characteristic}

For $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$, define the \emph{cell packet}
\[
\defineTerm{CZ}(h)\coloneqq\R[Zh]
\]
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}): the real line with inner product
$\langle a,b\rangle_{\CZ(h)}=Zh\,ab$.

\begin{theorem}[Characteristic factorization]
\label{thm.characteristic-factorization}
Let $(X,u)$ be a unitary representation of $\Z$ and $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$.
The bounded map
\begin{equation}\label{eq.characteristic-coordinates}
\defineTerm{Wchar}_u\colon T^*X\to X[Zh]\oplus X[Zh],
\qquad
\Wc_u(q,\xi)
\coloneqq
\frac1{2h}
\bigl(\riesz_X(\xi)/Z+D_uq,\ \ \riesz_X(\xi)/Z+D_u^\dagger q\bigr),
\end{equation}
is a morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq.characteristic-morphism-type}
\Wc_u\colon\bigl(T^*X,\ F_u\bigr)
\longrightarrow
\bigl(X[Zh]\oplus X[Zh],\ u\oplus u^{-1}\bigr)
\quad\text{in }\Dyn.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity function $X\to X[Zh]$ is bounded, and $\riesz_X$, $D_u$,
and $D_u^\dagger$ are bounded, so $\Wc_u$ is bounded.  For
\cref{eq.characteristic-morphism-type} we must show
$\Wc_u\circ F_u=(u\oplus u^{-1})\circ\Wc_u$; since every state
begins a trajectory, it suffices to check this along trajectories.  Let
$(q(t),\xi(t))_{t\in\N}$ be a trajectory of $F_u$ and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq.characteristic-trajectory}
\bigl(w^{\leftarrow}(t),\,w^{\rightarrow}(t)\bigr)
\coloneqq\Wc_u\bigl(q(t),\xi(t)\bigr);
\end{equation}
the claim is the propagation law
\begin{equation}\label{eq.characteristic-propagation}
w^{\leftarrow}(t+1)=u\,w^{\leftarrow}(t),
\qquad
w^{\rightarrow}(t+1)=u^{-1}w^{\rightarrow}(t).
\end{equation}
By \cref{eq.characteristic-coordinates,eq.momentum-position} and
$D_u=\id-u^{-1}$,
\[
2h\,w^{\leftarrow}(t)=\dot q(t)+D_uq(t)=q(t+1)-u^{-1}q(t).
\]
Since $D_u^\dagger D_u=(\id-u)(\id-u^{-1})=2\,\id-u-u^{-1}$, we have
$D_u-D_u^\dagger D_u=u-\id$; and \cref{eq.discrete-wave} at time
$t+1$ reads $\dot q(t+1)=\dot q(t)-D_u^\dagger D_u\,q(t+1)$.  Therefore
\begin{align*}
2h\,w^{\leftarrow}(t+1)
&=\dot q(t+1)+D_uq(t+1)
=\dot q(t)+(D_u-D_u^\dagger D_u)\,q(t+1)\\
&=u\,q(t+1)-q(t)
=u\bigl(q(t+1)-u^{-1}q(t)\bigr),
\end{align*}
which is $2h\,u\,w^{\leftarrow}(t)$.  The right-travelling
case is the same computation with $D_u^\dagger$, $u^{-1}$ in place
of $D_u$, $u$, using $D_u^\dagger-D_u^\dagger D_u=u^{-1}-\id$.
This proves \cref{eq.characteristic-propagation}, hence
\cref{eq.characteristic-morphism-type}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Zero modes]\label{rmk.zero-modes}
$\Wc_u(q,\xi)=0$ says $\riesz_X(\xi)/Z=-D_uq=-D_u^\dagger q$;
subtracting the two right-hand sides and using
$D_u-D_u^\dagger=u-u^{-1}$, the assignment
$q\mapsto\bigl(q,\ -Z\riesz_X^{-1}(D_uq)\bigr)$ is an isomorphism
\[
\ker(u^2-\id_X)\To{\cong}\ker\Wc_u.
\]
For the shift representation this kernel is $0$: no nonzero $2$-periodic
sequence is square-summable.  For a cyclic chain
(\cref{prop.cyclic-chain}) it contains the constant displacements---and,
at even $K$, the alternating mode; because these modes exist, $\Wc_u$
is a morphism and not in general an isomorphism.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Naturality of the characteristic factorization]
\label{prop.characteristic-natural}
The assignments
\[
\begin{gathered}
(X,u)\longmapsto\bigl(T^*X,\ F_u\bigr),
\qquad
\phi\longmapsto T^*\phi,\\
(X,u)\longmapsto\bigl(X[Zh]^{\oplus2},\ u\oplus u^{-1}\bigr),
\qquad
\phi\longmapsto\phi\oplus\phi
\end{gathered}
\]
are functors $\mathrm U(\Z)\to\Dyn$, and
$\Wc\colon(T^*\blank,\ F_{\blank})\Longrightarrow
(\blank[Zh]^{\oplus2},\ \blank\oplus\blank^{-1})$
is a natural transformation: for every
$\phi\colon(X,u)\to(Y,v)$ in $\mathrm U(\Z)$,
\[
(\phi\oplus\phi)\circ\Wc_u=\Wc_v\circ T^*\phi.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assignment is the composite of
$\Lat_{Z,h}(\blank)\colon\mathrm U(\Z)\to\Harm$
(\cref{prop.lat-functor}), the endofunctor $\scale_h$ of $\Harm$
(companion to \cref{def.harm}), and $\Phih$ on isomorphisms
(\cref{lem.lift-iso}).  For the second: $\phi$ is unitary
$X[Zh]\to Y[Zh]$, and $\phi u=v\phi$ together with
$\phi u^{-1}=v^{-1}\phi$ makes $\phi\oplus\phi$ a morphism of $\Dyn$;
composition and identities are those of $\Hilb$.  For naturality,
compare components at $(q,\xi)\in T^*X$.  The first component of
$(\phi\oplus\phi)\circ\Wc_u$ is
$\frac1{2h}\bigl(\phi\riesz_X(\xi)/Z+\phi D_uq\bigr)$, and that of
$\Wc_v\circ T^*\phi$ is
$\frac1{2h}\bigl(\riesz_Y\bigl((\phi^*)^{-1}\xi\bigr)/Z+D_v\phi q\bigr)$;
these agree by the Riesz identities \cref{eq.riesz-identities}, which
give $\riesz_Y\circ(\phi^*)^{-1}=\phi\circ\riesz_X$ for unitary $\phi$,
and by the naturality of $D$ (\cref{sec.background_hilbert}).  The
second component is the same computation with $D^\dagger$, using
$\phi D_u^\dagger=D_v^\dagger\phi$, again from $\phi u=v\phi$.
\end{proof}

For the shift representation, the target of
\cref{eq.characteristic-morphism-type} is the \emph{shift system}:
since $\ell^2(\Z)[Zh]=\ell^2\bigl(\Z,\CZ(h)\bigr)$ and
$(\sigma^{\leftarrow})^{-1}=\sigma^{\rightarrow}$, put
\[
\mathcal C_{Z,h}
\coloneqq
\ell^2\bigl(\Z,\CZ(h)\bigr)
\oplus
\ell^2\bigl(\Z,\CZ(h)\bigr),
\qquad
\Sigma_{Z,h}
\coloneqq
\sigma^{\leftarrow}\oplus\sigma^{\rightarrow}
\colon\mathcal C_{Z,h}\to\mathcal C_{Z,h},
\]
\begin{equation}\label{eq.shift-system}
\defineTerm{shift}_{Z,h}\coloneqq(\mathcal C_{Z,h},\Sigma_{Z,h})\in\Dyn.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[The lattice characteristics]
\label{cor.lattice-characteristic}
Put $Q\coloneqq\ell^2(\Z)$ and
$\Wc_{Z,h}\coloneqq\Wc_{\sigma^{\leftarrow}}$.  Then
\[
\Wc_{Z,h}\colon\bigl(T^*Q,F_{Z,h}\bigr)\longrightarrow\Shift_{Z,h}
\quad\text{in }\Dyn,
\]
and applying $\Ufun$ \cref{eq.dyn-forgetful} and
\cref{prop.phase-semantics} gives a 2-cell
\begin{equation}\label{eq.characteristic-two-cell}
\Ufun(\Wc_{Z,h})\colon
\Phiphase\bigl(\scale_h\,\Lat_{Z,h}\bigr)
\Longrightarrow
\Ufun(\Shift_{Z,h})
\quad\text{in }\pc.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The two categorical steps have different types.  The operad functor
$\Phiphase$ sends the operation
$\scale_h\,\Lat_{Z,h}\in\harr(I;I)$ to an object of the hom-category
$\pc(\yon,\yon)$.  The map $\Wc_{Z,h}$ is a morphism in that
hom-category, hence a 2-cell in $\pc$.  They are therefore not consecutive
1-arrows in a single category.

\section{Universal queues and subdivision}
\label{sec.queues}

\subsection{The queue representation theorem}

Fix a real Hilbert space $B\in\Hilb$ and an integer $N\geq1$.  Define
\[
Q_N(B)\coloneqq B^{\oplus N}.
\]
Define bounded linear maps
\[
\begin{gathered}
S_N\colon Q_N(B)\to Q_N(B),
\qquad
J_N\colon B\to Q_N(B),\\
P_N\colon Q_N(B)\to B,
\qquad
U_N\colon Q_N(B)\oplus B\to Q_N(B)
\end{gathered}
\]
by, for every $x=(b_0,\ldots,b_{N-1})\in Q_N(B)$ and every $b\in B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq.queue-structure}
\begin{aligned}
S_Nx&\coloneqq(b_1,\ldots,b_{N-1},0),\\
J_Nb&\coloneqq(0,\ldots,0,b),\\
P_Nx&\coloneqq b_0,\\
U_N(x,b)&\coloneqq S_Nx+J_Nb.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The lossless queue step is
\begin{equation}\label{eq.finite-queue-step}
\defineTerm{step}_N\colon Q_N(B)\oplus B\longrightarrow B\oplus Q_N(B),
\qquad
\step_N(x,b)\coloneqq\bigl(P_Nx,U_N(x,b)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Exact-delay realization]
\label{def.delay-realization}
Fix $B\in\Hilb$ and an integer $N\geq1$.  A \emph{bounded linear
realization of exact $N$-step delay on $B$} consists of a Hilbert space
$X\in\Hilb$ and bounded maps
\[
T_X\colon X\to X,
\qquad
G_X\colon B\to X,
\qquad
R_X\colon X\to B
\]
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq.delay-laws}
R_XT_X^N=0,
\qquad
R_XT_X^kG_X
=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\leq k<N-1,\\
\id_B,&k=N-1.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
A morphism $f\colon(X,T_X,G_X,R_X)\to(Y,T_Y,G_Y,R_Y)$ is a bounded
linear map $f\colon X\to Y$ satisfying
\[
fT_X=T_Yf,
\qquad
fG_X=G_Y,
\qquad
R_X=R_Yf.
\]
Write $\defineTerm{delay}_N(B)$ for the resulting category.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Delay realizations are linear Moore machines]
\label{rmk.delay-moore}
By linearity, a realization $(X,T_X,G_X,R_X)$ is the same data as a
bounded linear \emph{Moore machine} $(X,U_X,R_X)$ with update
$U_X\colon X\oplus B\to X$ and readout $R_X\colon X\to B$: set
$U_X(x,b)\coloneqq T_Xx+G_Xb$ and recover $T_X=U_X(\blank,0)$ and
$G_X=U_X(0,\blank)$; the morphism conditions match likewise.
\Cref{sec.harmonic_segments} will regard such machines, at the level of
underlying sets, as $1$-cells in $\pc$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Queue representation]\label{thm.queue-representation}
Fix $B\in\Hilb$ and an integer $N\geq1$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The map $\step_N$ of \cref{eq.finite-queue-step} is unitary.

\item The realization
\[
\mathbb Q_N(B)
\coloneqq
\bigl(Q_N(B),S_N,J_N,P_N\bigr)
\]
is terminal in $\DelayCat_N(B)$.

\item For every object
$M=(X,T_X,G_X,R_X)\in\DelayCat_N(B)$, the terminal map is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_M\colon X&\longrightarrow Q_N(B),\\
\Theta_M(x)&\coloneqq
\bigl(R_Xx,R_XT_Xx,\ldots,R_XT_X^{N-1}x\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Its underlying map in $\Hilb$ is a split epimorphism.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq.lossless-observability}
\lVert x\rVert_X^2
=
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\lVert R_XT_X^kx\rVert_B^2
\qquad(x\in X),
\end{equation}
then $\Theta_M$ is a unitary isomorphism in
$\DelayCat_N(B)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map $\step_N$ is the coordinate permutation
\[
\bigl((b_0,\ldots,b_{N-1}),b\bigr)
\longmapsto
\bigl(b_0,(b_1,\ldots,b_{N-1},b)\bigr),
\]
with the first component designated as output and the last $N$ components
designated as the new state; hence it is unitary.

The maps in \cref{eq.queue-structure} satisfy the delay laws
\cref{eq.delay-laws}.  Fix an object
$M=(X,T_X,G_X,R_X)\in\DelayCat_N(B)$.  Its delay laws give
\[
P_N\Theta_M=R_X,
\qquad
\Theta_MT_X=S_N\Theta_M,
\qquad
\Theta_MG_X=J_N.
\]
Thus $\Theta_M$ is a morphism to $\mathbb Q_N(B)$.  Its $k$th component
must be $R_XT_X^k$, so it is the unique such morphism.

A section of $\Theta_M$ is
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_M\colon Q_N(B)&\longrightarrow X,\\
L_M(b_0,\ldots,b_{N-1})
&\coloneqq
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}T_X^{N-1-k}G_Xb_k.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, \cref{eq.delay-laws} gives
$\Theta_ML_M=\id_{Q_N(B)}$.  Finally,
\cref{eq.lossless-observability} says exactly that $\Theta_M$ is isometric;
the split epimorphism is then unitary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Harmonic segments as open subsystems}
\label{sec.harmonic_segments}

We factor the shift system as a hom-composite in $\pc$: a window of
$K$ sites is a queue and the rest of the line is its environment.  We
first compute the relevant hom-categories.

\begin{lemma}[Hom-categories at a monomial interface]
\label{lem.monomial-homs}
Let $B_0$ be a set and put $p\coloneqq B_0\yon^{B_0}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\pc(\yon,p)$ is isomorphic to the category of \emph{Moore
machines}: objects are triples $(S,r,u)$ of a set $S$, a readout
$r\colon S\to B_0$, and an update $u\colon S\times B_0\to S$;
morphisms are functions of state sets commuting with readouts and
updates.
\item $\pc(p,\yon)$ is isomorphic to the category of \emph{Mealy
environments}: objects are pairs $(S',\langle u',e\rangle)$ of a set
$S'$ and a function
$\langle u',e\rangle\colon S'\times B_0\to S'\times B_0$; morphisms
are functions commuting with $\langle u',e\rangle$.
\item Under these isomorphisms, hom-composition
$\pc(\yon,p)\times\pc(p,\yon)\to\pc(\yon,\yon)$ is given on
objects by
\begin{equation}\label{eq.loop-transition}
\bigl((S,r,u),\,(S',\langle u',e\rangle)\bigr)
\longmapsto
\Bigl(S\times S',\ \
(s,s')\mapsto
\bigl(u(s,\,e(s',r(s))),\ u'(s',r(s))\bigr)\Bigr)
\end{equation}
and on morphisms by the product of functions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Since $\yon$ is the $\otimes$-unit, $[\yon,p]\cong p$, so an
object of $\pc(\yon,p)$ is a $p$-coalgebra
$S\to p(S)=B_0\times S^{B_0}$, i.e.\ a pair $(r,u)$ as stated; its
morphisms are the coalgebra morphisms, which unwind to the stated
commutations.

(ii) An object of $\pc(p,\yon)$ is a coalgebra $S'\to[p,\yon](S')$,
equivalently a polynomial map $\Store(S')\otimes p\to\yon$
(\cref{sec.background_categories}).  On positions such a map is the
unique function $S'\times B_0\to1$; on directions it selects, at each
$(s',b)$, an element of $S'\times B_0$.  This is exactly a function
$\langle u',e\rangle\colon S'\times B_0\to S'\times B_0$, and
coalgebra morphisms unwind to functions commuting with it.

(iii) The composite coalgebra is carried by $S\times S'$, with
structure map the composite
\[
\Store(S\times S')\cong\Store(S)\otimes\Store(S')
\longrightarrow p\otimes\Store(S')
\longrightarrow\yon
\]
of the two structure maps, after the symmetry
\cite[\S2.2.4]{cdlm}.%sec.org
On positions it is unique; on directions at $(s,s')$, the second map
selects $\bigl(u'(s',r(s)),\,e(s',r(s))\bigr)$ and the first returns
$u\bigl(s,e(s',r(s))\bigr)$ in the $S$-coordinate, giving
\cref{eq.loop-transition}.  Morphisms compose componentwise.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stateless 1-cells]\label{lem.stateless}
For polynomials $p,p'$, evaluation gives
$[p,p'](1)\cong\poly(p,p')$, so every polynomial map
$f\colon p\to p'$ determines a $1$-cell in $\pc(p,p')$ with a single
state, its \emph{stateless} $1$-cell.  The assignment preserves
identities, hom-composition, and $\otimes$; we elide it, writing $f$
for the stateless $1$-cell of $f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A coalgebra with carrier $1$ is a point of $[p,p'](1)$, and
$[p,p'](1)\cong\poly(p,p')$ \cite{niu2025polynomial}.  On single-state
carriers, hom-composition and $\otimes$ of coalgebras restrict to
composition and $\otimes$ of the selected polynomial maps, by the same
unwinding as in \cref{lem.monomial-homs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Queue and environment 1-cells]
\label{def.queue-environment}
Fix $B\in\Hilb$ and an integer $K\geq1$, and put
$\Lambda'_K\coloneqq\Z\setminus\{1,\ldots,K\}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We write again
$\mathbb Q_K(B)\colon\yon\to\lvert B\rvert\,\yon^{\lvert B\rvert}$
for the Moore machine (\cref{lem.monomial-homs}) underlying the
terminal realization (\cref{thm.queue-representation,rmk.delay-moore}):
states $\lvert Q_K(B)\rvert$, readout $P_K$, update $U_K$.
\item The \emph{left environment}
$\fun{env}^{\leftarrow}_K\colon
\lvert B\rvert\,\yon^{\lvert B\rvert}\to\yon$
has states $\lvert\ell^2(\Lambda'_K,B)\rvert$, reply
$e(s,b)\coloneqq s_{K+1}$, and update $u'(s,b)$ given by
$u'(s,b)_0\coloneqq b$ and $u'(s,b)_i\coloneqq s_{i+1}$ at all other
$i\in\Lambda'_K$.
\item The \emph{right environment} $\fun{env}^{\rightarrow}_K$ has the
same states, reply $e(s,b)\coloneqq s_0$, and update given by
$u'(s,b)_{K+1}\coloneqq b$ and $u'(s,b)_i\coloneqq s_{i-1}$ at all
other $i\in\Lambda'_K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Segment factorization]\label{thm.segment-factorization}
Fix $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$ and $K\geq1$, and put $B\coloneqq\CZ(h)$.  In
$\pc(\yon,\yon)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq.segment-factorization}
\Ufun(\Shift_{Z,h})
\;\cong\;
\bigl(\fun{env}^{\leftarrow}_K\circ\mathbb Q_K(B)\bigr)
\otimes
\bigl(\fun{env}^{\rightarrow}_K\circ\mathbb Q_K(B)\bigr),
\end{equation}
the isomorphism given on the left factor by
$a\mapsto\bigl((a_1,\ldots,a_K),\,a|_{\Lambda'_K}\bigr)$ and on the
right factor by
$a\mapsto\bigl((a_K,\ldots,a_1),\,a|_{\Lambda'_K}\bigr)$, for
$a\in\ell^2(\Z,B)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the left factor, let $a\in\ell^2(\Z,B)$ correspond to the pair
$(x,s)$ with $x\coloneqq(a_1,\ldots,a_K)\in Q_K(B)$ and
$s\coloneqq a|_{\Lambda'_K}$.  In the composite transition
\cref{eq.loop-transition}: the queue emits $b=P_K(x)=a_1$; the
environment replies $e(s,b)=s_{K+1}=a_{K+1}$ and updates to the sequence
with value $b=a_1$ at $0$ and $s_{i+1}=a_{i+1}$ at all other
$i\in\Lambda'_K$, which is $(\sigma^{\leftarrow}a)|_{\Lambda'_K}$;
and the queue updates to $U_K(x,a_{K+1})=(a_2,\ldots,a_{K+1})$, the
window part of $\sigma^{\leftarrow}a$.  So under the stated bijection
the composite transition is $\sigma^{\leftarrow}$.  Symmetrically, on
the right factor the queue stores the window in the order
$(a_K,\ldots,a_1)$, emits $a_K$, receives the reply $a_0$, and the
composite transition is $\sigma^{\rightarrow}$.  Finally, $\otimes$ in
$\pc(\yon,\yon)$ is the parallel product of dynamical systems and
$\Sigma_{Z,h}=\sigma^{\leftarrow}\oplus\sigma^{\rightarrow}$
\cref{eq.shift-system}, so the two bijections together form an
invertible 2-cell as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The queue inside the lattice]
\label{cor.queue-in-lattice}
Composing \cref{eq.characteristic-two-cell} with
\cref{eq.segment-factorization} gives a 2-cell in $\pc(\yon,\yon)$:
\[
\Phiphase\bigl(\scale_h\,\Lat_{Z,h}\bigr)
\Longrightarrow
\bigl(\fun{env}^{\leftarrow}_K\circ\mathbb Q_K(B)\bigr)
\otimes
\bigl(\fun{env}^{\rightarrow}_K\circ\mathbb Q_K(B)\bigr),
\qquad B=\CZ(h).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Characteristic queues]\label{cor.harmonic-queues}
Fix $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$ and an integer $K\geq1$.  Let
$(q(t),\xi(t))_{t\in\N}$ be a trajectory of the phase transition
$F_{Z,h}$, and let
$w^{\leftarrow}(t),w^{\rightarrow}(t)$ be its characteristic trajectory
\cref{eq.characteristic-trajectory}.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq.segment-states}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf w_K^{\leftarrow}(t)
&\coloneqq
\bigl(w_1^{\leftarrow}(t),\ldots,w_K^{\leftarrow}(t)\bigr),\\
\mathbf w_K^{\rightarrow}(t)
&\coloneqq
\bigl(w_K^{\rightarrow}(t),\ldots,w_1^{\rightarrow}(t)\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
as elements of $Q_K(\CZ(h))$, and define the incoming packets
$b^{\leftarrow}(t)\coloneqq w_{K+1}^{\leftarrow}(t)$ and
$b^{\rightarrow}(t)\coloneqq w_0^{\rightarrow}(t)$.
Then the two segment trajectories satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
\step_K\bigl(\mathbf w_K^{\leftarrow}(t),b^{\leftarrow}(t)\bigr)
&=
\bigl(w_1^{\leftarrow}(t),\mathbf w_K^{\leftarrow}(t+1)\bigr),\\
\step_K\bigl(\mathbf w_K^{\rightarrow}(t),b^{\rightarrow}(t)\bigr)
&=
\bigl(w_K^{\rightarrow}(t),\mathbf w_K^{\rightarrow}(t+1)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently each one-way segment is the terminal exact
$K$-step delay on the packet space $\CZ(h)$, and each update satisfies the
energy balance obtained from the unitarity of $\step_K$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{eq.characteristic-propagation}, the characteristic trajectory
is a trajectory of $\Ufun(\Shift_{Z,h})$; transport it through the
isomorphism of \cref{thm.segment-factorization}.  On the left factor its
queue leg at time $t$ is $\mathbf w_K^{\leftarrow}(t)$, and by
\cref{eq.loop-transition} one tick emits $w_1^{\leftarrow}(t)$,
receives the environment's reply
$b^{\leftarrow}(t)=w_{K+1}^{\leftarrow}(t)$, and updates by $U_K$; by
\cref{eq.finite-queue-step} this is the first line of the display.  The
right factor is symmetric, with the travel-order convention of
\cref{eq.segment-states}.  Terminality and losslessness follow from
\cref{thm.queue-representation}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Subdivision as time change}

For every integer $r\geq1$, multiplication by $r$ is a monoid
homomorphism $r\cdot\colon\N\to\N$; time change along it
(\cref{sec.background_hilbert}) is
\[
(r\cdot)^*\colon\Dyn\longrightarrow\Dyn,
\qquad
(r\cdot)^*(X,F)=(X,F^r).
\]
Thus a map $\rho\colon X\to Y$ identifies one coarse step with $r$ fine
steps precisely when
$\rho\colon(X,F)\to(r\cdot)^*(Y,G)$ in $\Dyn$,
or equivalently $\rho F=G^r\rho$.

For a real Hilbert space $C$, integers $L,r\geq1$, and the standard queue
step $\step_L$ on $C$, define the $r$-fold block step
\[
\step_L^{[r]}\colon
Q_L(C)\oplus C^{\oplus r}
\longrightarrow
C^{\oplus r}\oplus Q_L(C)
\]
as follows.  Given $x_0\in Q_L(C)$ and
$(c_0,\ldots,c_{r-1})\in C^{\oplus r}$, define
\[
(y_j,x_{j+1})\coloneqq\step_L(x_j,c_j)
\quad(0\leq j<r),
\qquad
\step_L^{[r]}(x_0,c_0,\ldots,c_{r-1})
\coloneqq(y_0,\ldots,y_{r-1},x_r).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Queue subdivision]\label{thm.queue-subdivision}
Fix real Hilbert spaces $B,C\in\Hilb$, integers $N,r\geq1$, and an
isometric embedding
$\sigma\colon B\hookrightarrow C^{\oplus r}$.
Let
$\lambda_{N,r}\colon
Q_N(C^{\oplus r})\To{\cong}Q_{rN}(C)$
be the unitary that removes parentheses while preserving order, and define
\[
\rho_{N,r,\sigma}
\coloneqq
\lambda_{N,r}\circ Q_N(\sigma)
\colon
Q_N(B)\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow Q_{rN}(C).
\]
Then the following square commutes:
\begin{equation}\label{eq.queue-subdivision-square}
\begin{CD}
Q_N(B)\oplus B
@>{\step_N}>>
B\oplus Q_N(B)\\
@V{\rho_{N,r,\sigma}\oplus\sigma}VV
@VV{\sigma\oplus\rho_{N,r,\sigma}}V\\
Q_{rN}(C)\oplus C^{\oplus r}
@>>{\step_{rN}^{[r]}}>
C^{\oplus r}\oplus Q_{rN}(C).
\end{CD}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq.queue-dilated-equivariance}
\rho_{N,r,\sigma}S_N
=
S_{rN}^{\,r}\rho_{N,r,\sigma},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}\label{eq.queue-dyn-morphism}
\rho_{N,r,\sigma}\colon
\bigl(Q_N(B),S_N\bigr)
\longrightarrow
(r\cdot)^*\bigl(Q_{rN}(C),S_{rN}\bigr)
\quad\text{in }\Dyn.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map $\rho_{N,r,\sigma}$ replaces each coarse packet by its ordered
$r$-packet subdivision.  The top of \cref{eq.queue-subdivision-square}
removes the first coarse packet and appends one coarse input.  The bottom
removes its $r$ refined packets and appends the $r$ components of the
refined input.  Both routes are therefore the same coordinate
reassociation.  Setting the input equal to zero gives
\cref{eq.queue-dilated-equivariance,eq.queue-dyn-morphism}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dyadic harmonic subdivision]
\label{cor.dyadic-subdivision}
Fix $Z,d\in\R_{>0}$.  For every $n\in\N$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq.dyadic-data}
K_n\coloneqq2^n,
\qquad
h_n\coloneqq d/K_n,
\qquad
C_n\coloneqq \CZ(h_n),
\qquad
H_n\coloneqq Q_{K_n}(C_n).
\end{equation}
The packet map
$\sigma_n\colon C_n\hookrightarrow C_{n+1}^{\oplus2}$,
$\sigma_n(b)\coloneqq(b,b)$,
is an isometric embedding.  The state refinement
\begin{equation}\label{eq.dyadic-state-refinement}
\rho_n
\coloneqq
\lambda_{K_n,2}\circ Q_{K_n}(\sigma_n)
\colon H_n\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow H_{n+1}
\end{equation}
is therefore an isometric embedding and satisfies
$\rho_nS_{K_n}=S_{K_{n+1}}^2\rho_n$.
The full input-output refinement is the instance of
\cref{eq.queue-subdivision-square} determined by
$B=C_n$, $C=C_{n+1}$, $N=K_n$, $r=2$, and $\sigma=\sigma_n$.
Applying the same square to both summands of $H_n\oplus H_n$ refines the
two characteristic queues of \cref{cor.harmonic-queues}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every $b\in C_n$,
\[
\lVert\sigma_n(b)\rVert^2
=
2Zh_{n+1}b^2
=
Zh_nb^2
=
\lVert b\rVert^2.
\]
The result is now \cref{thm.queue-subdivision}.
\end{proof}

\section{Completion and interval algebra}
\label{sec.completion}

\subsection{The completed characteristic state}

Fix $Z,d\in\R_{>0}$ and let $(H_n,\rho_n)_{n\in\N}$ be the directed system
\cref{eq.dyadic-data,eq.dyadic-state-refinement} of isometric embeddings.
Using the increasing-union and completion constructions of
\cref{sec.background_hilbert}, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq.completed-state}
H_\infty(d,Z)
\coloneqq
\bigcup_{n\in\N}H_n,
\qquad
\defineTerm{EZ}(d)\coloneqq\widehat{H_\infty(d,Z)},
\end{equation}
and for duration zero put $\EZ(0)\coloneqq0$.  By
\cref{lem.isometric-colimits}, maps out of $\EZ(d)$ are uniformly
bounded compatible families \cref{eq.completion-universal-property}: it
is the colimit of $(H_n,\rho_n)$ in Hilbert spaces and contractions.

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic completion]\label{thm.dyadic-completion}
Fix $Z,d\in\R_{>0}$ and let $\EZ(d)$ be the Hilbert space
\cref{eq.completed-state}.  There is a canonical unitary
\begin{equation}\label{eq.interval-realization}
\iota_Z(d)\colon
\EZ(d)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }L^2([0,d),Z\,dx).
\end{equation}
For every $n\in\N$, the image of $H_n$ consists of the functions constant
on each interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq.dyadic-cells}
\left[
\frac{jd}{2^n},
\frac{(j+1)d}{2^n}
\right),
\qquad
0\leq j<2^n.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every $n\in\N$, send
$x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{2^n-1})\in H_n$ to the step function with value $x_j$
on the $j$th interval in \cref{eq.dyadic-cells}.  This map is isometric
because
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}
\int_{jd/2^n}^{(j+1)d/2^n}Zx_j^2\,dx
=
\frac{Zd}{2^n}\sum_{j=0}^{2^n-1}x_j^2
=
\lVert x\rVert_{H_n}^2.
\]
The maps commute with $\rho_n$, so they assemble into an isometry from
$H_\infty(d,Z)$ onto the subspace of dyadic step functions, which is dense
in $L^2([0,d),Z\,dx)$; completion gives \cref{eq.interval-realization}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Ordered intervals}

\begin{definition}[Ordered interval operad]\label{def.interval-operad}
The nonsymmetric colored operad $\defineTerm{intop}$ has colors
$d\in\R_{\geq0}$ and operation sets
\[
\Int(d_1,\ldots,d_k;d)
\coloneqq
\begin{cases}
\{*\},&d=d_1+\cdots+d_k,\\
\varnothing,&d\neq d_1+\cdots+d_k,
\end{cases}
\]
for every integer $k\geq0$ and all
$d,d_1,\ldots,d_k\in\R_{\geq0}$.  Substitution is induced by
associativity of addition.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The characteristic interval algebra]
\label{thm.characteristic-interval-algebra}
Fix $Z\in\R_{>0}$.  For every integer $k\geq0$ and durations
$d_1,\ldots,d_k\in\R_{\geq0}$, interval concatenation gives a unitary
\[
\Gamma^Z_{d_1,\ldots,d_k}\colon
\bigoplus_{i=1}^k\EZ(d_i)
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
\EZ(d_1+\cdots+d_k).
\]
These maps make
$\EZ\colon\Int\to\Hilb$, $d\mapsto \EZ(d)$,
a strong $\Int$-algebra in $(\Hilb,0,\oplus)$
(\cref{sec.background_categories}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $s_0\coloneqq0$ and
$s_i\coloneqq d_1+\cdots+d_i$ for $1\leq i\leq k$.  Under
\cref{eq.interval-realization}, the map
$\Gamma^Z_{d_1,\ldots,d_k}$ translates the function on $[0,d_i)$ to
$[s_{i-1},s_i)$.  These intervals are disjoint and cover $[0,s_k)$, so
the map is unitary; for $k=0$ it is the identity of $\EZ(0)=0$.
Translation in stages equals translation all at once,
which is the substitution axiom of $\Int$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Delay lines from interval algebras}

\begin{theorem}[Interval-algebra delay line]
\label{thm.interval-delay-line}
Let $A\colon\Int\to\Hilb$ be a strong $\Int$-algebra whose structure maps
$\Gamma^A_{d_1,\ldots,d_k}\colon
\bigoplus_{i=1}^kA(d_i)\To{\cong}A(d_1+\cdots+d_k)$
are unitary.  Fix $d\in\R_{>0}$ and $a\in(0,d]$.  The composite
\begin{equation}\label{eq.abstract-delay-step}
\begin{aligned}
\step^A_{d,a}\colon
A(d)\oplus A(a)
&\xrightarrow{\ (\Gamma^A_{a,d-a})^{-1}\oplus\id\ }
A(a)\oplus A(d-a)\oplus A(a)\\
&\xrightarrow{\ \id\oplus\Gamma^A_{d-a,a}\ }
A(a)\oplus A(d)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
is unitary.

For every $a_1,a_2\in\R_{>0}$ with $a_1+a_2\leq d$, the two-step and
one-block composites agree:
\begin{equation}\label{eq.temporal-coherence}
\begin{aligned}
(\id\oplus\step^A_{d,a_2})
\circ(\step^A_{d,a_1}\oplus\id)
&=
\bigl((\Gamma^A_{a_1,a_2})^{-1}\oplus\id\bigr)
\circ\step^A_{d,a_1+a_2}\\
&\qquad\circ
\bigl(\id\oplus\Gamma^A_{a_1,a_2}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where canonical direct-sum associators are suppressed.

Finally, fix an integer $N\geq1$ and $a\in\R_{>0}$, and put $d\coloneqq
Na$ and $B\coloneqq A(a)$.  The iterated structure map
$\gamma^A_{N,a}\colon Q_N(B)\To{\cong}A(d)$
identifies $\step_N$ with $\step^A_{d,a}$; equivalently, the square
\begin{equation}\label{eq.queue-delay-line-square}
\begin{CD}
Q_N(B)\oplus B
@>{\step_N}>>
B\oplus Q_N(B)\\
@V{\gamma^A_{N,a}\oplus\id}VV
@VV{\id\oplus\gamma^A_{N,a}}V\\
A(d)\oplus B
@>>{\step^A_{d,a}}>
B\oplus A(d)
\end{CD}
\end{equation}
commutes.  Hence $A(d)$, equipped with the corresponding shift, insertion,
and readout maps, is terminal in $\DelayCat_N(B)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two arrows in \cref{eq.abstract-delay-step} are unitary, so their
composite is unitary.  Equation \cref{eq.temporal-coherence} is the
associativity axiom for the strong $\Int$-algebra $A$: both sides split off
the first intervals of durations $a_1$ and $a_2$, preserve their order, and
append the two input intervals in that order.  The same associativity axiom
gives \cref{eq.queue-delay-line-square}.  Terminality follows from
\cref{thm.queue-representation}.
\end{proof}

Applying \cref{thm.interval-delay-line} to the algebra
$\EZ\colon\Int\to\Hilb$ gives the completed lossless queue
\begin{equation}\label{eq.completed-delay-step}
\step^Z_{d,a}
\coloneqq
\step^{\EZ}_{d,a}\colon
\EZ(d)\oplus \EZ(a)
\longrightarrow
\EZ(a)\oplus \EZ(d).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Finite queues determine the completed queue]
\label{thm.finite-completed-queue}
Fix $Z,d\in\R_{>0}$ and integers $n,m\in\N$ with $n\leq m$.  Put
\[
\begin{gathered}
K_m\coloneqq2^m,
\qquad
h_m\coloneqq d/K_m,
\qquad
C_m\coloneqq \CZ(h_m),
\qquad
H_m\coloneqq Q_{K_m}(C_m),\\
h_n\coloneqq d/2^n,
\qquad
M_{n,m}\coloneqq2^{m-n},
\qquad
B_{n,m}\coloneqq Q_{M_{n,m}}(C_m).
\end{gathered}
\]
Let
$\jmath_m\colon H_m\to \EZ(d)$ and
$\epsilon_{n,m}\colon B_{n,m}\to \EZ(h_n)$
be the step-function embeddings supplied by
\cref{eq.interval-realization}.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq.finite-completed-square}
\begin{CD}
H_m\oplus B_{n,m}
@>{\step_{K_m}^{[M_{n,m}]}}>>
B_{n,m}\oplus H_m\\
@V{\jmath_m\oplus\epsilon_{n,m}}VV
@VV{\epsilon_{n,m}\oplus\jmath_m}V\\
\EZ(d)\oplus \EZ(h_n)
@>>{\step^Z_{d,h_n}}>
\EZ(h_n)\oplus \EZ(d)
\end{CD}
\end{equation}
commutes.  For fixed $n$, the union of the top rows over all $m\geq n$ is
dense in the bottom row.  Consequently $\step^Z_{d,h_n}$ is the unique
unitary extension of the compatible finite block steps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both paths in \cref{eq.finite-completed-square} cut off the first interval
of duration $h_n$, record it as output, and append the input interval.
The square is therefore the restriction of
\cref{eq.queue-delay-line-square} to dyadic step functions.  Density
follows from \cref{thm.dyadic-completion}.  For uniqueness: the maps
$(\epsilon_{n,m}\oplus\jmath_m)\circ\step_{K_m}^{[M_{n,m}]}$ form a
compatible family of isometries on the directed system
$\bigl(H_m\oplus B_{n,m}\bigr)_{m\geq n}$, whose completed union is
$\EZ(d)\oplus\EZ(h_n)$; by \cref{lem.isometric-colimits} they admit
exactly one bounded extension, and $\step^Z_{d,h_n}$ is it.
\end{proof}

The continuous queue is thus derived from the harmonic lattice in four
typed steps: the characteristic morphism
\cref{eq.characteristic-morphism-type}, the segment factorization
\cref{eq.segment-factorization}, the time-changed subdivision
\cref{eq.queue-dyn-morphism}, and the universal completion
\cref{eq.completion-universal-property}.  The finite-to-continuum comparison
is the commuting square \cref{eq.finite-completed-square}.

\section{Continuous waves}
\label{sec.waves}

This section uses local $L^2$ analysis that the rest of the paper does
not, so we record that background here rather than in
\cref{sec.background_hilbert}.

\paragraph{Local spaces and distributions.}
Let $\Omega\subseteq\R^2$ be open.  We write $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$
for the space of measurable functions square-integrable on every compact
subset.  Every $u\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ is a distribution on
$\Omega$ and has distributional partial derivatives; we write
$H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ for the space of
$u\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ whose first distributional derivatives
again lie in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$
\cite{brezis2011functional}.  For a bounded interval $J\subseteq\R$, the
Sobolev space $H^1(J)$ consists of the $u\in L^2(J)$ with distributional
derivative $\partial_xu\in L^2(J)$; we write $H^1(J)/\R$ for its quotient
by the constant functions.  Translation acts strongly continuously on
$L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$: for $u\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$, the translates of $u$
converge to $u$ in $L^2$ on compacta as the displacement tends to $0$.  We
use the distributional Poincar\'e lemma on rectangles: if
$v,e\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ with $\Omega$ an open rectangle satisfy
$\partial_\tau e=\partial_xv$ distributionally, then the closed
distributional one-form
$v\,d\tau+e\,dx$ has a potential $u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$---that
is, $\partial_\tau u=v$ and $\partial_xu=e$---unique
up to an additive constant \cite{schwartz1966distributions}.

\paragraph{The translation representation.}
For $Z\in\R_{>0}$ and $a\in\R$, the translation
$\lambda_a\colon L^2(\R,Z\,dx)\to L^2(\R,Z\,dx)$,
$(\lambda_af)(x)\coloneqq f(x+a)$, is unitary, and
$\lambda\colon a\mapsto\lambda_a$ makes $L^2(\R,Z\,dx)$ a unitary
representation of $\R$ (\cref{sec.background_hilbert}).  For
$h\in\R_{>0}$, write $e_h\colon\Z\to\R$ for the group homomorphism
$1\mapsto h$; time change along it restricts $\lambda$ to the single
unitary $\lambda_h$.

\begin{proposition}[Sampling]\label{prop.sampling}
Fix $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$.  The map
\[
s_h\colon\ell^2\bigl(\Z,\CZ(h)\bigr)\longrightarrow L^2(\R,Z\,dx),
\qquad
s_h(w)\coloneqq\sum_{i\in\Z}w_i\,\mathbf1_{[(i-1)h,\,ih)},
\]
is an isometric morphism of unitary representations of $\Z$
\[
s_h\colon
\bigl(\ell^2(\Z,\CZ(h)),\ \sigma^{\leftarrow}\bigr)
\longrightarrow
e_h^*\bigl(L^2(\R,Z\,dx),\ \lambda\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The summands have disjoint supports, so
$\lVert s_h(w)\rVert^2=Z\sum_{i\in\Z}w_i^2\,h
=\sum_{i\in\Z}Zh\,w_i^2=\lVert w\rVert^2_{\ell^2(\Z,\CZ(h))}$, and
$s_h$ is a well-defined isometry.  For the intertwining, let $x\in\R$;
then $\bigl(\lambda_hs_h(w)\bigr)(x)=s_h(w)(x+h)=w_i$ exactly when
$x+h\in[(i-1)h,\,ih)$, i.e.\ $x\in[(i-2)h,\,(i-1)h)$; reindexing by
$j\coloneqq i-1$ gives
$\lambda_hs_h(w)=\sum_{j\in\Z}w_{j+1}\mathbf1_{[(j-1)h,\,jh)}
=s_h(\sigma^{\leftarrow}w)$.
\end{proof}

Under the interval realization \cref{eq.interval-realization}, the completed
delay step \cref{eq.completed-delay-step} is cut-and-translate.  Fix
$Z,d\in\R_{>0}$ and $a\in(0,d]$.  For every
$q\in \EZ(d)$ and every $b\in \EZ(a)$, write
$\operatorname{next}_{d,a}(q,b)\in \EZ(d)$ for the state component of
$\step^Z_{d,a}(q,b)$.  For almost every $x\in[0,d)$,
\[
\operatorname{next}_{d,a}(q,b)(x)
=
\begin{cases}
q(x+a),&0\leq x<d-a,\\
b(x-d+a),&d-a\leq x<d.
\end{cases}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Characteristic-wave correspondence]
\label{thm.characteristic-wave}
Let $\Omega\subseteq\R^2$ be an open rectangle with coordinates
$(\tau,x)$, and let
$w^{\leftarrow},w^{\rightarrow}\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$.
The following are equivalent.

\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $a\in\R_{>0}$ for which the displayed arguments belong to
$\Omega$, the local translation laws
\begin{equation}\label{eq.local-translation-laws}
w^{\leftarrow}(\tau+a,x)
=
w^{\leftarrow}(\tau,x+a),
\qquad
w^{\rightarrow}(\tau+a,x)
=
w^{\rightarrow}(\tau,x-a)
\end{equation}
hold for almost every $(\tau,x)\in\Omega$.

\item The distributional transport equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq.transport-equations}
(\partial_\tau-\partial_x)w^{\leftarrow}=0,
\qquad
(\partial_\tau+\partial_x)w^{\rightarrow}=0
\end{equation}
hold on $\Omega$.

\item There is a function $u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, unique up to
an additive constant, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq.characteristic-reconstruction}
\partial_\tau u
=
w^{\leftarrow}+w^{\rightarrow},
\qquad
\partial_xu
=
w^{\leftarrow}-w^{\rightarrow},
\end{equation}
and the weak wave equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq.wave-equation}
\partial_\tau^2u=\partial_x^2u.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Conversely, for every
$u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ satisfying
\cref{eq.wave-equation}, the formulas
\begin{equation}\label{eq.wave-characteristics}
w^{\leftarrow}
\coloneqq
\frac{\partial_\tau u+\partial_xu}{2},
\qquad
w^{\rightarrow}
\coloneqq
\frac{\partial_\tau u-\partial_xu}{2}
\end{equation}
define the unique characteristic fields satisfying
\cref{eq.characteristic-reconstruction}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Strong continuity of translation on $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$
(recorded at the start of this section) identifies
the difference quotients of \cref{eq.local-translation-laws} with the two
distributional derivatives in \cref{eq.transport-equations}.  Conversely,
the coordinates $(\tau,x+\tau)$ for $w^{\leftarrow}$ and
$(\tau,x-\tau)$ for $w^{\rightarrow}$ show that
\cref{eq.transport-equations} makes each field constant along its
characteristics, which gives \cref{eq.local-translation-laws}.

Put
$v\coloneqq w^{\leftarrow}+w^{\rightarrow}$ and
$e\coloneqq w^{\leftarrow}-w^{\rightarrow}$.  The transport equations give
\begin{equation}\label{eq.closed-energy-form}
\partial_\tau e=\partial_xv.
\end{equation}
Hence the distributional one-form $v\,d\tau+e\,dx$ is closed.  Since
$\Omega$ is an open rectangle, the distributional Poincar\'e lemma
(recorded at the start of this section) gives
$u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ satisfying
\cref{eq.characteristic-reconstruction}, uniquely up to a constant.
Equation \cref{eq.closed-energy-form} then gives
\cref{eq.wave-equation}.  The converse follows by substituting
\cref{eq.wave-characteristics} into \cref{eq.transport-equations}.
\end{proof}

The discrete and continuous semantics fit together: every lattice
trajectory samples a weak solution of the wave equation.

\begin{corollary}[Lattice trajectories are sampled waves]
\label{cor.sampled-waves}
Fix $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$.  Let $(q(t),\xi(t))_{t\in\N}$ be a trajectory of
$F_{Z,h}$ and let $w^{\leftarrow}(t),w^{\rightarrow}(t)$ be
its characteristic trajectory \cref{eq.characteristic-trajectory}.  Put
$\Omega\coloneqq\R_{>0}\times\R$ and define
$W^{\leftarrow},W^{\rightarrow}\in L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ as the
translation orbits of the sampled initial fields:
\[
W^{\leftarrow}(\tau,\blank)\coloneqq
\lambda_\tau\,s_h\bigl(w^{\leftarrow}(0)\bigr),
\qquad
W^{\rightarrow}(\tau,\blank)\coloneqq
\lambda_{-\tau}\,s_h\bigl(w^{\rightarrow}(0)\bigr).
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{item.sampling} for every $t\in\N$, the time-$th$ slices
sample the discrete trajectory:
\[
W^{\leftarrow}(th,\blank)=s_h\bigl(w^{\leftarrow}(t)\bigr),
\qquad
W^{\rightarrow}(th,\blank)=s_h\bigl(w^{\rightarrow}(t)\bigr);
\]
\item\label{item.wave} $W^{\leftarrow},W^{\rightarrow}$ satisfy the
translation laws \cref{eq.local-translation-laws} on $\Omega$, and hence
there is a $u\in H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, unique up to an additive
constant, satisfying the weak wave equation \cref{eq.wave-equation} whose
characteristic fields \cref{eq.wave-characteristics} are
$W^{\leftarrow},W^{\rightarrow}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every time slice lies in $L^2(\R,Z\,dx)$ with norm independent of $\tau$
($\lambda$ is unitary and $s_h$ isometric), and
$(\tau,x)\mapsto s_h(w)(x\pm\tau)$ is measurable, so both fields lie in
$L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ by integrating over compacta.

For \cref{item.sampling}: iterating the intertwining of
\cref{prop.sampling} gives
$\lambda_{th}\circ s_h=s_h\circ(\sigma^{\leftarrow})^t$, and iterating
the propagation law \cref{eq.characteristic-propagation} gives
$(\sigma^{\leftarrow})^t\,w^{\leftarrow}(0)=w^{\leftarrow}(t)$; hence
\[
W^{\leftarrow}(th,\blank)
=\lambda_{th}\,s_h\bigl(w^{\leftarrow}(0)\bigr)
=s_h\bigl(w^{\leftarrow}(t)\bigr).
\]
The right-travelling case is the same with $\lambda_{-th}$ and
$\sigma^{\rightarrow}$, using
$\lambda_{-h}\circ s_h=s_h\circ\sigma^{\rightarrow}$, which follows
from \cref{prop.sampling} because the shifts and the translations are
inverse pairs.

For \cref{item.wave}, the translation laws hold because the fields are
$\lambda$-orbits: for every $a\in\R_{>0}$,
$W^{\leftarrow}(\tau+a,\blank)
=\lambda_a\bigl(W^{\leftarrow}(\tau,\blank)\bigr)$, which is the first
law of \cref{eq.local-translation-laws} almost everywhere, and likewise
$W^{\rightarrow}(\tau+a,\blank)
=\lambda_{-a}\bigl(W^{\rightarrow}(\tau,\blank)\bigr)$ is the second.
Now apply \cref{thm.characteristic-wave}, items (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii).
\end{proof}

The coordinate $x$ measures transit time.  If $y\in\R$ is physical position
and $v_{\mathrm{wave}}\in\R_{>0}$ is wave speed, define
$u_{\mathrm{phys}}(\tau,y)\coloneqq u(\tau,y/v_{\mathrm{wave}})$.
Then \cref{eq.wave-equation} becomes
\[
\partial_\tau^2u_{\mathrm{phys}}
=
v_{\mathrm{wave}}^2\partial_y^2u_{\mathrm{phys}}.
\]

\begin{corollary}[Energy coordinates]\label{cor.energy-coordinates}
Fix $Z,d\in\R_{>0}$ and let $J\subseteq\R$ be an interval of duration $d$.
Define
\[
\mathcal E_Z(J)
\coloneqq
\bigl(H^1(J)/\R\bigr)\oplus L^2(J)
\]
with squared norm
\[
\lVert([\varphi],v)\rVert_{\mathcal E_Z(J)}^2
\coloneqq
\frac Z2\int_J
\bigl(|\partial_x\varphi|^2+|v|^2\bigr)\,dx.
\]
After translating $J$ to $[0,d)$, the map
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa_{Z,J}\colon
\mathcal E_Z(J)&\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\EZ(d)\oplus \EZ(d),\\
\kappa_{Z,J}([\varphi],v)
&\coloneqq
\left(
\frac{v+\partial_x\varphi}{2},
\frac{v-\partial_x\varphi}{2}
\right)
\end{aligned}
\]
is unitary.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every $([\varphi],v)\in\mathcal E_Z(J)$,
\[
\left|\frac{v+\partial_x\varphi}{2}\right|^2
+
\left|\frac{v-\partial_x\varphi}{2}\right|^2
=
\frac12\bigl(|v|^2+|\partial_x\varphi|^2\bigr),
\]
so $\kappa_{Z,J}$ is isometric.  Given
$f,g\in L^2(J,Z\,dx)$, put $v=f+g$ and $e=f-g$.  There is a unique
$[\varphi]\in H^1(J)/\R$ with $\partial_x\varphi=e$, proving surjectivity.
\end{proof}

\section{Associative strings}
\label{sec.strings}

\subsection{Transparent serial composition}

\begin{definition}[Two-ended Moore systems]\label{def.moore-systems}
Fix a real Hilbert space $B\in\Hilb$.  A \emph{two-ended linear Moore
system on $B$} is a quadruple
$M=(X,L_M,R_M,U_M)$ consisting of a state space $X\in\Hilb$, bounded
readouts
$L_M,R_M\colon X\to B$,
and a bounded update
$U_M\colon X\oplus B\oplus B\to X$.
A morphism is a bounded linear map of state spaces commuting with the two
readouts and the update.  Write $\defineTerm{moore}_2(B)$ for the resulting
category.
\end{definition}

For $M_i=(X_i,L_i,R_i,U_i)\in\MooreCat_2(B)$,
$i\in\{1,2\}$, define the transparent serial product
$M_1\diamond M_2$ to have state $X_1\oplus X_2$, outer readouts
\[
L_{1\diamond2}(x_1,x_2)\coloneqq L_1(x_1),
\qquad
R_{1\diamond2}(x_1,x_2)\coloneqq R_2(x_2),
\]
and update
\[
U_{1\diamond2}(x_1,x_2,b^{\mathrm L},b^{\mathrm R})
\coloneqq
\left(
U_1(x_1,b^{\mathrm L},L_2(x_2)),
U_2(x_2,R_1(x_1),b^{\mathrm R})
\right)
\]
for every $x_i\in X_i$ and $b^{\mathrm L},b^{\mathrm R}\in B$.  On
morphisms, put $f_1\diamond f_2\coloneqq f_1\oplus f_2$.

\begin{proposition}[Serial composition is hom-composition in $\pc$]
\label{prop.seam}
Fix $B\in\Hilb$ and put
$p\coloneqq\lvert B\rvert\,\yon^{\lvert B\rvert}$.  Forgetting
Hilbert structure, a two-ended system $M\in\MooreCat_2(B)$ is a
$1$-cell $\mathsf M\colon\yon\to p\otimes p$ in $\pc$: the
coalgebra with states $\lvert X\rvert$, positions $(L_M,R_M)$, and
update $U_M$.  Let
$\fun{seam}\colon p\otimes p\to\yon$ be the polynomial map that is
unique on positions and returns, on directions at $(b_1,b_2)$, the pair
$(b_2,b_1)$, regarded as a stateless $1$-cell (\cref{lem.stateless}).
Then for all
$M_1,M_2\in\MooreCat_2(B)$,
\[
\mathsf M_{1\diamond2}
=
\bigl(\id_p\otimes\fun{seam}\otimes\id_p\bigr)
\circ
\bigl(\mathsf M_1\otimes\mathsf M_2\bigr)
\colon\yon\to p\otimes p,
\]
where $\mathsf M_{1\diamond2}$ denotes the $1$-cell of
$M_1\diamond M_2$ and $\id_p$ is stateless (\cref{lem.stateless}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both sides have state set
$\lvert X_1\rvert\times\lvert X_2\rvert$, the seam and the
identities being stateless.  At a state $(x_1,x_2)$, the $1$-cell
$\mathsf M_1\otimes\mathsf M_2$ presents the four positions
$(L_1(x_1),R_1(x_1),L_2(x_2),R_2(x_2))$, of which the right-hand
composite presents the outer pair $(L_1(x_1),R_2(x_2))$---the readouts
of $M_1\diamond M_2$.  Given outer directions
$(b^{\mathrm L},b^{\mathrm R})$, the identities relay them to the
first and fourth ports, and the seam returns $(L_2(x_2),R_1(x_1))$ to
the second and third; the state updates to
$\bigl(U_1(x_1,b^{\mathrm L},L_2(x_2)),\
U_2(x_2,R_1(x_1),b^{\mathrm R})\bigr)$, the update of
$M_1\diamond M_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rmk.seam-native}
The serial product is therefore not a new composition: it is
hom-composition in $\pc$ against a stateless seam, and the two-ended
strings below live in the same $2$-category as the phase semantics of
\cref{sec.hilbert_semantics}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Associativity]\label{prop.serial-associativity}
Fix $B\in\Hilb$.  The product $\diamond$ makes
$\MooreCat_2(B)$ a semigroupal category
(\cref{sec.background_categories}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $M_i=(X_i,L_i,R_i,U_i)$, $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, both parenthesizations have
state $X_1\oplus X_2\oplus X_3$, outer readouts $L_1$ and $R_3$, and
update
\[
\left(
U_1(x_1,b^{\mathrm L},L_2(x_2)),
U_2(x_2,R_1(x_1),L_3(x_3)),
U_3(x_3,R_2(x_2),b^{\mathrm R})
\right).
\]
The direct-sum associator therefore gives the semigroupal associator and
satisfies the pentagon.

There is no stateless Moore unit when $B\neq0$: a zero-state Moore system
has zero current output and cannot transmit its current input.  A unit
requires direct feedthrough, which belongs to Mealy rather than Moore
semantics.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strings from an interval algebra}

\begin{theorem}[Interval algebras produce associative strings]
\label{thm.interval-strings}
Let $A\colon\Int\to\Hilb$ be a strong $\Int$-algebra with unitary structure
maps $\Gamma^A$, and fix $h\in\R_{>0}$.  Put $B\coloneqq A(h)$.  For every
$d\in\R_{\geq h}$, write the delay step \cref{eq.abstract-delay-step} as
\[
\step^A_{d,h}(q,b)
=
\bigl(P^A_{d,h}(q),U^A_{d,h}(q,b)\bigr)
\]
for every $q\in A(d)$ and $b\in B$.  Define
$\mathsf S_{A,h}(d)\in\MooreCat_2(B)$ to have state
$A(d)\oplus A(d)$,
left and right readouts
\[
L_d(q^{\rightarrow},q^{\leftarrow})
\coloneqq P^A_{d,h}(q^{\leftarrow}),
\qquad
R_d(q^{\rightarrow},q^{\leftarrow})
\coloneqq P^A_{d,h}(q^{\rightarrow}),
\]
and update
\[
U_d(q^{\rightarrow},q^{\leftarrow},
b^{\mathrm L},b^{\mathrm R})
\coloneqq
\left(
U^A_{d,h}(q^{\rightarrow},b^{\mathrm L}),
U^A_{d,h}(q^{\leftarrow},b^{\mathrm R})
\right).
\]

For all $d_1,d_2\in\R_{\geq h}$, the state map
\begin{equation}\label{eq.serial-string-isomorphism}
\begin{aligned}
\chi^A_{d_1,d_2}
\bigl(
q_1^{\rightarrow},q_1^{\leftarrow},
q_2^{\rightarrow},q_2^{\leftarrow}
\bigr)
\coloneqq
\bigl(
&\Gamma^A_{d_2,d_1}
(q_2^{\rightarrow},q_1^{\rightarrow}),\\
&\Gamma^A_{d_1,d_2}
(q_1^{\leftarrow},q_2^{\leftarrow})
\bigr)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
is a unitary isomorphism
\[
\chi^A_{d_1,d_2}\colon
\mathsf S_{A,h}(d_1)\diamond\mathsf S_{A,h}(d_2)
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
\mathsf S_{A,h}(d_1+d_2)
\]
in $\MooreCat_2(B)$.  The family
$(\chi^A_{d_1,d_2})$ satisfies semigroupal associativity.  Hence
\[
d\in\R_{\geq h}
\longmapsto
\mathsf S_{A,h}(d)
\]
is a strong semigroupal representation of
$(\R_{\geq h},+)$ in
$(\MooreCat_2(B),\diamond)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each queue is stored in the order in which its packets leave.  The
right-travelling state of the second string therefore precedes that of the
first, while the left-travelling state of the first precedes that of the
second.  This gives the two orders in
\cref{eq.serial-string-isomorphism}.

The internal seam in $\diamond$ sends each exposed packet into the adjacent
queue.  Temporal coherence \cref{eq.temporal-coherence} shows that
$\chi^A_{d_1,d_2}$ intertwines both updates and both outer readouts.  The
associativity coherence is the substitution axiom for the
$\Int$-algebra $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Associative harmonic strings]
\label{cor.associative-harmonic-strings}
Fix $Z,h\in\R_{>0}$ and apply
\cref{thm.interval-strings} to the characteristic interval algebra
$\EZ\colon\Int\to\Hilb$.  The resulting assignment
\[
d\in\R_{\geq h}
\longmapsto
\mathsf S_{\EZ,h}(d)
\]
is a strong semigroupal representation in
$\MooreCat_2(\EZ(h))$.  Its state is
$\EZ(d)\oplus \EZ(d)$, the Hilbert completion of the two characteristic
queues obtained from the harmonic phase system by
$\Wc_{Z,h}$.
\end{corollary}

Under $\Phiphase$, a box receives its inputs as covectors: the directions
of $\Phiphase\binom{A}{B}$ at a position include the cotangent directions
of $\cotof B$ (\cref{thm.phase-functor}).  If a string is to be attached to
such an interface, the Riesz isomorphism
$\EZ(h)\To{\cong}\EZ(h)^*$
converts each incoming packet to the required variance.  This conversion
belongs to the interface.  The mechanics remains entirely in the
$\harr$-operation $\Lat_{Z,h}$ and its phase semantics; the completed
string uses the characteristic state selected by the 2-cell
\cref{eq.characteristic-two-cell}.
